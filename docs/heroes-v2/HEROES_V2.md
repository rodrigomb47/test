# Heroes v2: design

Original anime-style heroes that lead factions, work at their bases and fight like bosses.

Status: design, 2026-11-12. Nothing here is implemented. It replaces ROADMAP phase 4 ("owner-supplied heroes with 64x64
skins"): the mod now ships its own 200 original heroes with animated models. Numbers marked PROPOSED go to BALANCE.md
when they are built. When the work starts, this file, `ROSTER_PLAN.md`, `SLICE_PLAN.md` and `roster/` move to
`docs/heroes/v2/` in the repository, ROADMAP phase 4 is rewritten to point at them, and `HERO_FORMAT.md` stays as the
format-1 reference.

Companion files (this folder):
- `ROSTER_PLAN.md`: the 200 heroes (100 female, 100 male), generated and checked by `roster/`.
- `SLICE_PLAN.md`: the first milestone, 8 complete heroes and hero-led factions in a real campaign.
- `MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md`: heroes' superhuman mobility (jumps, climbing, gliding, flight, swimming) and work mastery;
  it wins over this document on those two subjects.
- `research-archetypes.md`, `research-animation.md`, `research-systems.md`: the three studies this design merges.
  Where a study and this document differ, this document wins.

Reused: `docs/heroes/HERO_FORMAT.md` (format v1, "v1 §n" below) stays the base of the kit format. Its conventions
(v1 §3), stats and role presets (§5), elements and the damage formula (§6), gear (§7), behaviour (§8), basic attack
(§9), abilities, forms, conditions and amounts (§10-12), effect catalogue (§13), energy (§14), cooldown floor (§15),
caps (§16), passives (§17), marks (§19), summons (§20), validation pipeline and message format (§26) and safety limits
(§26.5) apply unchanged unless this document says otherwise. Its skin, skin-network and owner-folder parts (§1, §2, §24,
the skin half of §25) are superseded.

## 0. The request and the decisions

The owner's request, in short: create original heroes instead of using owner-supplied ones; each hero has its own
appearance and combat style, and fighting one feels like fighting a boss; heroes get a more advanced skin with custom
animations, combat animations and facial animation such as blinking; research anime characters and anime-styled games
for archetypes (cat girls, shrine maidens and so on) without copying anyone; half male, half female; 200 heroes, each
with complex behaviour and non-combat skills; every faction starts with a random hero as its leader, and the faction's
name and decisions follow that hero's personality.

Decisions (the owner confirms or corrects them; section 17 lists the real open questions):

1. **A hero is its own entity type**, `factionclash:hero` (`HeroEntity extends FactionNpc implements GeoEntity`). It
   keeps everything a faction NPC has (jobs, inventory, hunger, gear, navigation, stuck recovery, saving, war council)
   and adds the hero runtime. Workers keep today's renderer. (v1 had "a FactionNpc with a hero id"; a separate type is
   needed for the animated renderer.)
2. **Models and animation use GeckoLib 4.9.3**, embedded in the Faction Clash jar (jar-in-jar, MIT licence) and vendored
   in the repository (`libs/maven`), so players and servers install one file and builds stay offline-safe. It gives
   custom geometry, layered animations, sound and marker keyframes, glow layers and items on bones, and its files open
   in Blockbench. The fallback, if it is ever ruled out, is vanilla keyframe animation with our own model loader
   (section 4.1).
3. **The look is Minecraft-style cubes with anime proportions and features**: one shared rig with elbows, knees, hair,
   ears, tails, capes and skirts, in three body forms (chibi, teen, adult) with big heads, hair built from layered
   cubes and outfits with volume; a parts library; 256x128 pixel-art textures; faces at double resolution whose eyes,
   mouth and cheeks switch between painted frames for blinking and expressions. Not smooth high-polygon models, which
   would not match the world (open question 1).
4. **All art is generated by our own tools** (`tools/herogen`, Python) from one spec per hero: model, texture, face
   frames, animations and the server timing data. Output is deterministic and committed; every file opens in
   Blockbench for hand polishing later. Quality is checked by automatic rules, contact sheets from a software preview
   renderer, and in-game screenshots.
5. **One authored file per hero**, `herosrc/heroes/<id>.hero.json` (format 2), holds kit, boss phases, personality,
   leader identity, talents, voice, lines, art spec and animation recipes. The generator splits it into the server
   definition and the client assets. Attack timings exist once: the animation's `hit` markers and the server's move
   frames come from the same recipe, and a unit test fails if they ever differ.
6. **Two combat modes.** In NPC wars a hero fights at v1 scale ("field mode", a strong officer worth about two to
   three soldiers). When a player engages it, it switches to **boss mode**: scaled health that keeps its share, a
   per-hit damage cap, poise and stagger, two or three phases that each add a mechanic, telegraphed attacks with
   growing ground markers, reactions to what the player does, enrage, and a yield at zero health where the player may
   spare or finish it.
7. **Factions are slots with an identity generated from their leader** (name, short name, colours, banner, base
   style, seat, naming culture). `FactionType` stays an enum because saves, network sync and the GameTests depend on
   it. Milestone 1 uses the four existing slots; four more slots come in milestone 2.
8. **Personality drives decisions.** Eight traits plus one of 30 temperaments. Every director knob (raids, targets,
   garrison, recruitment, building, trade, later diplomacy) is a published formula of the traits that returns today's
   constant when every trait is 50, `leaders.personalityStrength = 0` reproduces today's director numbers exactly (the
   leaders still exist and fight), and `leaders.enabled = false` reproduces today's campaign.
9. **Non-combat skills are talents from a fixed catalogue of 35** with mod-owned numbers (tiers at hero levels 1, 10
   and 20), plus a duty (any worker job, really worked at player speed) and work animations. A leader has the new
   `court` duty. Edicts, festivals and morale come in milestone 2.
10. **Content ships in order**: a vertical slice of 8 heroes (4 female, 4 male) with every engine piece, then 8
    batches of 24, each gated by checks, a contact sheet, in-game screenshots and a short campaign.
11. **Player-like rules hold.** No teleporting (decoys are visual and pop; dashes, leaps and charges are physical
    pushes), abilities never place, break or ignite blocks, kit items are vanilla items bound to the hero, damage goes
    through vanilla armour, shields and enchantments, NPC wars never see boss numbers. Superhuman mobility (decision
    15) is physical movement too, so it keeps this rule.
12. **The v1 owner-hero workflow is retired** (open question 2): no hero folder with owner skins, no skin payloads. The
    config folder stays for operators to override a built-in hero's kit or personality; a format-1 file still loads
    and uses a neutral fallback model.
13. **All heroes are adults with modest outfits.** Youthful looks are a drawing style, not an age.
14. **No recorded voices.** Dialogue is text (chat line and a speech bubble) with a talking mouth and pitched vanilla
    "voice blips". Recording voices for 200 original characters is out of scope.
15. **Heroes move and work better than players** (the owner's request of 2026-11-12; this reverses the earlier "heroes
    move like players, nobody flies"): each hero has a mobility trait with a tier ceiling (runner, charger, leaper,
    acrobat, climber, glider, flyer, hover, swimmer; snow and fire footings), about 13% of the roster flies with a
    stamina bar, and every hero works its duty with a proficiency plus a specialty from its primary talent. Fairness is
    counterplay enforced by the mod (flyers land to strike and can be shot down; a fighting hero comes back within
    reach), teleporting stays out, and workers and soldiers stay player-like. The owner's answers of the same day:
    heroes may use their abilities to make things "unfair" (they cross hostile walls in raids, sieges and scouting and
    reach players anywhere; an escaping hero may make pursuit impossible), the 27 flyers stay, and every hero has all
    of its abilities from level 1 at a power that grows with level (section 8). `MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md`.

## 3. The hero entity

### 3.1 Type and registration

- `ModEntities.HERO` = `factionclash:hero`, `EntityType.Builder.of(HeroEntity::new, MobCategory.MISC).sized(0.6F,
  1.8F).eyeHeight(1.62F).clientTrackingRange(10)`, attributes from `FactionNpc.createAttributes()`.
- `ClientSetup.onRenderers` registers `HeroRenderer`. `HeroEntity` implements `GeoEntity`
  (`registerControllers`, `getAnimatableInstanceCache` from `GeckoLibUtil.createInstanceCache(this)`); GeckoLib is on
  both sides, so this is safe on a dedicated server as long as controller handlers touch no client class.
- `getBoundingBoxForCulling()` is inflated by 1.5 blocks so wings, tails and long weapons do not vanish at the screen
  edge.

### 3.2 What it inherits from `FactionNpc`

Inventory and backpack, hunger, gear and wear, affixes, levels and work XP, jobs (its duty), `NpcNavigator`, `DigOut`
and stuck recovery, roster records (`NpcRecord.heroId`), alarms and the war council. Every `instanceof FactionNpc`
check keeps working. Its `NpcRole` (synced) is the combat role that fits its style (melee styles `SWORDSMAN` or
`AXE_FIGHTER`, `bow` `ARCHER`, `crossbow` `CROSSBOWMAN`, thrown styles `SWORDSMAN` with its own goal), so recruitment
counts stay right; `retrainIdleWorker` never touches a hero.

The director gathers soldiers with `getEntitiesOfClass(FactionNpc.class, ...)` filtered by role
(`FactionDirector.raids`, the garrison `keep` count, `living(f, b, role)` needs), so without a rule a hero with a
soldier role would be sorted into raid parties like any swordsman. Rule: a hero **counts** as one soldier of its role
for needs, garrison and war-council strength, but is **never picked** for a raid party by the soldier sort; it joins a
raid only through `LeaderBiases` "leader goes with the raid" (leaders) or its own `behaviour.raids` (others), and while
it has the `court` duty it is never sent alone. GameTest `heroNotDraftedIntoRaidByDefault`.

### 3.3 Hero state

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| Synced entity data | `DATA_HERO` (String, hero id), `DATA_ACTION` (Integer), `DATA_STATE` (Byte), `DATA_MOOD` (Byte): section 10.1 |
| Entity NBT `FcHero` | hero id, cooldown ready-at game times, charges, state timers, energy, current phase, low-health re-arm flags (as v1 §25.1) |
| `CampaignData.heroes[id]` (`HeroState`) | owner faction or free, base, entity UUID, alive, `reviveAt`, level, XP, affixes, kills, deaths, casts per move, boss fights, staggers, yields, spares, definition hash; later bonds and posts |
| Transient (`HeroCombat`, `BossController`) | move in progress and its stage, poise, boss-mode players, attack tokens, telegraph entities, reaction cooldowns; rebuilt on load (a reload ends boss mode and keeps the health share) |

### 3.4 Life cycle

- **Spawn**: a leader at its capital's reporting station when the base is built (section 5.6); a hero joining by
  `/fc admin hero give` (milestone 1), tokens and recruitment (milestone 3). One living entity per hero id; a duplicate
  joining the level is discarded (v1 §25.1 rule).
- **Death in field mode**: v1 revival (`heroes.reviveSeconds`, 600 s) at the home base's spawn point. Owner decision
  (2026-11-12): every hero, leaders included, comes back 10 minutes after its death; there is no longer a separate
  leader timer (`leaders.reviveMultiplier` is gone). A death-related ability may change that (owner, same day): the
  `revival_rite` talent (7.2) brings the faction's fallen heroes back sooner, and a hero's own `revival` block (12.3)
  sets its own delay and where it rises (a phoenix reborn where it fell, a jiangshi rising again). With both, the
  hero's own delay comes first and the talent shortens it; never under `heroes.minReviveSeconds` (30, PROPOSED), so
  a hero cannot rise in the middle of the fight that killed it. Kit items vanish and return (v1 §7, §25.4).
- **Defeat in boss mode**: the yield beat (section 6.13) first; finished or not spared in 5 s, it dies as above.
- **Removed or broken definition**: a hero whose definition disappears keeps its entity and saved state and fights as
  a plain soldier of its duty with the fallback model, name tag `(dormant)` (v1 §25.5).

### 3.5 Goals

| Priority | Goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `FloatGoal`, `StunnedGoal` | v1 |
| 1 | `HeroYieldGoal` | kneels, passive, while yielding |
| 2 | `HeroMoveGoal` | replaces v1's `HeroAbilityGoal`: picks and runs moves in both modes (section 6.3); MOVE+LOOK while a move runs |
| 3 | `NpcMeleeAttackGoal` / `NpcRangedAttackGoal` / `HeroThrowGoal` | basic attacks, leash from aggression (v1 §8), skirmisher step-back, `keep_distance` |
| 4 | `WithdrawGoal` | `retreat_below` from personality (section 5.4); never while boss mode is on; away from its home base the withdrawal is an escape with any mobility (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 7.7); at home, and within 10 minutes of an escape, it withdraws as soldiers do |
| 5+ | `WorkGoal` | its duty, or `court` for a leader |
| target | `HeroTargetGoal` | focus, protects, aggression radius (v1 §8), attack tokens (section 6.2) |

### 3.6 Size

Rig v3 has three body forms (4.3): **chibi** 35 px to the top of the head, **teen** 45 px and **adult** 52 px, each
with the same 10 px head, and the whole model is drawn at the rig's **render scale 0.56** (style pass 2: longer
bodies, so the head with its hair is about a quarter of an adult's height, and a head about the size of a vanilla
player's). In the world that is about **1.23, 1.58 and 1.82 blocks**. `HeroEntity` takes its hitbox height from the
form (`visual.json` `height_px` x `render_scale` x scale / 16; PROPOSED: at least 1.2 blocks so a chibi is not much
harder to hit than a player crouching; eye height (`head_y` + 3) x `render_scale` x scale), width 0.6 like players.
The vanilla `generic.scale` attribute sets size: `art.scale` 85-110% (PROPOSED). The hitbox is capped at 1.98 blocks
so every hero still walks through 2-high tunnels and doors; "large" heroes are broader through their base body, and
horns or ears may stick out of the hitbox visually. GeckoLib's renderer applies `getScale()` (checked: 
`GeoEntityRenderer` scales the pose by `LivingEntity.getScale()`). Vanilla scales the hitbox with the same attribute,
so a state `size` on top of `art.scale` (Benika: 108% x 105%) would exceed 1.98: `HeroEntity` overrides
`getDefaultDimensions(pose)` to clamp the scaled height to 1.98 (eye height clamped in proportion) and calls
`refreshDimensions()` when the scale changes; the model keeps drawing at the full scale.

## 4. Model and animation stack

### 4.1 Why GeckoLib

| Need | GeckoLib 4.9.3 | Hand-written (fallback) |
|---|---|---|
| Custom geometry: hair, ears, tails, wings, skirts, big weapons, elbows and knees | yes (Bedrock geometry, per-face UV, rotated cubes, inflate) | own loader for cubes with per-face UV |
| Several animations at once (legs walk, arms attack, tail sways, face separate) | yes, controllers override per bone and channel | additive only (`KeyframeAnimations.animate`) |
| Easing, transitions, loops, hold on last frame | yes (30+ easings, `step` included) | linear and spline only |
| Sound, particle and custom marker keyframes | yes | no (a sidecar file) |
| Glow masks, items on bones | `AutoGlowingGeoLayer`, `BlockAndItemGeoLayer` | own layers |
| Artist tooling | Blockbench GeckoLib format | Blockbench "modded entity" export plus a converter |
| Cost to us | about 600-900 lines of integration | about 2000 lines of engine plus the integration |
| Risks | the host is blocked here until allowed; jar grows; a future Minecraft upgrade means GeckoLib 5 (renderer rewrite, assets unchanged) | we own every bug |

AzureLib (a fork with API breaks between minor versions and one maintainer) and player-animation libraries (player
model only, no custom parts, no face) were rejected (`research-animation.md` section 2). GeckoLib 4.9.3 supports
NeoForge 21.1.150 and later (we use 21.1.209), is MIT-licensed and adds about 0.6 MB.

### 4.2 Adding it to the build

1. **Once**: reach `dl.cloudsmith.io` (GeckoLib's Maven host). On 2026-11-11 the session proxy refused it (403,
   `research-animation.md` section 12); on 2026-11-12 the critic pass downloaded from it directly (HTTP 200), so no
   approval may be needed. If it is refused again, the implementing session proposes the host with its environment
   tools, which shows the owner an approval prompt.
2. Download once and copy to `libs/maven/software/bernie/geckolib/geckolib-neoforge-1.21.1/4.9.3/`:
   `geckolib-neoforge-1.21.1-4.9.3.jar`, `.pom` and `.module`. The POM carries Gradle's "published-with-gradle-metadata"
   marker, so the `.module` file is vendored too (checked 2026-11-12: its variants have **no dependencies**, so nothing
   else needs vendoring). Next to the version folder goes a hand-written `maven-metadata.xml` that lists only 4.9.3
   (Gradle reads it to resolve the version range). Verified facts of the published jar (2026-11-12): 634,531 bytes,
   SHA-1 `5cfe835472a5c5457ae9ac192d9d7b317454d5ac` (equals the published `.sha1`), SHA-256
   `20a1995e4074f387ff549e2c57ea79dd7d41ee83c6afb9dd6a6e840e592d1c47`; its `neoforge.mods.toml` declares mod id
   `geckolib`, version 4.9.3, licence MIT, `neoforge [21.1.150,)`, `minecraft [1.21,)`, one mixin config and an access

4. `build.gradle`: an `exclusiveContent` repository (`file('libs/maven')`, group `software.bernie.geckolib` only;
   `metadataSources { gradleMetadata(); mavenPom(); artifact() }` so a missing `.module` can never fail the build), and
   `jarJar(implementation("software.bernie.geckolib:geckolib-neoforge-${minecraft_version}")) { version { strictly
   project.geckolib_version_range; prefer project.geckolib_version } }`; `geckolib_version_range` added to
   `replaceProperties`.
5. `neoforge.mods.toml`: a required `geckolib` dependency with that version range, side BOTH.
6. Ship `META-INF/licenses/geckolib-LICENSE.txt`; README "Credits".
7. Verify: `unzip -l build/libs/factionclash-0.1.0.jar | grep jarjar` lists the embedded jar; all unit tests and
   GameTests pass with GeckoLib on the headless server; a dedicated server from `tools/setup_server.sh` and the Xvfb
   `clientViewer` start with only our jar.

### 4.3 The shared rig ("hero rig v3")

Units are Bedrock model pixels; feet at y = 0; the whole model is drawn at the rig's `render.scale` (**0.56**), so 16
model px are about 9 world px. Rig v3 (`herosrc/rig/rig_v3.json`, 2026-11-12, replacing rig v2 after the owner's feedback
"the head feels too big and their faces are quite similar": style pass 2, `notes/artgen.md`) keeps v2's bone names and
its **three forms** with the **same 10x10x10 head** on each, so the face, the face texture layout and every
head-anchored part (hair, ears, hats) fit every form; the bodies are 1.5-1.6 times longer, to the proportions measured
on the owner's references and other creators' models (`notes/reference-study.md`, `notes/reference-survey.md`):

| Form | Head top | Head share with hair (refs) | Legs (hip) | Torso (hips, waist, chest) | Arms (shoulder pivot to hand bottom) | World height | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `chibi` | 35 px | 0.34 (0.32) | 12 | 2 + 2.5 + 7 | 22.5 to 11.5 (hip line) | 1.23 blocks | a small child |
| `teen` | 45 px | 0.27 (0.25) | 20 | 2.5 + 3 + 7 | 31.5 to 16.5 (upper thigh) | 1.58 blocks | petite |
| `adult` | 52 px | 0.24 (0.22-0.24) | 24.5 | 3 + 3.5 + 8.5 | 38.5 to 19.5 (mid-thigh) | 1.82 blocks | about player height, long legs |

Measured on the fixtures after the art direction pass 2 (`notes/artgen.md`), the hair top is 2.8 px over the head on
every style except the spiky one. The visual head shares are 0.234 (adult), 0.268 (teen; spiky hair 0.283) and 0.339
(chibi). The owner's screenshot gives about 0.20-0.22, 0.25-0.26 and 0.32-0.35. Closing the adult's remaining gap
needs a longer body (PROPOSED for the owner's decision: head tops 56 / 48 / 37 px at a render scale of about 0.52),
not a smaller head.

Two-segment arms and legs, a 1.5-2.5 px neck, 2.5-3.5 px "mitten" hands, a 1-1.5 px toe. Every height is a base dim (`hip_y`, `knee_y`,
`waist_y`, `chest_y`, `chest_top`, `head_y`, `shoulder_y`, `elbow_y`, `wrist_y`, ...), so pivots are expressions over
the dims and differ by form, while **bone names are the same on every base**: an animation keyed for one base plays on
all of them (rotations transfer exactly; root position offsets are in pixels, so a bob authored for the adult is a little
larger on a chibi).

The teen and adult female bases have a small bust cube; arms hang right next to the chest (shoulder pivot = half the
chest plus half the arm); legs stay inside the hips. `art.form` picks the form (default `adult`), `art.base` the family
and `art.build` the build (12.5). The style guide that sets these numbers (head-heights, face, hair, outfits) is in
`docs/heroes-v2/notes/artgen.md`.

**Naming conventions the renderer uses** (no per-hero code):

| Prefix or name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `fine_*` | detail hidden beyond 20 blocks (strands, frills, charms): level of detail |
| `p1_*`, `p2_*`, `p3_*` | shown only in that phase and later (`p1_` only in phase 1): extra tails, unfurled wings, a helmet, a second sword |
| `weapon_r`, `weapon_l` vs `weapon_back` | weapon in hand while the "weapon drawn" flag is set, otherwise the sheathed copy |
| `item_r`, `item_l` | where vanilla work tools are drawn; hidden while the weapon is drawn |
| `sash`, `fine_sash_*` | tinted with the faction colour |
| `eyes`, `mouth`, `cheeks` | face planes whose UVs the renderer shifts to the current frame |

**Required and optional bones.** The tree above has 66 bones when every option is present (v1's 61 plus `ahoge`,
`sleeve_r`, `sleeve_l`, `tail_4`, `tail_5`). 33 are **required** in every
model (root, body, waist, chest, neck, head, face, eyes, mouth, cheeks, face_ctrl, the 10 arm bones with their
locators and weapon bones, sash, the 6 leg bones and the 5 `vfx_*` locators); every other bone exists only when a part
uses it (a hero without wings has no `wing_*` bones). Extra tails beyond the first chain use 2 bones each
(`tail_b_1`, `tail_b_2`, ...), and every `p2_`/`p3_`/`fine_` group is its own bone, because visibility is per bone.

**Budgets** (validator, PROPOSED, style pass 2): at most **100 bones** (typical 50-80; generators add chain and
`fine_` bones), at most **300 cubes** (typical 130-230; the six style fixtures use 131-225, of which 68-90 are hair
strands) and at most **130 cubes outside `fine_` bones** (what is still drawn beyond the fine distance; the fixtures
54-92); texture 256x128 (every fixture fits) or 256x256 for winged heroes, many tails or very large weapons; at most 24
animations in a hero's own file. Why 300 and not the references' 300-500: up to about 50 heroes on screen, each vertex
transformed on the CPU every frame; with the LOD rule (4.9) 12 near heroes at about 230 cubes and 38 far ones at about
90 cost about 6,200 cube-renders, against about 22,500 at 450 cubes each (`notes/artgen.md`, "Cube budget"). Texture memory: 256x128 RGBA is 128 KiB (256x256: 256 KiB), plus the same
for a glow mask and a phase texture when present; textures load when a hero is first drawn, so a campaign with 8-40
heroes on screen holds 2-15 MiB, and all 200 at once would be about 25-75 MiB. Bones are cheap next to cubes (one matrix push per
bone; only keyed bones are sampled), so the cube budget is the one that protects frame time.

### 4.4 Animation controllers

GeckoLib runs controllers in order; a later controller overrides an earlier one on the bones and channels its current
animation keys.

| Controller | Transition | Plays | Driven by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base` | 4 ticks | `move.idle`, `combat.idle`, `personal.1-3` (rare idles), `move.walk`, `move.run`, `move.swim`, `move.fall`, `move.climb`, `move.crouch`, `rest.sit`, `rest.sleep`, `carry.loop` | speed, `onGround`, `isInWater`, pose, weapon-drawn flag, mood |
| `action` | 2 ticks | every action of the code table (appendix D), `death` | `DATA_ACTION` (a new sequence number = a new action), `isDeadOrDying()`, vanilla `swinging` for work hits |
| `secondary` | 6 ticks | `sec.*` loops for bones no base or action animation keys (G3: a controller overrides per bone and channel, so a `sec.tail` that keys `tail_1` would erase the follow-through baked into walk, run and every swing; the library bakes the rig's chain bones, and part-declared chains sway procedurally, below) | always on; Molang `query.ground_speed` scales the sway |
| face (Java, not a controller) | | eye, mouth and cheek frames | section 4.6 |

Procedural touches in `HeroModel.setCustomAnimations` (cheap, every frame): head look (the generated animations key
`neck`, never `head`, so the look stays free); cape lift and tail lag from velocity and turn rate, and the sway of the
chains a hero's parts declare (twin tails, scarf ends), with their stiffness and damping from `visual.json`
`secondary` (G3), added after the animation pass; a small hit flinch from `hurtTime` (skipped under super armour); ears flatten while hurt or
angry; bone visibility for `fine_`, phase prefixes, weapon and items. GeckoLib bones are shared by every instance of a
model, so visibility is set on every frame (heroes are unique, so there is normally one instance).

**Form-scaled keys (G4).** Kneels, sits, sleep and death drop the hips by an amount that depends on the legs' length
(5 px on a chibi, 11 on an adult), so the core library writes those root heights as the Molang expression
`"v + k * v.fc_dhip"`. `HeroModel.applyMolangQueries` sets the variable every frame for the model being drawn:
`MathParser.setVariable("v.fc_dhip", () -> leg_r.getPivotY() - 18.25)` (the `leg_r` bone's pivot y is the hip height;
18.25 is the middle of the forms' 12 and 24.5). GeckoLib creates an unknown variable with the value 0 (checked in the
4.9.3 jar), so without this line every form plays the middle-form compromise, up to 3 px off on chibis and adults.
`death` lays the body down itself: `HeroRenderer` returns 0 from `getDeathMaxRotation` (GeckoLib's own death roll would
add a sideways 90 degrees), and heroes sleep and sit on the ground through `rest.sleep` and `rest.sit`, not vanilla's
`Pose.SLEEPING` (which rotates the model again); the entity stands at the hips, the sleeping body lies toward +z.

### 4.5 Where an animation comes from

`HeroModel.getAnimationResource` returns the hero's own file; `getAnimationResourceFallbacks` returns its style
library, then the core library. GeckoLib looks a name up in that order, so a hero overrides any shared animation by
defining it.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `_core.animation.json` | movement, rest, carry, work (16), emotes (10), hurt, stagger, stun, interrupt, death, defeated, generic dodge, intro and victory |
| `_style_<style>.animation.json` (16) | `combat.idle`, `move.run` arm carry, `attack.1-3`, `attack.ranged`, `guard` (`.loop`, `.hit`), `dodge`, `draw`, `sheathe`, `intro`, `victory`, `taunt` |
| `<hero>.animation.json` | `move.<id>` for each move, `ultimate` (`.loop`, `.end`), `phase.2`, `phase.3`, `intro`, `personal.1-3`, `state.<name>`, any override |

The generator always writes a hero's own file (an empty `"animations": {}` is valid), because GeckoLib throws when a
file in the chain is missing; a unit test resolves every action code for every hero through the chain. Shared
libraries may key optional bones (`tail_1`, `cape_1`, `ear_l`): GeckoLib skips a keyed bone the model lacks as long as
`GeoModel.crashIfBoneMissing()` stays false, its default (checked in `AnimationController.process`). One `HeroModel`
serves every hero: `getBakedModel` switches the processor's active model when the resource changes (checked), which
re-registers that model's bones, a cost of a few microseconds per hero per frame.

### 4.6 Faces: blinking and expressions

Texture frames on flat planes, not eyelid cubes: anime faces are 2D drawings, and frames can draw closed arcs, happy
eyes, crossed pain eyes and wide surprise, which cubes cannot. Bones still move where depth helps (ears, tails,
ponytails, a visor, a mask).

- **Resolution**: the head's front face maps 10x10 px to 20x20 texels (double density); the rest of the model,
  hair included (rig v3: the strands are cubes now), is one texel per pixel like vanilla skins.
- **Planes**: `eyes` 10x5 px (20x10 texels, eyes and brows), `mouth` 4x1.5 px (8x3 texels), `cheeks` 8x1 px (16x2
  texels). Bangs sit in front of them. Rig v3 puts the eyes low and wide like the references (eye centre 0.30 of the
  head height above the chin, was 0.45). Where the planes sit on the 10x10 head front (head-local px: y 0 is the chin,
  the head spans x -5..5, y 0..10, with its front at z -5; face rows of 0.5 px counted from the top of the face):

| Plane | x | y (head-local) | Face rows | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (head front) | -5..5 | 6.5..10 | 0-6 | forehead (under the bangs) |
| `eyes` | -5..5 | 1.5..6.5 | 7-16 | texel rows 0-1 brows, row 2 skin, row 3 the upper lash (lower for a shape whose corner sits lower), rows 4-9 the eye body |
| `mouth` | -2..2 | 0..1.5 | 17-19 | mouth (its line on the middle row) and chin |
| `cheeks` | -4..4 | 0.5..1.5 | 17-18 | blush, sweat drop, tears (transparent elsewhere; in front of the mouth plane's edges) |

- **One skin layer** (style pass 2): skin, jaw shading and the face marks are drawn once; the head front is that layer
  with holes, every eye frame starts from its rows 7-16 and every mouth frame from rows 17-19, so marks, glasses and an
  eye patch cross the planes without seams and blink with the eyes. The face system (eye shape families, iris, pupil and
  highlight styles, lashes, brows, mouths, blush, marks, glasses, eye patch) is in 13.5.

- **No z-fighting by construction.** The 20x20 head-front texture is **transparent (alpha 0) exactly under the `eyes`
  and `mouth` planes**, which are fully opaque, so the planes sit 0.05 px in front of the head with nothing behind them
  to fight (a 0.02 px offset with a painted face behind it would flicker beyond about 30 blocks with a 24-bit depth
  buffer). The `cheeks` plane is cut-out (only blush pixels are opaque), sits 0.1 px in front, and is hidden beyond 16
  blocks with the face freeze. `herogen check` verifies the alpha holes and that no cube face in front of the face,
  rotation included, reaches below head-local y 5.5 within |x| < 4.5 (bangs may cover the brows, never the lash or the
  eyes; a hair cap open at the front may enclose the head); side locks outside that width may hang lower. The strand
  generator shortens fringe strands to keep that rule by construction.
- **Frames** (fixed texture regions, the same for every hero):

| Strip | Region (u, v) | Frames |
|---|---|---|
| eyes | 64-143, 0-29 (4 x 3 grid of 20x10) | 0 open, 1 half, 2 closed, 3 happy, 4 angry, 5 sad, 6 surprised, 7 pain, 8 focused, 9 smug, 10 glowing, 11 sleeping |
| mouth | 64-127, 30-32 (8 of 8x3) | 0 neutral, 1 smile, 2 talk A, 3 talk B or shout, 4 grit teeth, 5 frown, 6 cat mouth, 7 fang grin |
| cheeks | 64-127, 33-34 (4 of 16x2) | 0 none, 1 blush, 2 sweat drop, 3 tears |

- **Renderer** (`HeroRenderer`, about 40 lines): `renderCubesOfBone` sets a UV shift for the bones named `eyes`,
  `mouth` and `cheeks` from the entity's `HeroFace`; `createVerticesOfQuad` adds the shift to each vertex's UV. GeckoLib
  bakes UVs **normalised** (texel / texture size, checked in `GeoQuad.build`), so the shift is `frame offset in texels /
  texture width` (and height): 256 wide (and 128 or 256 high), read from the baked model's texture size, never
  hard-coded. The
  glow layer renders through the same path, so the glow mask's eye frames line up. Plan B if it ever conflicts with
  something: one plane per frame, shown or hidden with `GeoBone.setHidden` (more cubes, no renderer code).
- **Who picks the frame** (`HeroFace`, client, per entity; highest first):
  1. dead or defeated: eyes closed, mouth neutral;
  2. authored keys: the running action animation keys `face_ctrl` (`position.x` = eye frame + 1, `position.y` = mouth
     frame + 1, `position.z` = cheek frame + 1, 0 = no override; every change is a jump, written as a Bedrock
     pre/post key: GeckoLib's `step` easing is two steps and would sit on the midpoint frame for half a segment, G3):
     how an ultimate shouts at 0.4 s or a victory winks. The value is read from the bone after the animation pass and used only when it is within 0.05 of a
     whole number: controller transitions and GeckoLib's reset lerp pass through fractional values for a few ticks,
     and those must not flash a wrong frame;
  3. talking (the `talking` flag while a line shows): the mouth cycles talk A, talk B, neutral every 2-3 ticks with
     a little noise, the eyes follow the mood;
  4. mood (`DATA_MOOD`): a table maps each of 12 moods to an eye, mouth and cheek frame, with per-hero overrides from
     `visual.json` (a stoic hero's "happy" is calm eyes and a faint smile); a change holds at least 10 ticks;
  5. blink, over open, focused, angry, sad and smug eyes: next blink after a random 2.5-6 s (personality profile:
     `nervous` 1.5-3.5 s, `calm` 4-7 s, `sleepy` 3-5 s with a slow 0.4 s blink); a blink is half 1 tick, closed 2,
     half 1; 15% chance of a double blink; seeded from the entity UUID so heroes never blink in step; **no blinking
     during a channel or a wind-up** (the server sets `DATA_STATE` bit 6 for both), itself a readable "it is
     concentrating" tell;
  6. distance: beyond 16 blocks the face stays on the mood frame (no blink, no talk).
- **Moods**: `calm`, `happy`, `angry`, `focused`, `hurt`, `tired`, `sad`, `smug`, `surprised`, `asleep`, `flustered`,
  `determined`. The server picks the mood from a small priority stack: hurt wince 0.5 s > wind-up focus > phase or
  enrage > personality reaction (a kill, a spare, a yield, a gift) > situation (night, working, idle at home) >
  temperament default. No network traffic for blinking; mood and talking are a byte and a bit.

### 4.8 Server-driven animation and agreed timings

- **One synced integer** (`DATA_ACTION`, section 10.1) carries the action code, a 4-bit sequence number, a playback
  speed and a variant. The server's move runner sets it at the start of each wind-up; vanilla sends changed entity
  data to every tracking player in the same tick, and a player who starts tracking mid-cast receives the current value
  too (GeckoLib's own `triggerAnim` packets do not reach late trackers and carry no speed, so they are not used).
- **The client** sees a new sequence number, calls `forceAnimationReset()`, sets the speed and plays the animation
  named by the code (`move.<id>` for move codes, resolved through the hero's `visual.json` move list). Channels and
  loops (`guard.loop`, `stun.loop`, `ultimate.loop`) play until the next code or code 0.
- **No blend on stop.** When the `action` controller stops, GeckoLib does not transition out: the bones it keyed snap
  to whatever the `base` controller sets that frame (only bones no controller keys ease back to the rest pose, over the
  model's reset time; checked in `AnimationProcessor.tickAnimation`). So every action animation's last key equals the
  first key of the style's `combat.idle` (or `move.idle` for work and emotes) on every bone it keys; the generator
  appends that settle key and `herogen check` enforces it (13.6).
- **Late trackers** (a player who comes into range mid-cast) start the animation from 0 while the server is already
  part-way through the wind-up, so their swing lands visibly late for that one move. Accepted: it is rare and only
  cosmetic.
- **Timings agree by construction.** Every combat animation carries markers in its Bedrock `timeline` as GeckoLib
  custom-instruction keyframes, for example `{"0.45": "hit;vfx:slash_arc", "0.70": "hit"}`. The generator writes the
  move's `frames` (wind-up = first `hit`, the active hits, recovery = length minus last hit) into the server
  definition from the same recipe, and `HeroAssetsTest` parses both from `src/main/resources` and fails on any
  difference above one tick. As built (G3): every animation file has a **server timing file**,
  `data/factionclash/hero_timings/<file>.json` (`_core`, `_style_<style>`, `<hero>`, the client's chain), listing per
  animation its length and ticks, loop mode, kind, class, every marker with its tick, and for one with `hit` or `cast`
  markers its `frames` and `frame_ticks` (basic attacks live in style libraries, so the server needs those too); the
  server definition's `generated.moves` comes from the same clips (`anim/timing.py`). Markers sit on whole ticks,
  before a clip's last tick (GeckoLib ends a clip on that frame before it fires the frame's events).
- **The controller blend comes first (G3 review, read in GeckoLib 4.9.3's `AnimationController`).** A controller that
  starts an animation first blends from the current pose to the clip's first frame for its transition length T
  (`action` 2 ticks, `base` 4), then plays the clip from 0; keyframe events are not delayed (they fire once clip tick +
  T reaches the event's tick). So a pose at clip time p shows T after the start, and an event written at p would fire
  T early: the server's hit would land 2 ticks before the swing shows it. The generator therefore writes every event
  at p + T, and timing files and `frames` count from the action's start (the server's frame of reference):
  `attack.1` cuts at clip time 0.40 s, its `hit` is written at 0.50 s, and its server wind-up is 10 ticks.
  Timing files (format 2) state each animation's `transition_ticks` and `total_ticks`; `HeroModel`'s controllers must
  use these transition lengths, and the Java test compares them.
- **Speed**: haste, slowness and the attack-speed stat divide wind-up and recovery by `s` (0.5-2.0) on the server and
  send `speed = s`, so the `hit` marker still lands on the server's hit tick (GeckoLib scales the whole controller
  clock, the blend included, so the frames above divide by `s` exactly).
- **Latency**: the client starts one tick plus ping after the server; the damage the server applies at the wind-up's
  end reaches the client after the same delay, so the swing and the hurt flash line up on screen.
- **Interrupts**: a stun or a knockback above 0.5 during a wind-up cancels it (v1 rule, half the cooldown back) and
  sends `interrupt`; a poise break sends `stagger`. The 2-tick transition cuts the swing cleanly.
- **Chains (G4)**: a one-shot that leads into a loop ends on the loop's first frame, so the server plays them back to
  back: `stun` then `stun.loop`; `defeated` then `defeated.loop`, then `spared` or `death`; `work.fish_cast`, then
  `work.fish_wait` (its `.loop` follows), then `work.fish_reel`. A code 0 between them snaps to the base pose. `death`
  holds its last frame (`hold_on_last_frame`). G4 review: 0.75 s, so with the 2-tick blend it ends on tick 17 and
  the body lies still before vanilla removes the entity at deathTime 20 (1.0 s ran past it: the hero vanished as it
  landed).
- **Movement moves** (`dash`, `leap`, `charge`) stay physical on the server (no root motion); the animation is
  authored in place with the same duration, and the leap's airtime comes from the animation length.
- **Work**: today's jobs already call `swing(hand)` for block breaking; while the action controller is idle, a fresh
  swing plays `work.mine`, `work.chop`, `work.till` or `attack.1` by the held tool. Longer work (fishing, cooking,
  smithing, praying, reading) sends its `work.*` code. As built (G4), the swing-driven strokes (and `work.build`,
  `emote.greet`, `emote.wave`, `emote.talk`) key only the upper body, so a hero can swing or wave while it walks.
- **Client-only effects**: sound keyframes; particle keyframes and `vfx:*` markers spawn particles at locator bones
  (world positions with bone tracking on for locators only). Everything that affects play stays on the server.
- **Sound markers** (S1, `SOUND.md` section 5): a key's `sound` and a marker's `sfx:<id>` part name a full id,
  `minecraft:<event>` or `factionclash:hero.<set>.<name>` from the catalogue in `herosrc/sfx` (a bare id is a vanilla
  one, as `ResourceLocation.parse` reads it); `sfxgen check` fails on any other. Swings, throws and recovery details
  are markers; wind-ups, impacts, the big releases (ultimates), states, passives and the boss beats are played by the
  server (`SOUND.md` section 7, the slot rule), so every nearby player hears them whatever the client animates.

### 4.9 Level of detail and culling

`fine_` bones hidden beyond 20 blocks (style pass 2: hair strands, rolled tail boxes, frills, bows and small plates
are `fine_`; at most 130 cubes stay, the fixtures keep 54-92 of 131-225, about 45-60% fewer), face frozen beyond 16,
`secondary` controller
paused beyond 32, locator tracking off beyond 24, vanilla frustum culling with the inflated box (section 3.1). An
unknown hero id on the client renders `_fallback.geo.json` (the neutral rig) tinted with the faction colour.

Replacing the enum with an open class was rejected: it changes every signature, every `EnumMap`/`EnumSet`, the sync
type and the GameTests, for more than eight factions, which map size and tick time do not allow anyway.

### 5.2 The leader draft

- **Pool**: every loaded hero with `leader.eligible` not false (all 200 built-ins are eligible). Weights by rarity 4,
  4, 2, 1 (common, rare, epic, legendary; PROPOSED).
- **Rules**, by a seeded greedy draw with up to 200 retries, relaxed from the last to the first when they cannot all
  hold: (1) no two leaders share an archetype; (2) primary colours differ by at least 25 (CIE76 delta-E) **and no two
  share a `dye_primary`** (wool, carpets and beds can only show the 16 dyes, so two factions 25 apart in RGB could
  still build in the same wool; rule 2 is never relaxed below "different dyes"); (3) no two
  share a temperament; (4) with two or more factions both genders are present; (5) with three or more, not every
  leader has aggression 70 or more, and at least one has aggression 55 or more.
- **Seed**: `leaders.seed` when set, else world seed XOR the campaign start game time; the seed is printed at start,
  so a cast can be reproduced for tests and bug reports.
- **Overrides**: `leaders.fixed = ["<hero id>", ...]` and `/fc start <radius> [factions] [hero ids...]` fill slots
  first (tab-completed); the draw fills the rest.

### 5.3 Identity from the leader

The leader's `leader` block (section 12.12) supplies two or more faction names, a short name, a title, a motto, three
colours, a banner (base dye and 2-4 vanilla pattern layers; never the company-logo pattern), a base style, a culture
and an economy focus. When a field is missing, `logic/leader/FactionNameGen` (seeded, generic English word banks keyed
by archetype motifs and temperament group nouns, uniqueness enforced in the campaign) and the archetype tables fill
it. Banners and other dyed accents are decoration: they stay out of the repair plan (as beds and signs are today), so a
base never waits for a blue banner.

**Base styles**: 16 families (appendix B). Every wall, floor, roof and perimeter block belongs to an existing
`MaterialRules` group, or is added to `STONE_BLOCKS` (cut sandstone, smooth stone, mud bricks, tuff bricks, polished
tuff, red sandstone, polished diorite, quartz bricks), so builders repair with substitutes exactly as today.

### 5.4 The personality model

| Trait | Low (0) | High (100) | Drives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aggression` (a) | avoids war | seeks battle | raid rate, attack odds, soldier share, stranger hostility, its own fight AI |
| `honour` (h) | anything goes | keeps its word | betrayal, declarations, sparing the beaten, duels, ambushes |
| `ambition` (m) | content | expansionist | recruitment pace, building ahead, glory targets, enchanting, tribute |
| `caution` (c) | reckless | careful | garrison kept, attack threshold, food reserve, repairs, ranged share |
| `greed` (g) | generous | acquisitive | loot weight, storage, trade terms, payment conditions |
| `sociability` (s) | isolationist | outgoing | pacts, opinion recovery, traders, festivals, welcome to strangers |
| `loyalty` (l) | fickle | steadfast | joining allies, leaving losing allies, defection, raid cohesion |
| `volatility` (v) | predictable | whimsical | noise on every decision (sigma 0.2 x v), whims |

- **Temperament** (one of 30, appendix A) sets default values for all eight traits, the fight-AI defaults, dialogue
  tone, face defaults and at most one special rule. Authoring rule: each trait may differ from its temperament default
  by at most 20 (ADJUSTED otherwise), so the label stays true.
- **Fight AI from personality** (any v1 `behaviour` field the file sets wins): aggression word = bold when a >= 65,
  cautious when a <= 35, else steady; `retreat_below` = role default + 20 (c - 0.5) - 10 (a - 0.5), clamped 0-60;
  focus `heroes_first` for hot-blooded and proud temperaments, `strongest` for battle maniacs and the righteous,
  `weakest` for schemers and tricksters, else by role; a hero with h >= 60 never strikes a yielding enemy.
- **Scaling**: every formula reads `t_eff = clamp(0.5 + k (t - 0.5), 0, 1)` with `k = leaders.personalityStrength /
  100` (0-2, default 1). `k = 0` makes every faction neutral, which reproduces today's numbers. The clamp matters: at
  `k = 2` an unclamped trait reaches -0.5 or 1.5, and products such as `a x m` in the raid target cost would turn two
  very low traits into a positive "glory" term.

### 5.6 The leader in the world

- **Founding**: `BaseBuilder.buildSettlement` spawns the leader beside the reporting station after the starting
  roster; it says its `founding` line to the faction ("From today this land is the Still Crane's.").
- **`court` duty**: the leader keeps to the capital (walks between the station, the heart and the gates; stands at
  the station when a member player is near so it can be talked to), answers every alarm (`protects: heart` during a
  defence), and raids as `LeaderBiases` says. A leader whose first talent belongs to a job with a workplace at the base
  (a forgemaster, a chef, an angler, a surveyor, a sage of letters once the base has an enchanting table) spends
  daylight at that workplace and counts as that worker. Jobs that travel far (scout, trader, cave explorer, hauler) are
  never a leader's: such a leader patrols instead, and its talent still works at the base.
- **Announcements in the leader's voice**: raid launched, base attacked, base lost, hero fallen, new recruit
  milestones; the leader's line first, then today's plain message. Temperament fallback lines cover missing events.
- **Death**: announced to everyone; no new raids until it returns unless a >= 0.7, in which case one vengeance raid
  goes against the killer's faction; a raid it was on falls back when `(c + 1 - l) / 2 > 0.5`. Revival after 10
  minutes, like every hero (3.4, death-related abilities included), at the capital or the nearest standing base. (Morale effects arrive with milestone 2.)
- **Elimination** (last base falls; `CampaignManager.eliminate` kills every NPC today): heroes are no longer killed;
  the leader becomes an exiled free hero, never a leader again in this campaign, recruitable later (milestone 3).
- **Succession** happens only with `leaders.permadeath` (off): the faction's highest-level hero with compatibility
  0.5 or more takes over, else a regent keeps the saved personality. The faction name never changes mid-campaign.

### 5.7 Milestone 2: diplomacy v2, player standing, morale, edicts

Adopted from `research-systems.md` sections 2.6-2.8 and 4.3 as written there; summary:

- **Relations** per pair: `WAR` (today's default between known factions), `PEACE` (optionally timed: a truce), `PACT`
  (allied: `sameSide`, joint raids, shared reports). Contact rule: a leader deals only with factions its scouts have
  reported or its people have fought. Messages travel "by envoy" through the reports system.
- **Opinion ledger** (-100..100) with grudges, a public **reputation** (0-100), and a **daily council** that proposes,
  accepts, keeps or breaks agreements: `P = 0.35(s-0.5) + 0.30(c-0.5) - 0.35(a-0.5) + 0.25 clamp(T-1,0,2) + O/200 +
  N`, offer when P > 0.15; betrayal = inclination x opportunity, where inclination = `0.5(m-0.5) + 0.5(g-0.5) +
  0.4(a-0.5) - 0.8(h-0.5) - 0.6(l-0.5)`; honourable leaders (h >= 0.75) declare instead of betraying.
- Today's **pact against a conqueror** becomes a coalition each leader joins when its fear passes 0.8; with `k = 0` or
  no leader every survivor joins, exactly today's rule.
- **Players**: a standing per player per faction; strangers are welcomed, ignored, warned or attacked by the leader's
  thresholds, never at first sight; joining may need standing; members earn ranks and can petition the leader.
- **Morale** per base (0-100) from events, with Inspired / Steady / Shaken / Despairing bands that change work speed,
  retreat thresholds and recruitment.
- **Edicts** chosen by the council (festival, martial law, conscription, open or closed gates, trade drive, bounty,
  tribute demand, fortify) and small whims for volatile leaders.

Each has its own config switch (`leaders.diplomacy`, `strangerStance`, `morale`, `edicts`); with all of them off and
`personalityStrength = 0` the campaign plays as today.

## 6. Boss mechanics

What makes a fight feel like a boss in Minecraft (from vanilla's wither, dragon, warden and evoker and from action-game
boss craft, `research-archetypes.md` part 3 and `research-systems.md` section 3.1): presence (a bar, an entrance, a
title, a sound); durability with a per-hit ceiling; readable danger (every big attack has a visible, audible wind-up
whose length grows with the danger, then a recovery that is the punish window); patterns that react to the player;
phases that add a new mechanic; ways to win by skill (stagger, weak points, counters); pressure over time; arena sense;
and a defeat moment.

### 6.1 Two modes

- **Field mode** (default): v1 numbers; a hero is a strong officer in NPC wars (v1 war power: a level-20 hero in iron
  is about 2.3 soldiers). Moves, phases, animations and faces work the same; there is no boss bar, no health scaling,
  and ground decals are drawn only when a player is within 32 blocks.
- **Boss mode**: on while at least one player is **engaged** (it hurt the hero in the last 15 s or is its target,
  within 32 blocks; creative and spectator never count). It ends 10 s after the last engaged player leaves. Entering
  and leaving keep the current health share, so the bar never jumps.

### 6.2 Numbers (`logic/hero/BossRules`, pure)

| Rule | Value (PROPOSED) |
|---|---|
| Boss health | `fieldMax x health_scale / 100 x role factor x (1 + 0.5 (n - 1))`, n = engaged players, at most 5; one attribute modifier `factionclash:boss_health` (`ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL`) and `setHealth(share x newMax)` |
| Role factor | vanguard 1.0, striker 1.2, skirmisher, support and controller 1.25, marksman and summoner 1.3 (12.4): the fragile roles start from 24-26 field health against a vanguard's 34, so without it a support boss dies in two-thirds of a vanguard's time |
| `health_scale` default | common 250, rare 300, epic 350, legendary 400; a leader +50; allowed 200-600; config `boss.healthScale` (25-300%) multiplies every hero's value |
| Example | an epic vanguard leader at level 10 (field 34 + 9 x 0.8 = 41) has 165 health against one player, 247 against two. At the reference rate (iron sword, 1.6 swings a second, half of them landing) that is about 36 s of landed hits, and a fight is two to three times its hitting time once wind-ups are dodged and phases change: about 70-110 s. Every slice hero lands between 24 and 39 s of hitting (SLICE_PLAN.md criterion 15) |
| Damage cap from players | one player-sourced hit at most `boss.max_hit_share` (12%) of boss max health; the excess becomes poise damage |
| Damage to players | v1 slot caps (skill 16, ultimate 30), then x `damage_vs_players` (115%, allowed 100-140) |
| No one-shot | a hero hit never takes a player who was at full health below 1 health point |
| Healing | no out-of-combat regeneration while in boss mode; authored `heal` and `drain` apply |
| Attack tokens | at most `boss.attackTokens` (2) heroes **press** one player (melee and heavy moves) at a time; the others use ranged and support moves, guard allies or circle at 8-12 blocks; a token returns after the move's recovery; no tokens against NPCs |

### 6.3 The fight loop

```
INTRO (first engagement of the in-game day, 1.0-1.5 s, super armour)
  -> NEUTRAL (spacing, basic attacks, waits the phase's gap)
  -> MOVE: WINDUP -> ACTIVE -> RECOVERY   (a combo runs its strikes back to back)
  -> NEUTRAL ...
     | REACTION (overrides the next pick)  | TRANSITION (phase)  | STAGGER  | YIELD
```

- **Gap** between moves (NEUTRAL time): phase 1 1.5-2.5 s, phase 2 1.0-2.0 s, phase 3 0.6-1.5 s; enraged x0.7. Basic
  attacks continue during the gap.
- **The pick** (`logic/hero/MoveSelector`, every 5 ticks when NEUTRAL and the gap is over; seeded per entity). The
  candidates are the moves of the current phase that have a ready charge, have a valid target in range
  and whose v1 `use_when` holds (default `always`):

```
score(move) = weight                        0-10, default 5 (0: never picked, only a followup or a reaction)
            x band fit                      1 in its band, 0.25 in an adjacent band, else 0
                                            bands: close <= 4 blocks, mid 4-10, far 10-24
            x prefer fit                    2 when its prefer_when condition holds, else 1
            x novelty                       0 if it was the last move (unless repeatable), 0.35 if one of the last 3
            x posture                       1 + 0.6 (a - 0.5) for offensive moves, 1 + 0.6 (c - 0.5) for zoning
                                            and defensive ones (personality traits)
pick by weighted random among candidates with score > 0; none -> keep spacing and basic attacks
```

- **Followups**: when a move hits, each listed followup may start at once (its own chance), which is how recognisable
  strings form ("the dash, then the rising cut 70% of the time"). A followup replaces the recovery of the move it follows;
  only the last move of a string plays its full recovery, so the punish window comes at the end of the string. A string
  is at most 4 moves long (the validator rejects longer or cyclic followup chains).
- v1 rules stay: 1 s global cooldown between moves, cooldown floors from move weight, the ultimate's energy and its
  20-60 s minimum gap. In field mode the same selector runs with the phase gap replaced by the global cooldown.

### 6.4 Moves, classes and telegraphs

| Class | Minimum wind-up | Minimum recovery | Tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| `light` | 0.4 s | 0.3 s | animation |
| `heavy` (heavy melee, leap, dash, charge, grab) | 0.8 s | 0.8 s | animation + sound + line or landing decal |
| `area` (cone, nova, zone, wave, rain, sweep) | 1.0 s | 0.8 s | animation + ground decal + sound |
| `ultimate` | 1.5 s | 1.5 s | all of the above + a spoken line + a wide sound + screen darkening for engaged players (2 s: the vanilla boss-bar flag `ServerBossEvent.setDarkenScreen(true)`, as the wither uses; no mob effect) |

- The validator raises a too-short wind-up or recovery to the class minimum (ADJUSTED); the class is inferred from the
  form when the file does not give it: `melee`, `combo`, `shot`, `volley`, `bolt`, `barrage`, `counter`, `self`, `ally`,
  `allies` are light; `leap`, `dash`, `charge`, `grab`, `line`, `chain` heavy; `cone`, `nova`, `zone`, `wave`, `rain`,
  `sweep` area. An authored class may be heavier than the form's, never lighter, except for a move that deals no damage
  and applies no stun, freeze, knockback or pull to enemies (a taunt or a slow may be light; ADJUSTED otherwise).
  Telegraphs never shorten below these minimums, even in phase 3 or enraged.
- **Ground decals** (`hero/Telegraphs`): vanilla `Display.BlockDisplay` entities, no client assets, laid flat (scale y
  0.01) in the hero's accent colour (stained glass of the nearest dye for normal tells, purple for unblockable grabs,
  red for the ultimate): a disc for circles, a stretched strip for lines and charges, a fan of 3-5 strips for cones, a
  ring for waves, a landing disc for leaps, one disc per impact for rain. They **grow from 0 to full size over the
  wind-up** (display transformation interpolation): a readable charging timer. A block display is a square, so a
  "disc" is two flat squares, the second turned 45 degrees (an octagon), and a ring is up to 12 tangent strips. The
  growth needs two steps: the decal spawns at scale 0 and, one tick later, gets its full-size transformation with
  `interpolation_duration` = the remaining wind-up ticks and delay 0 (a change sent in the spawn tick is applied without
  interpolation). Brightness is overridden to 15 so decals read at night and in caves. In 1.21.1 the setters are
  private (`Display.setTransformation`, `setTransformationInterpolationDuration`, `setTransformationInterpolationDelay`,
  `setBrightnessOverride`, `setViewRange`, `BlockDisplay.setBlockState`, checked in the decompiled sources), so an
  access transformer exposes them (fallback: build the entity from NBT: `transformation`, `interpolation_duration`,
  `start_interpolation`, `brightness`, `block_state`). At most 12 decals per hero (a disc counts 2), tagged, removed at
  the hit, on the hero's death, and on chunk load (left-overs from a crash).
- Wind-up start: signature-colour particles and the move's sound, positioned so players hear the direction; the face
  goes `focused` and stops blinking; ultimates speak their line.

passive.

### 6.7 Phases

- 1-3 phases (`boss.phases`, each with `at` = the health share at which it begins: 100, then for example 60 and 25).
  Common and rare heroes have 2 by default and may author a third, which they have from level 1 (the first draft
  unlocked it at level 15; changed with the owner's answer of 2026-11-12, section 8); epic, legendary and every
  leader have 3.
- **Transition** (0.8-2.0 s): 50% less damage taken and no stagger (super armour, shown by a glint), never
  invulnerable; a shockwave pushes everyone within 4 blocks back (strength 2, no damage, hazard-aware) to reset
  spacing; the phase line, a face change and the phase look (`p2_`/`p3_` bones, `_p2.png`, eyes glowing in the last
  phase); then the new moves, stats or states apply, and the boss bar gains its phase pip (`◆◆◇`).
- Validator NOTE when a phase adds no new move, add, state or mechanic ("phase 2 only changes numbers").
- `rebirth` (legendary only): once per fight, lethal damage instead starts a final phase at 30%; the bar shows it as
  an extra pip from the start, so it is never a surprise.

### 6.8 Reactions: reading the player

`boss.reactions`: up to 8 `{ when, move, chance, cooldown }`, checked every 5 ticks; a true `when` with a ready move
and a passed chance replaces the next pick (never a move in progress).

| `when` | Typical answer |
|---|---|
| `target_blocking` | a `breaks_guard` strike, a grab, or walking round the shield |
| `target_drawing_bow`, `target_far` | a gap closer, a barrage back, or a stance that advances |
| `target_eating` | a rush ("punish the heal"), mostly heroes with a >= 60 |
| `target_airborne`, `target_above` | an anti-air hit, a pull |
| `target_behind` | a turning sweep or a backstep |
| `target_unreachable` | far-band moves, then the anti-cheese rules (6.12) |
| `self_health_below`, ally hero low | a defensive or protective move (loyal heroes save allies first) |

### 6.9 Poise, stagger, punish windows, weak points, counters

- **Poise** (`boss.poise`, 50-200; default by role: vanguard 140, striker 100, skirmisher, support and controller 80,
  marksman and summoner 70): each player hit adds `damage x weapon factor` (sword 1.0, axe 1.6, mace 2.0 plus its fall
  bonus, trident 1.2, arrow 0.6, crossbow bolt 0.8); a **parry** (blocking a heavy move with a shield raised in the
  last 6 ticks; vanilla only blocks once a shield has been up 5 ticks, so this is `isBlocking()` with
  `getTicksUsingItem()` 5-11 when the hit lands) adds 25; a `charge` ending against a wall adds 40; damage over the per-hit cap converts 1:1. Poise
  decays 15 per second after 3 s without poise damage.
- **Stagger** at full poise: 2.5 s kneeling dizzy (`stagger` animation, stars, `on_stagger` passives), +25% damage
  taken; poise resets and its maximum grows 20% for the rest of the fight (no stun-lock). No stagger during
  transitions, an ultimate's wind-up or moves marked `super_armor`.
- **Punish windows**: during recovery the hero takes +15% damage, and +15% more from behind (more than 120 degrees
  from its facing).
- **Shields**: light moves are blockable like any hit; `breaks_guard` moves disable a blocking shield for 5 s;
  `unblockable` moves (grabs) always use the purple decal.
- **Weak points** (`boss.weak_points`, 1-4; the validator asks for at least one weakness: an element resistance of -10
  or lower, or a weak point):

| Weak point | Effect | Typical heroes |
|---|---|---|
| `back` | the backstab bonus doubles | shield bearers, knights |
| `recovery` | the recovery bonus doubles | heavy hitters, quick-draw duellists |
| `charge_wall` | wall stagger on its own charge | chargers |
| `channel` | a channelled move breaks after `interrupt` damage (15% of max) and gives a 2 s recovery | casters, priests, brewers |
| `iron` / `gold` | +15% from iron / golden weapons | folklore spirits / undead and fiends |
| `snowball`, `water` | a snowball hit or a water splash douses a fire hero (its burning state ends, -10% damage for 5 s) or startles a water-hating one | fire heroes, cats |
| `bell` | ringing a bell within 8 blocks stuns a spirit hero 1 s (once per 30 s) | spirits, undead |
| `light` | a torch or glowstone a player places within 4 blocks weakens a shadow hero 10% for 10 s | shadow and night heroes |
| `crit_above` | a falling critical hit staggers at once (a talisman on the forehead, a dish on the head) | hopping revenants, kappa |

  Each weak point shows on the hero sheet and is hinted by a dialogue line or a visible prop. Every trigger is a
  vanilla event a player can cause: `iron`/`gold` the attacker's main-hand `TieredItem` tier; `snowball` a
  `ProjectileImpactEvent` of a `Snowball` on the hero; `water` a thrown water bottle (`ThrownPotion` with water
  contents) hitting it, or the rising edge of `isInWaterRainOrBubble`; `bell` a player using a bell block
  (`PlayerInteractEvent.RightClickBlock`) or a projectile hitting one within 8 blocks; `light` a player placing a block
  with light emission 12 or more within 4 blocks (`BlockEvent.EntityPlaceEvent`); `crit_above` NeoForge's
  `CriticalHitEvent` from an attacker falling from above the hero's eyes.
- **Counter stance** (`counter` form): a melee hit during the stance is answered at once and mostly absorbed; the
  answer is ranged damage or waiting it out.

### 6.10 Enrage, reset, leash

- **Soft enrage** after `boss.enrage.after` s in boss mode (leaders 240, others 180; allowed 120-300): +15% damage,
  gap x0.7, poise maximum x1.25, the bar turns red, an angry face and line. All damage bonuses together never exceed
  +25%.
- **Hard enrage triggers** (chosen per hero): `ally_hero_died` nearby, `heart_attacked` while defending,
  `favoured_hurt` (its favoured hero or leader is hit).
- **Reset**: no engaged player for 10 s ends boss mode; 30 s out of combat heals 1% of field max health per second;
  a phase re-arms when health climbs 10% above its threshold. A leashed hero walks back to its post; it never
  teleports.

### 6.11 Adds

| Kind | What | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| `call_guards` | real soldiers of its faction take its target; nothing spawned | 1-4 per use |
| v1 `summon` | vanilla wolf, snow golem, iron golem (v1 §20) | v1 caps: 3 per hero, 12 per faction |
| `decoy` (milestone 1) | `hero/HeroDecoy`: renders **the owner hero's own model** (same GeckoLib model, synced owner id), copies its current action with a 2-tick delay, walks a short path, has 1 health and **no shadow** (the real hero has the vanilla shadow, the tell) and **no footstep sounds** (a second tell: players can switch entity shadows off, and shader packs often draw no vanilla shadow), pops with a smoke puff on any hit or after 10 s | 3 per hero |

### 6.12 Arena awareness and anti-cheese

- Every 2 s in boss mode the hero samples its surroundings: ceiling height (3 blocks or less: no `leap`, no knock-up
  moves, thrusts preferred), water and lava along dash and charge paths (those moves are skipped), drops over 4 blocks
  along a path (dashes and charges stop short), open sky (needed by visual lightning and `rain` outdoors).
- **Hazard-aware knockback** on players: if the landing area 4 blocks away has lava, fire, a drop over 6 or the void,
  the push is cut to 0.5 strength. No cheap deaths.
- **Unreachable target**: a hero first uses its mobility, at once, to reach a player on a pillar, in a pen or behind a
  wall (climb, leap, glide, flight, a reach flight outside aerial phases; on a player who left no cell beside him, a
  basic attack from the cling, the jump's apex or the air: `MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 3.7, the owner's answer of
  2026-11-12). When no link reaches (no path, or no melee reach for 5 s): far-band moves; after 10 s the hero uses the
  NPCs' own tools at player speed (`DigOut` pillaring and bridging, `BlockWork` breaking) to reach the player, exactly
  as a player would; after 30 s it disengages with a taunt line and resets. A hero without any far-band move gets a
  NOTE.
- **Home ground**: the `in_home_base` condition lets a hero use its walls (a bell rung to call guards, a stand at the
  heart).

### 6.13 Intro, boss bar, yield and rewards

- **Intro**: the first engagement between a player and a hero in an in-game day plays the `intro` animation (1.0-1.5
  s, super armour, 50% less damage), sends a title to engaged players (`ClientboundSetTitleTextPacket`: name in its
  colour, subtitle "title · faction", fade 10/40/10 ticks) and says the `intro` line.
- **Boss bar** (`ServerBossEvent`, `hero/HeroBossBars`): `★ Name · title  ◆◆◇` plus `⚡` when the ultimate is ready;
  colour = the nearest of the 7 bar colours to the hero's primary, red when enraged; shown to players within 32 blocks
  while boss mode is on; a second hero adds a second bar.
- **Yield**: in boss mode a killing blow from a player or a player's projectile is cancelled at 1 health; the hero
  kneels for 5 s, invulnerable and passive (`defeated.loop`, a defeat face, its `yield` line); engaged players see an
  action-bar prompt "Sneak and use to spare, strike to finish". **Spare**: it walks home (no combat for 10 minutes,
  counted as a defeat, not a death, no gear dropped; its `spared` line; standing and opinion effects in milestone 2).
  **Finish**: it dies (revival timer, v1 drops). No choice in 5 s: it collapses and dies. Killed by NPCs or in field
  mode: a 1 s defeat beat, then death. **Refused yield**: a hero with honour 30 or less, or a leader with caution 70
  or more, does not kneel but escapes at 1 health, once in 10 minutes: boss mode ends, nothing drops, no rewards, and
  the next hit that reaches it kills it (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 7.7).
- **Rewards** for a player-defeated hero: XP orbs (`7 x level`, at most 100), its non-kit gear, a `hero_crest`
  trophy item (named, one per hero per player per in-game day), and (milestone 3) a chance of a token fragment.

### 6.14 Fairness rules (enforced by the mod, not by authors)

1. Every telegraph meets its class minimum; no move is both unblockable and without a decal.
2. Stuns on players stay v1's Slowness and Mining Fatigue; knock-ups lift at most 1 block; grabs hold at most 1 s.
3. No single hero hit takes a player from full health to zero.
4. At most 2 heroes press one player.
5. Hazard-aware knockback (6.12).
6. Boss mode never starts against creative or spectator players; the damage cap applies only to player sources, so NPC
   wars are unaffected.
7. Mobility, as counterplay rather than limits on where a hero may go (the owner accepts "unfair" abilities,
   2026-11-12): an air budget per take-off, melee only on the ground or as a telegraphed `dive` (a basic attack may
   land from the air or a cling on a player who left no standable cell beside him), air poise (shot down by arrows,
   bolts, tridents, wind charges), travel bonuses off in fights, and while fighting at most 4 s at a time where no
   engaged player can reach it before it comes back. Heroes may cross any base's walls and reach players anywhere, and
   an escaping hero may go where no one can follow (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` sections 3.7, 5 and 7.7).

## 7. Non-combat skills

### 7.1 Duty and court

Every hero has a `duty` (v1 §8: guard, scout or any worker job). It does that job with today's code, with player-like
actions (blocks take ticks to break, real furnaces, vanilla loot) but better than a worker (work mastery: a proficiency
and a specialty from its primary talent, `MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` part 2), counts as that worker for its base's needs,
and is never retrained. A leader's duty becomes `court` while it leads
(section 5.6). Every hero, whatever its duty, answers alarms and fights.

**A leader's day** (court duty; PROPOSED): dawn, a short stop at the station (milestone 2: the council runs here);
daytime, its first work talent's workplace if it has one, otherwise a walk between the station, the gates and the
heart, greeting member players; dusk, the heart; night, a bed in the largest house (`rest.sleep`, eyes `sleeping`),
woken by any alarm.

## 8. Progression

- **Levels 1-30** on the NPC curve (`AffixRoller`: 40 + 30n per level), v1 combat XP (v1 §22.1); a leader also earns
  XP from faction events (a base built +40, a raid won +100, a pact sworn +40, an advancement +20, a festival +20).
  Each level rolls an affix from its duty job, as for any NPC.
- **Growth** profiles of v1 §5.3; `controller` and `summoner` default to `mystic`.
- **All abilities from level 1, power growing with level** (the owner's answer of 2026-11-12: "they should have all
  abilities but not full power"). Nothing in a format-2 hero is gated by level: every move and passive, every authored
  phase and both ultimates are there at level 1, and so is every mobility feature up to its ceiling
  (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 1.1, never below what the feature needs to work). Power grows: stats and ability power by
  the growth profile (v1 §5.3), talent numbers by tier (II at level 10, III at 20; 7.2), work proficiency, and
  mobility numbers (60% at level 1 to 100% at level 20). The first draft's gates, changed: moves and passives by
  `unlock_level` (now read and ignored with a NOTE, 12.7), a common or rare hero's third phase at level 15 (now there
  from level 1 when authored, 6.7), and an **awakened ultimate** (`ultimate.awakened`) replacing the first at level 25
  (now a second ultimate beside the first from level 1, 12.8). Format-1 files keep v1 §22.3.
- **Heroes per faction** (milestone 3): `heroes.maxPerFaction` 6 (the leader plus five); non-leaders take posts the
  leader assigns (general, steward, envoy, warden) or their own duty.
- **Recruiting more heroes** (milestone 3): v1 tokens; wandering heroes with a personality-driven join condition
  (duel, payment, reputation, gifts, visits, cause, rescue); heroes of fallen factions; defection when loyalty falls.
  Compatibility with the leader:
  `comp = 1 - (0.4|h - h_L| + 0.3|a - a_L| + 0.2|s - s_L| + 0.1|m - m_L|) + temperament affinity (±0.2) +
  relationship (friend, sibling, mentor, student, admirer +0.3; rival -0.2; sworn enemy -1)`; joins when comp >= 0.45
  (0.3 for paid mercenaries); loyalty `L = 0.6 l + 0.4 comp + bond / 100`.
- **Bonds** (milestone 3): heroes who fight together gain bond points; at 20 a pair gets +5% damage near each other and
  bond dialogue; `relationships` in the file start or block bonds.

## 10. Networking

### 10.1 Synced entity data (vanilla `SynchedEntityData`, sent to tracking players)

| Field | Type | Layout |
|---|---|---|
| `DATA_HERO` | String | hero id; set once at spawn |
| `DATA_ACTION` | Integer | bits 0-7 action code (appendix D); 8-11 sequence (+1 per new action, so a repeat restarts); 12-17 speed (`0.5 + n x 1.5 / 63`); 18-23 variant (combo step or a random variant); 24-31 reserved |
| `DATA_STATE` | Byte | bits 0-1 phase - 1; 2 enraged; 3 super armour; 4 weapon drawn; 5 talking; 6 in a wind-up or channel (no blinking); 7 yielding |
| `DATA_MOOD` | Byte | bits 0-3 mood (12 values); 4-5 intensity; 6 blush; 7 eyes glow |
| `DATA_LOCO` | Byte | locomotion mode (fly, glide, climb, hover...), one-shot and sequence bits, tired: `MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 4.2 |

Faction, role and variant stay in the existing `FactionNpc` fields.

### 10.2 Payloads

- `FactionIdentityPayload` (play, to client): slot ordinal, name, short name, slug, three colours, banner summary; sent
  at login and on change; read by `ClientFactions` for name tags, the map, the info screen and the tint layers.
- `InfoPayload` (existing) gains the hero sheet text and the station's leader and heroes section.
- `MapPayload` markers carry an RGB colour (0 = colour by kind).
- No art payloads: models, textures and animations are in the jar, so a client with the same jar has them.
- `FCNetwork` raises the registrar version from `"1"` to `"2"`, so a client with an older jar gets a clear version
  message instead of missing data.

### 10.3 Vanilla packets used

Boss events (bars), title packets (intro), display entities (decals), particles and sounds (`ServerLevel.sendParticles`
with fixed counts, `level.playSound`), entity data (actions, moods). GeckoLib registers its own payloads; heroes do not
use them (no `triggerAnim`).

### 10.4 Bandwidth

An action change is one entity-data packet of about 12 bytes; a busy boss fight sends 2-4 actions and 1-2 mood changes
a second, under 100 bytes/s per hero per watching player. A telegraph decal is one entity spawn plus its data (about
100 bytes), at most 12 per hero at once.

## 12. Hero data format v2

### 12.1 Files and how they are read

| File | Written by | Read by | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `herosrc/heroes/<id>.hero.json` | the author (Claude), scaffolded by `herogen new` | `herogen` | everything: the server fields below plus `art` and `animations` |
| `src/main/resources/data/factionclash/heroes/<id>.json` | `herogen build` | the server (`HeroRegistry`) | the source minus `art` and animation recipes, plus a `generated` block: each move's `frames` and `anim` name from the markers, the move list in action-code order, a content hash |
| `assets/factionclash/geo/hero/<id>.geo.json`, `animations/hero/<id>.animation.json`, `textures/entity/hero/<id>*.png` | `herogen build` | GeckoLib on the client | model, animations, texture, glow mask, phase texture |
| `assets/factionclash/heroes/<id>.visual.json` | `herogen build` | `HeroModel`, `HeroFace` | style library, blink profile, mood overrides, move list (index to animation name), phase looks, tint bones, voice |
| `config/factionclash/heroes/<id>.json` (optional) | an operator | `HeroRegistry` | an override: its top-level fields replace the built-in hero's (never `id`, `art`, `animations`) |

The server reads the built-in definitions with a `SimpleJsonResourceReloadListener` on `heroes` (registered in
`AddReloadListenerEvent`), so `/reload` re-reads them and a datapack could override them, then applies the config
overrides, then validates everything with the v1 pipeline extended for format 2 (section 14.1). Commands are v1 §27
without the skin parts: `/fc hero list|info|check|reload`, `/fc admin hero give|token|revive|release|test`, plus
`/fc admin hero lineup|preview|pose` for screenshots (section 13.8) and the operator test hooks `/fc admin hero
health|poise|yield|target-state` (set a health share, fill poise, resolve a yield as spare or finish, force a
reaction condition such as blocking or eating) used by screenshot sessions and manual checks. A v1 file (`"format": 1`) still loads: `skills`
read as moves, `empowered` as one state, `base_abilities` as talents; it gets the `_fallback` model.

### 12.2 Conventions

v1 §3 applies (snake_case, seconds, blocks, health points, whole percent, `_` keys are comments, forgiving loading with
strict reporting). Added: colours are `"#RRGGBB"`; part ids are `<category>/<name>`; animation names use
`[a-z0-9_.]`; recipe times are seconds from the start of the animation.

### 12.7 `moves`

A move is a v1 ability (v1 §10: `name`, `form` and form fields, `effects`, `look`, `line`, `use_when`, `cooldown`,
`charges`, `energy_gain`; a v1 `unlock_level`, here or on the ultimate or a passive, is read and ignored with a NOTE,
because everything is there from level 1: section 8) plus:

| Field | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | id 2-24 | required | unique in the file; animation `move.<id>` |
| `class` | `light`, `heavy`, `area`, `ultimate` | from the form | sets the minimum wind-up and recovery (6.4) |
| `frames` | `{ windup, active, recovery }` seconds | generated from the animation markers | authored only with `animations.source: manual`; clamped to the class minimums |
| `band` | list of `close`, `mid`, `far` | from form and range | where the selector likes it (6.3) |
| `weight` | 0-10 | 5 | selector weight; 0 = only as a followup or reaction answer (a NOTE if nothing leads to it) |
| `phases` | list of 1-3 | all | the phases it belongs to (also settable from `boss.phases[].moves`) |
| `prefer_when` | condition | none | doubles the score when true |
| `followups` | list of `{ move, chance }` | none | strings (6.3) |
| `repeatable`, `breaks_guard`, `unblockable`, `super_armor` | booleans | false | 6.4, 6.9 |
| `channel` | `{ interrupt }` % 5-30 | none | breakable by that share of max health in damage (weak point `channel`) |
| `telegraph` | `{ shape, color }` | from form | decal override (`disc`, `strip`, `fan`, `ring`, `landing`, `impacts`, `none` for light moves) |
| `per_shot` | list of effect lists | none | `barrage` only: each shot cycles to the next list |

### 12.8 `ultimate`

v1 ultimate (energy 100, `charge_rate`, minimum gap 20-60 s) plus move fields; class `ultimate`. `awakened`: a second
ultimate with the same shape, there from level 1 beside the first (the first draft unlocked it at level 25 to replace
the first; changed with the owner's answer of 2026-11-12, section 8). The two share the energy bar and the minimum
gap, and the selector picks between them by weight, band and `prefer_when`, as it does moves.

### 12.9 `states`

0-3 named states generalising v1's empowered state (v1 §18): a stance, a transformation, a rage. Fields: `name`,
`description`, `stats` (1-4, ±30%), `on_hit` (1-2 effects), `particle`, `color`, `size` (100-110), `skills_locked`,
`moves` (a replacement move list while in the state), `show`/`hide` (bones), `face` (a mood held while in the state),
`line`. Entered by `empower` with `"state": "<name>"`, by a phase (`boss.phases[].states`) or by a passive; the
condition `self_state` reads it. The animation `state.<name>` plays on entering.

| `damage_vs_players` | whole % 100-140 | 115 | |

| `phases` | 1-3 of `{ at, moves, states, adds, stats, transition }` | 2 or 3 by rarity | `at`: health share where it begins (first 100); `transition`: `{ seconds 0.8-2, line, shockwave {radius, strength}, effects, face }`; an `unlock_level` is read and ignored with a NOTE (every phase from level 1: section 8) |
| `reactions` | 0-8 of `{ when, move, chance, cooldown }` | none | 6.8 |

### 12.13 `talents` and duty

`talents`: 0-3 of `{ id, realm, affix, roles }` (section 7.2; `affix` and `roles` only for `aura`). The duty
(`behaviour.duty`) is the job it works when it is not leading.

### 12.14 `voice`

`{ blip, pitch, rate, hurt_pitch }`: `blip` from a fixed list of note-block instruments (`harp`, `bell`, `chime`,
`flute`, `xylophone`, `bit`, `hat`, `bass`, `guitar`, `pling`), played softly per syllable while a line shows; `pitch`
0.5-2.0 (default 1.25 female, 0.9 male, shifted by temperament); `rate` 2-4 ticks per syllable; `hurt_pitch` for the
vanilla player hurt and death sounds the hero uses.

Generated voices (S1, `SOUND.md` section 6): a hero with a sound set has its own blips, `factionclash:hero.voice.<id>`
(syllable variants made by `tools/sfxgen` from `herosrc/sfx/<id>.sfx.json`, played one per `rate` ticks with the
set's melody and jitter, levelled so a spoken line measures about -21, between a villager and a wandering trader);
`blip` and `pitch` remain the fallback for a hero without one, and `rate` drives both.

### 12.15 `lines`

v1 §23 events and rules, plus:

| Event | When | Placeholders |
|---|---|---|
| `founding` | it founds its faction at campaign start | `{name}`, `{faction}` |
| `intro` | boss intro | `{name}`, `{player}` |
| `phase_2`, `phase_3` | phase transitions | `{name}` |
| `enrage`, `stagger`, `counter` | those moments (rate-limited) | `{name}`, `{enemy}` |
| `yield`, `spared` | the defeat beat; spared | `{name}`, `{player}` |
| `escape` | it escapes: a refused yield, low health away from home, a lost raid, a leader saving itself (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 7.7) | `{name}`, `{enemy}` |
| `raid_announce`, `base_lost`, `hero_fallen` | leader announcements | `{name}`, `{faction}`, `{target_faction}` |
| `work`, `night`, `small_talk` | idle moments at home | `{name}`, `{player}` |
| `pact_offer`, `pact_accepted`, `pact_refused`, `peace_offer`, `declare_war`, `betrayal`, `betrayed`, `stranger_warning`, `welcome`, `gift_liked`, `gift_disliked` | milestone 2 and 3 events (read now, used later) | as they need, plus `{item}` |

v1's `unlock` event never fires for a format-2 hero (nothing unlocks: section 8); format-1 files keep it.

Each temperament has a fallback bank (`herosrc/temperaments/<t>.json`, 2-4 lines per event), so a hero needs only its
own voice for the events that matter: the authoring target is 40-60 lines per hero.

### 12.16 `recruit` and `relationships` (used from milestone 3)

`recruit`: `{ condition, value }` with `duel`, `payment`, `reputation`, `gift`, `visits`, `cause`, `rescue` (default by
temperament). `relationships`: 0-4 of `{ hero, kind }` with `friend`, `rival`, `sibling`, `mentor`, `student`,
`sworn_enemy`, `admirer`; checked across files (a relationship to an unknown hero is a NOTE).

### 12.17 Limits

v1 limits, plus: 12 moves, 3 states, 3 phases, 8 reactions, 4 weak points, 24 parts, 3 talents, 4 relationships, 12
lines per event; source file at most 256 KB, server file at most 128 KB.

## 13. Art pipeline

### 13.1 Principles

1. **Silhouette first, then colour blocks, then detail.** At Minecraft scale the head carries identity: ears, horns,
   hats, hair shape, halos, tails and wings. Every hero has one distinct head-silhouette element (ROSTER_PLAN.md
   "Look" column).
2. **Two or three main colours and one accent**, readable in greyscale (checked: luminance contrast between the
   largest two colour regions at least 15%).
3. **Shape language by temperament**: round shapes read friendly, square sturdy, spikes dangerous; the archetype and
   temperament templates bias part choice (a sweet-natured hero gets rounded hair and soft trims, a tyrant spikes).
4. **Anime look within pixel art**: hue-shifted shading, one-texel darker outlines on clothing and hair edges, a
   bright "ring" highlight band on hair, large expressive eyes at double resolution.
5. **Deterministic and editable**: the same inputs always give byte-identical outputs; every output opens in
   Blockbench; nothing is drawn by hand inside the pipeline, so 200 heroes stay consistent.

### 13.2 Sources (`herosrc/`, repository only)

**UV template** (fixed regions, same for every hero; everything else is packed):

| Region (u, v, w, h) | Contents |
|---|---|
| 0, 0, 64, 128 | base body (packed: head sides and top at 1x, torso, arms, legs; rig v3's longer bodies) |
| 64, 0, 80, 30 | eye frames (4 x 3 of 20x10) |
| 144, 0, 20, 20 | head front at 2x (20x20 texels for the 10x10 face) |
| 64, 30, 64, 3 | mouth frames (8 of 8x3) |
| 64, 33, 64, 2 | cheek frames (4 of 16x2) |
| rest of 256x128 (or 256x256) | packed parts and weapon (first fit on an occupancy grid, largest first, 1-texel gutters) |

**Integer texels.** Many cube sizes are fractional (a 2.5 px arm, a 6.5 px hip). Box UV would put such faces on
half-texel boundaries, which nearest-neighbour sampling turns into uneven pixel widths and bleeding at seams. So every
face gets a **per-face UV rectangle of whole texels**: `round(face size x density)`, at least 1 (density 1, or 2 for the
head front and face planes). A 2.5 px face drawn with 3 texels stretches them by 17%, which is invisible in game and
keeps every pixel crisp. The packer works in whole texels only, and `herogen check` rejects any fractional UV. A part
or cube may set `density` 2 (two texels per pixel); rig v2's hair and ears did, rig v3's do not (the strands are cubes,
so 1 texel per px keeps them flat-painted like the references and cuts the hair's texels by four).

### 13.3 The parts library

A part is a small JSON file: cubes attached to rig bones (or new bones it declares), parametric sizes, colour slots,
paint instructions, and tags. Head parts are authored **head-local** (`"offset": [0, "head_y", 0]`: y 0 is the chin,
the face is at z -5 and the head spans x and z -5..5, y 0..10 on every form) and at double density. Example (abridged
from `hair/high_ponytail_long`):

```jsonc
{
  "id": "hair/high_ponytail_long",
  "category": "hair",
  "params": { "length": { "min": 6, "max": 16, "default": 12 }, "locks": { "min": 2, "max": 10, "default": 6 } },
  "occupies": ["hair_top", "hair_back"], "conflicts": ["hair/twin_tails*"],
  "offset": [0, "head_y", 0], "density": 2,
  "bones": [ { "name": "tail_hair_1", "parent": "head", "pivot": [0, 9.25, 5.75] },
             { "name": "tail_hair_2", "parent": "tail_hair_1", "pivot": [0, 3.25, 6.5] } ],
  "cubes": [
    { "bone": "hair_top", "origin": [-5.5, 4, -5.5], "size": [11, 6.75, 11], "faces": ["up", "south", "east", "west"] },
    { "bone": "hair_top", "origin": [-5, 10.75, -5], "size": [10, 0.75, 10] },
    { "bone": "bangs", "origin": [-5.5, 8.5, -6], "size": [11, 2.75, 1] },
    { "bone": "bangs", "origin": [-5.25, 6.5, -6.5], "size": [2.5, 4.5, 0.75], "taper": 0.3 },
    { "bone": "hair_r", "origin": [-6.6, "max(-locks, 3 - head_y)", -5.4],
      "size": [1.75, "10 - max(-locks, 3 - head_y)", 1.75], "taper": 0.5, "cutout": "tip_jagged_1", "mirror": true },
    { "bone": "tail_hair_1", "origin": [-1.5, 8.25, 5.5], "size": [3, 2, 2], "slot": "trim", "material": "cloth" },
    { "bone": "tail_hair_2", "origin": [-1.75, "max(3.25 - length * 0.6, 3.5 - head_y)", 6.25],
      "size": [3.5, "3.5 - max(3.25 - length * 0.6, 3.5 - head_y)", 2.75], "taper": 0.75 }

  "secondary": { "chain": ["tail_hair_1", "tail_hair_2", "fine_tail_hair_tip"], "stiffness": 0.35, "damping": 0.6 }

```

- **Parameters** are numbers or small expressions over the part's params (`"4 + length * 0.45"`), evaluated by the
  generator with a whitelist parser (no `eval`).
- **Tags**: `occupies` (slots such as `head_top`, `hair_back`, `ears`, `back`, `waist`, `hand_r`) and `conflicts`
  stop impossible combinations (a full helmet with cat ears needs the `ears_out` helmet variant); `fine_` cube names
  give level of detail; `phase` turns a part into a `p2_`/`p3_` part; `mirror: true` builds the left side from the
  right.
- **Cutouts** are alpha masks applied when painting (jagged bangs, fur tips, lace, feather edges, torn capes); GeckoLib
  renders `entityCutoutNoCull`, so cut-outs show from both sides.
- **Secondary** declares a chain for the follow-through bake (13.6).
- **Rig v2 additions** (all in `parts.py`'s docstring): part `offset` and `density`; cube `density`, `shade`
  (`vertical`: lit top and darker tip, the default for hair; `flat`; `mass`: no vertical shading or hem outline, top
  lit like the sides, for the stacked slabs of one solid such as an ear), `skip_if_empty` (an optional piece a param can
  switch off, such as an ahoge); paint op `vee` `[y_tip, slope]` (a V neckline); cutouts `tri_down`, `peak` (an animal
  ear: full width at the base, a point on top) and `round_top`. A cutout without a taper is cut through the cube's broad
  faces and the narrow faces and caps follow it, so an ear has no full-height strip on its sides. A `jagged_N` cut never
  cuts the outermost column on either side, so a jagged slab's narrow faces stay whole and its bottom face cannot show
  through them as a striped "barcode".
- **The hair kit** (style guide in `notes/artgen.md`; art direction pass 2: the crown is **parted**, 5 strands on each
  side of a centre line lying over the dome, replacing the radial whorl that read as a pinwheel; the crown is 0.5 px

  cap open at the front, a stepped dome, a fringe block over the forehead, 3-5 strand cubes over it whose tips stop at
  the lash line, side cover, back cover and the style's own pieces (long side locks, layered back strands, ponytails and twin tails on chains, an
  ahoge). Touching hair cubes of one colour are painted as one mass (no outline between them).
- **Style pass 2 additions** (rig v3, `gen.py`, `notes/artgen.md`): **generators** next to plain cubes: `strands` (rows
  of rotated boxes along a line or an arc, each hanging from an anchor or standing on it, with `flare` outward, `fan`,
  `zigzag` and `lean` sideways, `layers` of depth, cycled `length`, `width`, `tone`, a deterministic `jitter`,

### 13.4 Palette and procedural painting (`paint.py`, `palette.py`)

- **Ramps**: each palette slot becomes a 5-tone ramp in OKLCH: tones -2..+2 change lightness by 0.08 per step; shadows
  shift hue toward blue-violet by up to 12 degrees and lose chroma, highlights shift toward yellow by up to 8 degrees
  (the hue-shifting of pixel-art shading). Skin ramps shift shadows toward red instead. **Hair ramps** (art direction
  pass 2, the `hair` and `hair_accent` slots) step 0.085 down and 0.095 up. Their shadows keep their chroma and lean
  orange-red for warm hair, or blue-violet for cool hair, so gold reads as gold-to-orange as in the references. Dark
  hair (L below 0.42) steps 0.12 up toward a cool blue sheen, so a black mass still shows its strands.
- **Materials** decide how a face of a cube is painted: `cloth` (soft vertical gradient, folds every 3-4 texels on long
  faces), `skin` (soft, blush region on the face plane only), `hair` (style pass 2: painted flat, 2-3 tones per face
  and no strand lines, the strands being cubes; art direction pass 2: the top 30% of a strand's side lit, the lowest
  40% one tone darker, the tip row the darkest; earlier: every strand cube lit at the top fifth,
  darker in its lower third, outlined at its tip; strand lines every 4 texels at 2x and short highlight streaks; no
  crown ring), `metal` (high contrast, a specular band, rim light), `leather` (mid contrast, stitch lines), `fur` (noise
  tufts, cut-out tips), `scale` (overlapping arcs), `wood`, `paper` (flat, ink marks), `lacquer` (glossy, sharp
  highlight), `bone`, `gem` and `glow` (bright, written to the glow mask).
- **Light baked by face direction**: top +1 tone, front and back 0, sides 0 and -1 (light from the hero's front-left),
  bottom -2; a one-tone darker band on the lowest third of tall faces; one-tone ambient occlusion where a child cube
  meets its parent (collars, cuffs, belts).
- **Outlines**: a one-texel band two tones darker on the silhouette-relevant edges of clothing and hair (bottom and side
  edges); never across skin interiors.
- **Patterns**: stripes, plaid, checks, dots, waves, hemp-leaf, petals, scales, fur tufts, lace, trim bands, and
  emblem stamps from the archetype (paw, flame, bell, crest, star, moon disc, leaf), each with 1-3 colour
  slots and a scale; applied per cube face with seams aligned across neighbouring faces.
- **Glow mask and phase texture**: `glow` regions write `<id>_glowmask.png`; any phase palette change writes
  `<id>_p2.png` (and `_p2_glowmask.png`).
- **Checks**: adjacent colour regions differ by delta-E 10 or more; the greyscale contrast rule of 13.1; no pure black
  or pure white outside eyes and highlights.

### 13.6 Animations (`anim/`)

- **As built (G3, 2026-11-12; details in `notes/artgen.md`, "G3")**: modules `anim/clip.py` (GeckoLib's keyframe
  semantics and easings), `skeleton.py` (forward kinematics), `poses.py`, `primitives.py`, `recipes.py`,
  `secondary.py`, `writer.py` (animation and timing files), `quality.py`, `library.py`, `timing.py`, `preview.py`;
  sources `herosrc/anim/{poses,core,styles}/*.json` and `herosrc/anim/secondary.json`. Generated: `_core` (`move.idle`
  24 s, `move.walk` 1.0 s, `move.run` 0.6 s) and `_style_katana` (`combat.idle`, `attack.1` with its cut at clip time
  0.40 s; its `hit` is written at 0.50 s, tick 10 from the action's start, after the action controller's 2-tick blend:
  4.8). One file serves every form: bone names are shared, and pixel offsets that depend on leg length (the root
  height of a gait, the leg tilt under a weight shift, a knee bend) are solved over the three forms as the compromise
  with the smallest worst error (within 0.5 px on all six bases). The sign conventions (a forward limb swing is
  negative rx, elbow flexion negative rx, knee flexion positive rx, +ry turns right, +rz raises `arm_r` sideways) are
  in `anim/poses.py`; the rig's joint limits carry the flex sign.
- **Pose library** (`herosrc/anim/poses/*.json`): named poses as bone rotations (degrees) and an optional root offset;
  poses extend others (`katana.windup` extends `katana.stance`), `mirror` builds the other side, `knees` bends the knees
  with planted feet, and the fixes `level_feet` and `ground` put the soles flat on the ground. Shared poses are
  authored once and checked against joint limits.
- **Primitives** (Python functions with parameters): `walk_cycle`, `run_cycle`, `idle_breathe`, `swing`, `thrust`,
  `slam`, `spin`, `leap`, `land`, `cast`, `draw_bow`, `fire_crossbow`, `throw`, `guard`, `dodge`, `hurt`, `stagger`,
  `kneel`, `death`, `greet_bow`, `wave`, `cheer`, `taunt`, `play_instrument`, `work_loop`. G3 built `walk_cycle`,
  `run_cycle`, `breathe`, `weight_shift`, `look` and `ear_twitch`; G4 built `crouch_cycle`, `carry_cycle`,
  `oscillate` (waves, nods, shakes, pumps, stirring, a dizzy circle) and `flinch` (a damped impulse), and authored the
  keyed motions (dodge, hurt, stagger, kneel, death, greet_bow, wave, cheer, the work strokes) as pose keys with those
  layers on top; the attack primitives come with G5 (G3's swing is pose keys).
- **Recipe**: `{ length, loop, keys: [ { t, pose | primitive + params, ease, root, face, marker, sound, particle,
  overshoot, settle } ] }` (example in 12.18). `ease` uses GeckoLib's easing names; `overshoot` adds a key past the pose
  with `easeOutBack`; `settle` adds a small return. `face` writes `face_ctrl` keys with `step` easing. As built (G3):
  primitives are `layers` (`[{primitive, params, add}]`, added to the pose keys or replacing the channels they key)
  rather than a field of a key; `bones` limits what a recipe keys (an attack keys the upper body, so the legs keep
  walking under it); `overshoot` k is an explicit key k x the last change past the pose and a return 0.1 s later;
  face changes are pre/post jumps, never `step` (4.6); `class`, `settle_to`, `kind`, `planted`, `ground`.
- **Secondary motion bake**: chains declared by parts (hair, ponytails, tails, ears, capes, skirts, sleeves) follow
  their parents through a damped spring simulated at 20 Hz; the result is keyed every 2 ticks and simplified (keys
  within 0.5 degrees dropped). At runtime, the `secondary` controller and the procedural lag add movement-driven sway.
  As built (G3): the shared libraries bake the rig's chain bones (`tail_1..5`, `tail_hair_1..3`, `ear_*`,
  `hair_back/l/r`, `sleeve_*`, `skirt_f/b/r/l`, `cape_1/2`) with the group settings of `herosrc/anim/secondary.json`;
  the spring answers the parent's motion only (a bone's own authored turn, an ear twitch, is kept sharp) and `hang`
  pulls sleeves, capes, ponytails, skirts and hair toward their bind direction like gravity; the chains a hero's parts
  declare and their stiffness and damping go to `visual.json` `secondary` for the procedural lag (4.4). G3 review: an
  action is baked over its settle clip as authored (that clip's own follow-through is not counted twice), bakes only
  the chain bones below the bones it keys (a swing leaves the skirt panels and the tail to the walk under it), and a
  bell sleeve's `hang` fades out (`hang_fade`) while the forearm is raised well above horizontal, with its lag behind
  the hang capped (`lag`), so its open end does not face the viewer as a disc.
- **Markers**: `hit`, `vfx:<id>`, `sfx:<sound>` become GeckoLib custom-instruction keyframes in `timeline`, sound
  keyframes in `sound_effects`, particle keyframes in `particle_effects`. Several instructions at one time are joined
  with `;`.
- **Output**: GeckoLib animation JSON (`format_version` 1.8.0) with per-bone `rotation`, `position` and `scale`
  channels, keyframes written the way Blockbench's GeckoLib export writes them (vector plus easing); the exact keys are
  checked against GeckoLib's keyframe parser when the writer is built. Checked (G3): GeckoLib reads a channel's keys in
  file order, so keyframe maps are written in ascending time order (not sorted as strings); a test ports its parser
  and reads every written channel back.
- **Quality rules** (`herogen check`): the anticipation before every `hit` meets the class minimum; joint limits
  (elbow and knee flex one way, 0-150 degrees; head yaw ±80, pitch ±60; waist twist ±45); feet stay planted (forward
  kinematics, drift at most 0.5 px) unless a key marks a step, leap or dodge; loops close (first key = last key); no
  animation shorter than its last marker; no root motion (movement is physical on the server); every one-shot action
  animation ends on the first key of its style's `combat.idle` (work and emotes: `move.idle`) for each bone it keys,
  because GeckoLib snaps rather than blends when the action controller stops (4.8); `face_ctrl` keys are whole numbers.
  As built (G3), on all six bases: planted clips keep every sole corner within 0.5 px, grounded clips keep the lowest
  sole within 0.6 px of the ground, gaits never sink a sole more than 0.6 px; root x and z within 1.5 px; neck yaw ±60
  and pitch ±40 added; face_ctrl changes are jumps; the timing file agrees with the markers. G3 review: an action
  that starts on its settle clip's pose starts there on its baked chain bones too; markers come before the last tick;
  timing files state the controller blend.

### 13.8 Preview renderer and screenshots

- **Software renderer** (`render/raster.py`, NumPy): reads the `.geo.json`, evaluates an animation at time t with
  GeckoLib's semantics (keyframe interpolation and easings, bone rotation order Z then Y then X, the pivot and
  rotation sign conversions GeckoLib applies when baking), builds cube quads with their UVs, rasterises them with a
  z-buffer and nearest-neighbour texture sampling, alpha cut-out at 0.1 like `entityCutoutNoCull`, and shades faces
  with vanilla's entity light directions so the result looks like the game. Output is pixel-crisp at 4x.
- **As built (G2, 2026-11-12; `notes/artgen.md`)**: the light is vanilla's world-space entity light (front and back
  0.74, sides 0.50, top 1.0, bottom 0.4 for a hero facing south or north; it does not follow the camera), normals go
  through GeckoLib's flat-cube fix, the glow layer is lit but ignores the light map, animations are read from the
  written files and played through an emulation of the base and action controllers (the action's 2-tick blend,
  events on the frame they fire), and the face comes from `visual.json` through a stand-in for `HeroFace` (moods,
  `face_ctrl`, seeded blinks). The camera is orthographic for previews and perspective for the fidelity check. Found
  by it: the zero-thickness face planes are lit apart from the head when the face points down or to one side
  (GeckoLib's flat-cube fix); recommended: A2 skips the fix for those bones. The G2 review added the coplanar-face
  check (`raster.coplanar_ties`, `preview --ties`, `previews/render/coplanar_faces.png`): the renderer settles a depth
  tie by draw order, the game z-fights, so two coplanar faces of different colour are drawn a second time in reverse
  order and every pixel that changes is reported (the sash layers of three style fixtures fight their coat or skirt;
  fixed in G6b, where it became a `check` rule); and `render-previews --check` (two committed GIFs had gone stale).
- **Fidelity check** (task G2, with A2): the test rig in three poses is rendered by the preview and screenshotted in
  game; the silhouettes must match within one pixel at 4x. This guards the sign and order conventions.
- **Contact sheet**: 24 heroes per sheet (6 x 4 cells); each cell has the 3/4 front view, a small back view, the phase
  look, the face strip (open, blink, happy, angry, focused, pain, talk), a palette swatch, and the name, archetype,
  role, element and weapon.
- **In game**: `/fc admin hero lineup [from] [count] [spacing]` places heroes with AI off in a row facing the caller;
  `/fc admin hero preview <id> <animation> [loop]` plays one animation; `/fc admin hero pose <id> mood <m> | phase <n>`
  sets the face or phase look. Screenshots come from the Xvfb `clientViewer` flow of HANDOFF section 4 (spectator
  camera placed with `tp`, `import -window root`).
- **Blockbench round trip**: geometry and animation files open with the GeckoLib plugin. A hand-polished animation is
  adopted with `import-anim`, after which the generator keeps that file and reads its frames from its markers; a
  hand-edited model or texture is a new part or a per-hero override part, never an edit of generated output.

A1a as built (2026-11-12), where it goes beyond or differs from the rows above:
- **A bind-pose renderer already.** A client needs a renderer for every entity type, so A1a registers a minimal
  `client/hero/HeroRenderer` (GeckoLib's `GeoEntityRenderer` at the visual's `render_scale`) and `HeroModel` (geometry,
  texture and the animation chain with its fallbacks from the registry; `_fallback` for fixtures and unknown heroes).
  `HeroEntity.registerControllers` is empty, so the hero stands in its bind pose; A2 adds the controllers, the face,
  the layers, bone visibility (the phase-2 halo still shows), the sash tint and LOD to these two classes.
- **`HeroActions` came with A1a** (listed under F3): the bit layouts of `DATA_ACTION`, `DATA_STATE`, `DATA_MOOD` and
  `DATA_LOCO` and the appendix D code table, with `HeroActionsTest`; F3 adds nothing to it unless the table grows.
- **Registry.** `HeroRegistry` reads `assets/factionclash/heroes/*.visual.json` from the mod file itself (a dedicated
  server loads no client assets, yet sizes heroes), lazily and again at every server start; a config override
  (`config/factionclash/heroes/<id>.json`) may set `name` and `title` in this build (other fields are reported as not
  read yet; `id`, `art` and `animations` never). A hero has the id's name ("Test Rig") until A1b reads definitions.
- **Size.** Hitbox as 3.6 with the PROPOSED 1.2-block floor (the eyes stay on the model) and the 1.98 cap (eyes in
  proportion), width 0.6 x scale; `getDefaultDimensions` divides by `generic.scale` so vanilla's own scaling lands on
  the capped size.
- **Kit.** Until kits exist, the kit is the role's basic weapon (stone sword or axe, bow and arrow, crossbow), bound
  with a custom-data marker: never dropped, never deposited, gone when storehouse gear replaces it, given again on return.
- **Life cycle.** `give` releases the hero from any faction first and spawns a new entity (new UUID) at the operator,
  a given position, or the base's spawn point; revival also makes a new entity and replaces the roster entry, so any
  stale copy of an older entity is discarded when it loads (`HeroState` keeps the last UUID after a release). A new
  campaign frees every hero. `heroes.reviveSeconds` (600) is the first key of the `[heroes]` config section.
- **Director.** A hero's roster entry carries `heroId`; it counts in every soldier count (needs, garrison, war council
  strength) but the raid party is picked from the non-heroes only, and `retrainIdleWorker` skips it.

A2 as built (2026-11-12), where it goes beyond or differs from 4.4-4.9 and the row above:
- **Controllers in common code.** `HeroEntity.registerControllers` hands over to `hero/HeroAnimator`, which builds
  `base` (4 ticks), `action` (2) and `secondary` (6) with the blends of the timing files (`HeroClips.BASE_TRANSITION`
  etc.; `HeroTimingsTest` checks every shipped timing file against them). It touches no client class: what a hero's
  chain holds is asked of a `Library` the client model installs. Each controller picks the first clip of a candidate
  list its chain has (`logic/hero/HeroClips`), so clips the libraries lack fall back (no swim clip: walk; no
  `work.mine`: `attack.1`). `DATA_LOCO` modes come first in the base list (`move.fly`, `move.glide`, `move.climb` ...)
  and fall back to the ground rules, so A10 only has to set the byte.
- **Walk and run speed (PROPOSED, by eye).** An NPC walks at a player's 4.3 blocks/s, more than G3's stroll covers, so
  `move.walk` plays at ground speed / 0.11 blocks per tick (about 2x for a walking NPC) and `move.run` (from 0.245
  blocks/tick, a sprint) at ground speed / 0.20, both clamped to 0.6-2.2 in steps of 0.1 and changed only by 0.3 or
  more (GeckoLib rescales the whole clip clock, which jumps the phase). The feet still slide a little at a walk.
- **Actions.** A new `DATA_ACTION` sequence restarts the action controller (`forceAnimationReset`, the packed speed);
  `name` then `name.loop` when the chain has one. The server clears the code to 0 when the clip has played
  (`HeroTimingFiles`: the timing file's total ticks divided by the speed; loops and channels are never cleared), so a
  player who starts tracking later does not replay an old action. While no action plays, a fresh vanilla swing plays
  the held tool's work clip (`work.mine`, `work.chop`, `work.till`, else `attack.1`): basic attacks show the katana
  cut, but the damage still lands at the swing's start until the move runner (A5) times basic attacks. Death plays
  `death` and holds. Sound keyframes play at the hero on the client; `vfx:` markers are only logged until `HeroVfx`.
- **Marker log and the timing check.** `-Dfactionclash.heroMarkers=true` (set for the `clientViewer` run) logs every
  marker with its animation tick and the game ticks since the action started. In the Xvfb viewer (software GL, about 10
  frames a second) `attack.1`'s `hit` fired at animation tick 10.36-10.72 (file: 10.00), 11.1-12.1 game ticks after the
  data arrived at 1x and 6.9 at 2x: GeckoLib starts the clip on the first frame after the blend and fires an event on
  the first frame past its tick, so each adds up to one frame; at 60 frames a second that is about 0.3 tick.
- **The face** (`client/hero/HeroFace` on `FaceRules`, `BlinkSchedule`, `FaceClock`): the 4.6 priority with the
  distance freeze applied right after "dead"; the mood byte's glowing eyes (and a phase look's `eyes_glow`) replace an
  open-type eye with frame 10, its blush adds cheek frame 1 when the mood has none; a blink in progress opens at once
  when the wind-up bit comes on and an overdue blink waits 6 ticks after it; the first mood seen is not a change for
  the 10-tick hold. The mood table is the visual file's resolved `face.moods` with `face.overrides` on top.
- **A first server mood stack** (`logic/hero/MoodRules`): an operator's forced mood, the hurt wince (vanilla's 10-tick
  hurt time), asleep, focused in a wind-up, determined while fighting, tired below 30% health, otherwise calm. Phase,
  reaction, situation and temperament moods slot in with A6 and L3/L4. The weapon-drawn flag is set while the hero
  has a target and for 5 s (100 ticks) after its last blow.
- **Renderer.** The UV shift is applied in `createVerticesOfQuad` while the `eyes`, `mouth` and `cheeks` cubes are
  drawn, divided by the baked model's texture size; tint bones are the visual file's `tint` list plus any
  `fine_sash*` bone, drawn with the faction's `rgb` as the vertex colour (a hero with no faction does not exist yet;
  previews take one). The glow layer is GeckoLib's `AutoGlowingGeoLayer` through the same path, skipped when the
  current texture has no `_glowmask` file and, for a mask that glows only in the glowing-eye frame, whenever that frame
  is not shown. The item layer draws vanilla items at `item_r`/`item_l` at vanilla size (scaled back by 1 /
  `render_scale`) with vanilla's third-person transforms; a hero whose model has its own weapon never shows a held
  weapon or its kit item and shows no item while the weapon is drawn; a fists-style hero or the neutral model shows
  what it holds (on a chibi a vanilla sword looks large; an open item for the art review).
- **Procedural touches, and a GeckoLib trap.** Head look (pitch +-60, yaw +-80), a 7-degree hit flinch on `chest`
  (none under super armour), ears back 25 and out 15 degrees while hurt or angry, and the sway of the visual file's
  parent-to-child chains against the body's turn (`Sway`: the G3 spring numbers; hanging chains swing on z, others turn
  on y; loose groups such as the two ears are left to the bake). GeckoLib's bone setters mark a bone as changed and its
  next pass then leaves that bone where it is, so offsets added after the pass would add up frame after frame on a bone
  no animation keys (found in the viewer: heads that slowly turned round); `setCustomAnimations` therefore clears the
  markers after its offsets. Cape lift from velocity is left to the baked follow-through.
- **Visibility and level of detail** (`logic/hero/HeroLod`): `fine_` bones within 20 blocks, the face within 16 (the
  cheeks plane hides with it), the secondary controller and the sway within 32, locator tracking within 24, each with
  a one-block band so a hero at a boundary does not flicker. Phase bones follow 4.3 (`p1_` only in phase 1), the
  visual file's phase looks show and hide their lists in phase order, the weapon bones follow the weapon flag, and the
  naming rules also cover bones a visual file forgot to list. Bone visibility is set on every frame.
- **Fallback chain.** Geometry missing: the `_fallback` model, texture and chain. Animation files missing from the
  chain are dropped (each logged once) so GeckoLib never looks a name up in a missing file; the `_p2` texture is used
  only when the file exists.
- **Operator screenshot tools** (`/fc admin hero ...`): `preview <id> [faction] [pos] [yaw]` stands a display copy
  (no AI, cannot be hurt or targeted, no hunger, job or roster entry, never counted by the director or the alarms, kept
  across restarts, named "(preview)"); `lineup [first] [count] [spacing] [faction]` stands a row of them 4 blocks in
  front of the source, facing it (from RCON: `execute as <player> at <player> run fc admin hero lineup 0 7 1.5`);
  `preview clear [id]` / `lineup clear`; `pose <id|all> mood <mood|auto> | action <name> [speed] | stop | base
  <idle|combat|walk|run|swim|fall|crouch|sit|sleep|climb|fly|glide|auto> | weapon <drawn|sheathed|auto> | phase <1-4> |
  talk|windup|blush|glow <on|off> | clear` on every loaded entity of that hero. A forced base pose needs one more
  synced byte, `DATA_POSE`, beside the five of 10.1 (zero except on a hero posed by hand).
- **The six style fixtures without shipping them.** A server reads `config/factionclash/hero_fixtures/<id>.visual.json`
  at every start as preview fixtures (they can be lined up and posed, never given to a faction, so never drafted); a
  client registers the visual files a resource pack adds for ids the mod does not ship (`ClientHeroVisuals`), so the
  pack's geometry, textures and face tables draw them. For the screenshots the fixtures were built with the art
  pipeline's own `build.build_files` / `build.write_files` into a temporary folder (nothing in `tools/herogen` changed),
  their visual files copied to the server's `config/factionclash/hero_fixtures/` and the folder packed as a resource
  pack in the viewer's `run/resourcepacks/`. Maren names `_style_sword_shield`, which does not exist yet: her chain

## 16. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The GeckoLib host is blocked again | no animated models | it answered on 2026-11-12 (4.2); otherwise the owner approves the host once (A0); the jar is then vendored and builds never need it again; the hand-written fallback (4.1) is the plan B |
| Art quality across 200 generated heroes (the biggest real risk) | samey or ugly heroes | a large parts library, archetype templates, hue-shifted painting, HD faces, per-hero signature animations, contact-sheet review per batch, in-game screenshots, the owner's review of the slice before scaling up, Blockbench-editable outputs for later hand polish |
| A hero reads as an existing character | originality broken | uniqueness rules in the roster checker and the validator, name and concept searches, a reviewer pass and the owner's review (ROSTER_PLAN.md section 8) |
| Client cost with many heroes on screen | frame drops in big wars | cube budget, LOD, culling, measurements after the slice and batches 4 and 8; most scenes have a few heroes |
| Client memory for 200 baked models | larger heap | measured; lazy baking if over 100 MB |
| Animation and server timings drift | unfair hits | one recipe writes both; `HeroAssetsTest` fails on a difference |
| Boss mode feels unfair or too easy | bad fights | class minimums, fairness rules enforced in code, PROPOSED numbers tuned from the slice campaign and the owner's play session |
| Personality makes campaigns end early or stall | bad campaigns | draft war-balance rule, `personalityStrength`, campaign checks comparing personalities |
| Refactoring `FactionType` reads breaks something | regressions | M1 is mechanical and keeps every test unchanged; slots and identity are separate steps |
| A future Minecraft upgrade | GeckoLib 5 rewrite | assets are Bedrock files and stay; only the renderer and model glue (about 600-900 lines) change |
| Scope (200 heroes with complex kits) | very long project | the slice proves every piece first; batches are independent and can ship one by one |

## 17. Open questions for the owner

1. **The look.** The heroes will be Minecraft-style models made of cubes with pixel-art textures: anime proportions,
   detailed hair, ears, tails, horns, wings and outfits, and large expressive faces that blink, talk and change
   expression. They will not be smooth high-detail anime models (Minecraft cannot draw those well, and they would
   look out of place). Is this the look you want? The slice screenshots will show it before anything is scaled up.
2. **Your own heroes.** Earlier you planned to write your own heroes with a chatbot and keep them in a private
   repository. With original heroes made by Claude, the design drops the "your own hero JSON plus a 64x64 skin" path
   (operators can still override a built-in hero's kit in the config folder). Do you still want your own heroes
   installable in some form, for example on the old player-model look without animations?
3. **Review cadence.** After you approve the 8-hero slice from screenshots, should each batch of 24 wait for your
   review of its contact sheet and screenshots, or should Claude continue through the batches and send you the
   galleries as they are done?
4. **Network permission.** Building this needs GeckoLib from `dl.cloudsmith.io`. That host was refused on 2026-11-11
   and answered on 2026-11-12, so you may see no prompt at all; if it is refused again when the work starts, you will
   see a prompt to allow it once. After that the library is stored in the repository and the host is no longer needed.
   (An action for you, not a design choice.)
5. **Mobility and work mastery.** *Answered on 2026-11-12* (`MOBILITY_AND_WORK.md` 19.1): heroes may use their
   abilities to make things "unfair" (they cross hostile walls and reach players anywhere), the 27 flyers stay, every
   hero has all of its abilities from level 1 but not at full power (applied to moves, phases, ultimates and talents
   too: section 8), and an escaping hero may make pursuit impossible. The size of the extra output was not answered:
   those numbers stay and are tuned after you play.
