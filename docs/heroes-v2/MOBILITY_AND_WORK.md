# Heroes v2: mobility and work mastery

Superhuman movement for heroes (jumps, climbing, gliding, flight, swimming) and heroes who work better than ordinary
NPCs outside battle.

Status: design, 2026-11-12. The pure rules (F5 of 18.1) are built (2026-11-13: `HEROES_V2.md` 15.2 "F5 as built");
the game does not read them yet (A10-A12, L5m). Companion of `HEROES_V2.md` (the design: "§n" below means a
section there), `SLICE_PLAN.md` (milestone 1) and `ROSTER_PLAN.md` (the 200 heroes, generated by `roster/`). Numbers
marked PROPOSED go to BALANCE.md when they are built. Where this file and `HEROES_V2.md` differ on mobility or work
mastery, this file wins; `HEROES_V2.md` points here from §0, §1, §3.5, §6.5, §6.12, §6.13, §6.14, §7.1, §8, §10.1,
§12.3, §12.15, §15 and §17.

**Owner answers (2026-11-12).** The owner answered the questions of section 19: heroes may use their abilities to make
things "unfair" (they cross hostile walls and reach players anywhere: 3.7, 5.1, 5.2), the 27 flyers stay, every hero
has all of its abilities from level 1 but not at full power (1.1, 15.9), and an escaping hero may make pursuit
impossible (7.7). The extra output sizes (question 4) were confirmed later the same day: those numbers stay and are
tuned after the owner plays. Section 19 has the answers verbatim and what each one changed.

**Owner decisions (2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13) applied here.** Heroes treat players and NPCs alike ("I would like
player only features interactions to be removed, regardless if its good or bad for the player"): there is one combat
mode, so every rule that read "boss mode" or "engaged player" now reads "while fighting" and "engaged enemy", and the
field-mode values survive only for raid travel outside a fight (2.3, 5.1.4). Attack tokens are gone ("its ok for more
than 2 heroes to target the same enemy"). And no hero yields ("a hero shouldnt yield. they either try to escape or
fight until die depending of what they want"): escape is what an escaping-kind hero does at its threshold in any
fight, a last-stand hero never flees, and the "refused yield" trigger is gone (7.7; `HEROES_V2.md` 6.13 decides which
kind a hero is). Section 19.2 lists what changed.

## 0. The request and the decisions

The owner's request (2026-11-12): "Also one thing I also want for the heroes is also to have better overall
capabilities. Like some can jump higher and longer, other may fly, if they work outside battle they can do it better."

This overrides the earlier choice that heroes move like players and nobody flies. Decisions:

1. **Heroes only.** Workers, soldiers and every other NPC stay player-like: they walk, jump 1.25 blocks, break blocks
   at player speed, use vanilla loot tables and only know what scouts saw. Nothing in this file applies to them, except
   that `aura` talents keep lifting their affixes within the bounds of §7.2 and that defenders answer an intruding
   hero (5.2: archers' target priority, interception), which is new AI for them, not new movement.
2. **Teleporting stays out** (stuck recovery is still the one exception). Every hero movement is physical: velocity
   set by the server, stopped by blocks, visible to players and to other NPCs.
3. **Mobility is a trait with a ceiling.** Nine families (`runner`, `charger`, `leaper`, `acrobat`, `climber`,
   `glider`, `flyer`, `hover`, `swimmer`) and two elemental footings that are only secondaries (`snowstride`,
   `ember_stride`). Each hero has one primary trait and may have one secondary (section 1). The authored tier (I-III)
   is the hero's ceiling, not an unlock gate: the hero has every feature up to its ceiling from hero level 1 (double and
   triple jumps, wall kicks, climbing, gliding, flight, the `dive`, swimming features), and the numbers (jump height,
   gap length, climb height, flight stamina and speed, swim speed) grow from 60% at level 1 to 100% at level 20, never
   below what a feature needs to work at all (1.1; the owner's answer of 2026-11-12, "all abilities but not full
   power"). Tier III is for epic and legendary heroes only.
4. **Flight is in, with a stamina bar.** About 13% of the roster flies (27 of 200, 14 female and 13 male; the owner
   kept the list), always on something the model shows: wings, a broom, a cloud, a sword, a paper crane, jets. Flyers
   land to strike, can be shot down and, while fighting, do not stay out of reach for long (sections 2 and 5).
5. **Fairness is enforced by the mod, not by authors** (section 5), as the boss rules of §6.14 are. Mobility never
   changes a move's numbers (range, damage, frames); it changes where a hero can be between moves and how it gets
   there. The owner accepts that heroes' abilities make things "unfair": the rules that remain are counterplay
   (shoot-down, air poise, landing to strike, the air budget, coming back to the fight), not limits on where a hero may
   go.
6. **Heroes cross walls; soldiers do not.** A hero may fly, glide, leap or climb over the walls of any base in raids,
   sieges, scouting rounds and fights, and uses its mobility at once to reach an enemy on a pillar, in a pen or behind
   a wall, a player or an NPC alike. Ordinary NPCs still need the gate or a breach. The AI uses the freedom (a raider
   gets in and opens the way for its soldiers, hunts the enemy hero, or strikes the heart or the storehouse) and
   defenders answer it (archers shoot an airborne hero first, guards and the home hero intercept, alarms): sections
   3.7 and 5.2.
7. **Work mastery** (part 2): every hero works its duty better than a worker (a proficiency: speed, backpack, reach,
   wear, hunger), and its primary talent adds a much stronger **specialty** when the hero does that work itself. The
   actions stay player-like (blocks still take ticks to break, furnaces and enchanting tables are real, items come from
   vanilla loot tables); the bonuses are hero bonuses, capped, and visible on the hero sheet.
8. **Vanilla loot is respected** by giving the hero's own tool virtual enchantment levels (Fortune, Looting, Luck of
   the Sea, Lure) for the loot calculation only, never above the vanilla maximum, and by the extra loot-table roll that
   affixes already use (section 12).
9. **Milestone 1 gets the core** (section 18): every family the slice heroes use (flight included: Ambrose rides his
   staff-broom), the `dive` form, the fairness rules, wall crossing with the defenders' answer, escape, and work
   mastery for the 15 milestone-1 talents. Hover, deep swimming, lava walking and the other 17 specialties come in
   milestone 2.
10. **Every hero in the roster has its mobility and work-mastery line** (`roster/roster_data.py`, checked by
    `check_roster.py`, listed in `ROSTER_PLAN.md`); the eight slice heroes are designed in full in section 15.
11. **Escape** (the owner's answers of 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13). No hero yields. A hero of the escaping kind breaks
    off a losing fight at its escape threshold, whoever it is fighting, and also leaves a lost raid or a lost base;
    a hero of the last-stand kind never flees and fights until it dies (`HEROES_V2.md` 6.13). An escaping hero may use
    any mobility, even where no pursuer can follow, and so may a scout stepping aside. While fighting it still wants
    to win and comes back, so a fight never becomes a chase. An escape ends the fight with no rewards, keeps the hero
    away for 5 minutes and happens at most once in 10 minutes; caught again within that window, it fights to the
    death (7.7).

# Part 1: Mobility

## 1. Mobility traits

### 1.1 Ceilings and level (the owner's answer of 2026-11-12: "all abilities but not full power")

- A hero file names a primary trait and its **ceiling** tier (1-3) and optionally a secondary of another family with a
  ceiling of 1-2, at most the primary's. The ceiling is the power the hero grows into, not an unlock gate. (The first
  draft gave tier I from level 1, II from 10 and III from 20; that is gone.)
- **Every feature up to the ceiling from level 1.** The hero has every feature the catalogue (1.3) lists for its
  ceiling tier and the tiers below it from hero level 1: air jumps (double and triple), wall kicks, chimneys and the
  mantle, climbing and clinging, working while clinging, the landing roll, gliding and gusts, flight, the `dive`,
  diving, under-water mining, water breathing and the surface leap, step height 1.0, no fall damage, the sprint burst,
  the traversal charge, cobweb immunity and the footings. On/off values and "any" (a ceiling-III climber's height) are
  features.
- **Numbers grow with level.** Power `p(L) = 0.6 + 0.4 x (min(L, 20) - 1) / 19` (PROPOSED: 60% at level 1, 68% at
  5, 79% at 10, 89% at 15, 100% from level 20, the level talents reach tier III; levels 21-30 add nothing to mobility).
  `logic/hero/MobilityRules` applies it in one of three ways, by the kind of number:

| Kind | At level L | Numbers of this kind |
|---|---|---|
| a bonus over what a worker has | worker value + p x (ceiling value - worker value) | jump height (over 1.25 blocks), safe fall (over 3), a hover hero's step height above 1.0 (1.0 itself is a feature), travel speed, sprint and charge boosts, movement efficiency, knockback resistance, the travel-hunger cut, swim speed (over x1.0), water movement efficiency, oxygen bonus, flight cruise and glide forward speed (over walking pace, 4.3 b/s), hover height, snowstride's speed on snow |
| a reach a worker does not have | p x ceiling value | air-jump height, gap length, climb height, glide time, flight stamina, flight climb rate, stamina regained, hover gap |
| a wait or a cost where less is better | ceiling value / p | cooldowns (sprint burst, traversal charge), the take-off threshold, glide sink rate |

  Link sizes (gaps, jump-ups, climbs) round down to whole blocks: a 6-block gap ceiling at 79% is 4.7, so 4. Counts (air
  jumps, gusts, dives) are features: a ceiling-III acrobat has its two air jumps at level 1, each lower than at level
  20. Section 15.9 shows the result for the eight slice heroes.
- **Floors.** Scaling never takes a number below the smallest use of its feature, so "every feature from level 1"
  holds in play and not only on paper: a leaper's jump height is at least 1.8 blocks (the smallest jump-up link of
  3.2 is 1.5 blocks, a field fence: without the floor a leaper I, 1.58 at level 1, would have no jump-up link until
  level 20), and a gap or hover-gap reach is at least 2 blocks (the smallest gap link; a hover I's 2-block drift is
  1.2 at level 1). Every other feature already works at 60% (15.9: a climber I climbs 3 blocks, a flyer I has 18 s of
  stamina and takes off at 50%, an acrobat I jumps up 1.5-block fences, a glider I glides 1.2 blocks per block of
  drop). `MobilityRulesTest` checks every family and ceiling at level 1 for a usable feature.
- A ceiling-I hero starts at 60% of the tier-I numbers, floors aside (the first draft gave it full tier I at level 1);
  a ceiling-III hero starts with every tier-III feature and 60% of its numbers, so it is much stronger at level 1 than
  in the first draft. Larger links within a feature come with level: a ceiling-I climber scales a 5-high base wall from
  level 13, Kagenori (leaper II) takes 2-block ledges from level 13. `mobility.levelPowerAtOne` and
  `mobility.fullPowerLevel` (8.5) tune the curve.
- A level change or a config reload re-applies the hero's attribute modifiers, all named
  `factionclash:mobility_<trait>`, so they never stack with themselves and are removed cleanly.
- `heroes.mobility.enabled = false` (section 8.5) removes every modifier: heroes then move exactly like workers, which
  is also what a format-1 hero gets.

### 1.2 Building blocks in vanilla terms

Every trait is assembled from these pieces; most are vanilla 1.21.1 attributes, so they sync to clients, show in
`/attribute` and work with potions exactly as for players. Checked against the 21.1.209 sources.

| Piece | Vanilla mechanism | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jump height | `generic.jump_strength` (`LivingEntity.getJumpPower`) | 0.42 = 1.25 blocks | apex with vanilla air physics: 0.52 = 1.84, 0.62 = 2.52, 0.75 = 3.54 blocks (simulated; a GameTest measures them) |
| Long jump, leap | an impulse `setDeltaMovement(h, v)` as v1 `leap` and vanilla's sprint-jump boost | - | ballistic: horizontal speed x0.91 per tick in air, vertical (v - gravity) x0.98; mobs get almost no air control (`getFlyingSpeed` 0.02) |
| Air jump | a second jump impulse (0.42) while airborne, counted per airtime | - | reset on landing |
| Step height | `generic.step_height` | 0.6 | 1.0 walks up full blocks like a horse |
| Fall safety | `generic.safe_fall_distance`, `generic.fall_damage_multiplier` | 3, 1.0 | multiplier 0 = no fall damage (cats are in `#fall_damage_immune`) |
| Gravity | `generic.gravity` | 0.08 | lowered only inside glide and hover controllers |
| Travel speed | `generic.movement_speed` modifier, applied only while travelling | - | never in combat goals |
| Slowing terrain | `generic.movement_efficiency` (soul sand, honey and other slow blocks) | 0 | 1.0 = not slowed |
| Webs, berry bushes, powder snow | `Entity.makeStuckInBlock` override | - | vanilla's fox ignores sweet berry bushes the same way |
| Knockback | `generic.knockback_resistance` modifier | role value | the charger's bonus: while travelling only, off in every fight (5.1.10) |
| Climbing | `onClimbable()` true while pressing into a wall in the climb state (vanilla `Spider` pattern) | - | vanilla ladder physics: the vertical speed is set to 0.2 after each move and gravity then applies, so about 0.118 blocks/tick up (2.35 b/s, a player on a ladder); descent clamped to 0.15 (3 b/s). Vanilla also clamps horizontal speed to 0.15 on a climbable, so `onClimbable` is true only in the climb state, never just for touching a wall. Vanilla's sneak-cling is player-only (`this instanceof Player`), so a clinging hero sets `setNoGravity` and zero speed itself |
| Gliding | a vertical-speed clamp plus a forward push per tick (vanilla `Chicken` slows its fall the same way); fall distance reset | - | not elytra fall-flying, which needs the item and player control |
| Flight | flying travel (`Allay.travel`: `moveRelative`, move, x0.91), `FlyingMoveControl`-style steering, `generic.flying_speed` | - | `setNoGravity` while powered. `flying_speed` is not in `LivingEntity.createLivingAttributes`, so `HeroEntity`'s attribute supplier adds it (`block_break_speed` and `submerged_mining_speed` are already in `FactionNpc`'s, which `BlockWork` reads) |
| Swimming | NeoForge `swim_speed`, `generic.water_movement_efficiency` (Depth Strider), `generic.oxygen_bonus` (Respiration) | 1.0, 0, 0 | water breathing through NeoForge `canDrownInFluidType` |
| Under-water work | `player.submerged_mining_speed` (Aqua Affinity) | 0.2 | 1.0 = full speed under water |
| Fire and lava | `fireImmune()` override, `generic.burning_time` 0; `canStandOnFluid` for lava (vanilla `Strider`) | - | |
| Freezing | `canFreeze()` override | - | walking on top of powder snow is decided per entity type or by leather boots in vanilla, so it is left out (1.15) |

### 1.4 `runner`

- **Vanilla**: a `movement_speed` modifier (`ADD_MULTIPLIED_BASE`) that `NpcNavigator.travel` switches on for the
  trip and off on arrival or when any combat goal runs; `step_height`; `movement_efficiency`; the trip's hunger cost
  goes through `NpcHunger` with the travel multiplier.
- **Navigation**: the ground navigation as today; step height 1.0 turns one-block rises into walking (the evaluator
  already reads `maxUpStep`). The sprint burst runs on path segments of 8+ blocks with no turn and nothing within 2
  blocks ahead.
- **Animations**: `move.run` (exists) and `move.sprint` for the burst (section 4.1).
- **Fairness**: travel only; in a fight, against anyone, a runner moves at its role speed (§12.4).
- **Outside combat**: every trip; traders, scouts and haulers gain the most. **In combat**: nothing beyond step height.

### 1.5 `charger`

- **Vanilla**: `step_height` 1.0, a `knockback_resistance` modifier while the hero's horizontal speed is above walking
  pace, `safe_fall_distance`, `movement_efficiency`, and `makeStuckInBlock` skipping cobwebs and sweet berry bushes
  from tier II (the fox's rule for berry bushes). The traversal charge is a speed modifier over a straight path segment,
  with the `move.charge_run` loop; it stops at doors, fences, water and drops like the `charge` form (§6.5).
- **Navigation**: ground. **Animations**: `move.charge_run`, `move.skid` (stopping).
- **Fairness**: the knockback bonus and the traversal charge are travel only: off in every fight, against players
  and NPCs alike (the first draft kept them on in "field mode"; with one mode the fight value holds everywhere).
  Cobweb immunity stays in fights for ceiling-III chargers, from level 1 (a deliberate answer to web traps; those
  heroes carry another weakness, such as Benika's `snowball`).
- **Outside combat**: loaded haulers, miners on stairs, builders carrying stacks. **In combat**: step height and web
  immunity only; a charger's footing in a fight comes from its role and its kit (Benika's `rage`), not from this
  trait.

### 1.6 `leaper`

- **Vanilla**: `jump_strength` and `safe_fall_distance` modifiers; a gap leap is one impulse chosen by the leap solver
  (3.3) from the gap and the height difference. The lowest-speed impulses (vertical, horizontal per tick) that land on
  the far cell's centre within 0.15 blocks in the vanilla air simulation, from the near cell's centre on level ground:
  (0.42, 0.52) for a 3-block gap, (0.50, 0.72) for 5, (0.54, 0.82) for 6 and (0.63, 1.00) for 8 (apex 1.25 to 2.6
  blocks, 12-17 ticks in the air); a 1-block rise needs about 0.07 more vertical. (The first draft's (0.65, 0.90) for 6
  overshot by a full block, onto the cell beyond: the solver, not hand-picked numbers, decides.)
- **Navigation**: jump-up links of 1.5 (fences and walls), 2 and 3 blocks, up to the hero's current jump height minus
  0.3; gap links up to its current gap width; drop links down to its safe fall distance (3.2). Walls of hostile bases
  are terrain like any other (3.7).
- **Animations**: `move.crouch_jump` (the 0.3 s wind-up, the tell), `move.leap`, `move.fall`, `move.land_heavy`,
  `move.land_roll`.
- **Fairness**: a leap is never aimed next to a drop deeper than the safe fall distance, into lava, fire or the void;
  in a fight it never crosses a gap whose floor is one of those (3.3). While fighting it may leap across a gap its
  enemies cannot follow, but comes back within 4 s (rule 5.1.15); escaping, it leaves the gap between them (7.7).
- **Outside combat**: ravines, streams, fences and ledges without bridging or detours. **In combat**: reaches high
  ground and an enemy standing on it at once (3.7) and may start a `dive` from its own jump apex (7.3).

### 1.7 `acrobat`

- **Vanilla**: an air jump sets the vertical speed to 0.42 again (1.25 more blocks) once per airtime (twice at III); the
  wall kick is an air jump off a wall face the hero is touching (`horizontalCollision`), pushing 0.4 away from it; the
  mantle lifts the hero onto a ledge whose top is within 1 block of its hands at the apex (a short upward push and a
  forward step, never a position jump). III sets `fall_damage_multiplier` to 0.
- **Navigation**: the leaper's links with the acrobat's reach (jump-up 2 / 2 / 3 blocks, gaps 4 / 5 / 6 at full
  power) plus, for ceilings II-III, chimney links: up a shaft 1-2 blocks wide by alternating wall kicks.
- **Animations**: `move.air_jump` (a tuck or twist), `move.wall_kick`, `move.mantle`, `move.land_roll`.
- **Fairness**: as the leaper. Air jumps make a hero good at jumping `wave` rings (heroes against heroes); anyone
  who jumps clears them too.
- **Outside combat**: scouts and fishermen over rocks and ravines; guards vault to an alarm. **In combat**: hops over
  ground hazards, kicks off arena walls to get round a shield (positioning only: no move starts in the air except a
  `dive` from the apex).

### 1.8 `climber`

- **Vanilla**: the spider pattern: `onClimbable()` returns true while the hero is in the climb state and presses into
  the wall face, so vanilla's own ladder physics move it (2.35 b/s up, the descent clamped to 3 b/s); it clings when it
  stops: `HeroMovement` stops pressing into the face (no `horizontalCollision`, so no climb push), zeroes its speed and
  sets `setNoGravity` until it moves on (vanilla's sneak-cling on ladders only works for players). From II, `BlockWork`
  counts a clinging climber as standing (vanilla divides break speed by 5 for anyone not on the ground: a player on a
  ladder is that slow).
- **Navigation**: climb links (3.4); `WallClimberNavigation`'s direct-pursuit behaviour when chasing a target up a
  face.
- **Animations**: `move.climb` (exists), `move.climb_idle` (clinging), `move.climb_down`, `move.mantle`.
- **Fairness**: climbs any wall within its height, a hostile base's included (3.7, 5.2); any hit or a knockback of 0.5
  or more makes it let go (it falls, safe fall applies, 0.5 s before it can grip again); no move starts while clinging;
  while fighting it may climb out of reach but comes back within 4 s (rule 5.1.15); escaping, it climbs where no one
  follows (7.7). Ceiling cling is left out (1.15).
- **Outside combat**: woodcutters fell tall trees from the trunk instead of building the scaffold pillar (`Scaffold`),
  miners climb out of shafts and pits instead of pillaring (`DigOut`), builders work on walls, scouts climb cliffs and
  trees for a lookout (+16 sighting radius from 12+ blocks up, the flyers' altitude bonus) and a rival's wall to look
  inside (5.2). **In combat**: climbs out of a pit and up to an enemy's ledge or pillar at once, a player's or an NPC
  archer's (3.7); on a raid, up the wall into the yard (5.2).

### 1.9 `glider`

- **Vanilla**: while gliding, a falling hero's vertical speed is clamped to its current sink rate and a forward push
  keeps its forward speed at its current value (ceiling and level, 1.1); fall distance is reset every tick (no fall
  damage, as with Slow Falling). A gust is one upward push of 1.5 blocks (fans, a wind sack, a kite). Gliding ends on
  landing, on any projectile hit (the hero falls with its safe fall distance), on touching water or after the time cap
  (then it sinks at 6 b/s). An escaping glider (7.7) uses gusts and the time cap as usual: a glide still only goes down.
- **Navigation**: glide links (3.5) from an edge to a landing cell; long trips prefer a ridge launch when it saves at
  least 30% of the walking path.
- **Animations**: `move.glide_start`, `move.glide`, `move.gust`, `move.land`; the carrier opens (4.3).
- **Fairness**: a glide never ends higher than it began (gusts only offset part of the drop), so a glider cannot stay
  aloft; it may glide over walls, a hostile base's included (3.7).
- **Outside combat**: down from ridges, cliffs and the walls; across valleys and rivers from a height. **In combat**:
  from a ledge into a `dive`; on a raid, from a height outside over the wall into the yard (5.2).

### 1.11 `hover`

- **Vanilla**: no gravity while a supporting surface (ground or a fluid surface) is within its current height plus
  0.5; a small controller keeps the feet at that height above it; `step_height`; `canStandOnFluid` for water (the
  strider's rule, for water instead of lava). Farmland is trampled by `fallOn` with a fall distance over 0.5; a hovering
  hero keeps its fall distance at 0, so it never tramples. Over a gap the support is far below: it keeps its height for
  its current gap width, then sinks at 4 b/s.
- **Navigation**: the ground navigation with water as a walkable path type (malus 0) and gap links up to its current
  gap width.
- **Animations**: `move.hover` (idle, feet off the ground, hem and sleeves hanging), `move.drift` (moving).
- **Fairness**: never more than 1.5 blocks above its support, so always in melee reach; not over lava without
  `ember_stride`.
- **Outside combat**: farmers on irrigated fields, fishermen on the water surface, haulers over mud and water. **In
  combat**: crosses water and small gaps while spacing.

### 1.12 `swimmer`

- **Vanilla**: NeoForge `swim_speed` (x1.5 / x2.0 / x2.5), `water_movement_efficiency`, `oxygen_bonus` (the Respiration
  rule: each air loss is skipped with chance bonus / (bonus + 1)); ceiling III overrides `canDrownInFluidType`. Ceilings
  II-III, from level 1: `submerged_mining_speed` 1.0 (Aqua Affinity) and diving: the float goal is suspended while it
  heads for a target below the surface (the drowned do the same).
- **Navigation**: a ceiling-I swimmer swims with the ground navigation (no new navigator, so milestone 1 needs none);
  ceilings II-III switch to vanilla `AmphibiousPathNavigation` while a path crosses or ends in water.
- **Animations**: `move.swim` (exists), `move.swim_fast`, `move.tread` (idle in water), `move.swim_down`,
  `move.surface_leap` (III).
- **Fairness**: while fighting, a swimmer more than 3 blocks under water for 4 s counts as unreachable and surfaces
  (5.1.9); escaping, deep water is a refuge (7.7).
- **Outside combat**: crosses rivers and lakes instead of bridging or detouring; fishermen reach the deepest water;
  miners work under water at full speed. **In combat**: fights in water unslowed (players and soldiers are slowed
  there; thematic, and the hero still has to come out to reach an enemy on land).

### 1.13 `snowstride` and `ember_stride`

- `snowstride` (frost heroes and snow spirits): `canFreeze()` false and `makeStuckInBlock` skips powder snow (the
  hero still sinks into it but is not slowed); II adds +20% speed on blocks tagged `#snow` or `#ice`.
- `ember_stride` (fire heroes and phoenixes): `fireImmune()` true and `burning_time` 0; II: `canStandOnFluid` for lava
  (the strider's rule) and lava as a walkable path type. Vanilla's `LiquidBlock` collision holds an entity up only on a
  source block (level 0) with nothing of the same fluid above, so only lava sources are walkable cells; flowing lava
  stays a hazard (the hero is fire-proof there, but sinks). While fighting it never stands on lava or fire for more
  than 2 s (no enemy can follow, so that counts as unreachable: 5.1.9); escaping, it may cross a lava lake (7.7). The
  hazard rules (§6.12) still keep everyone's knockback away from lava.

### 1.14 Combining traits

- A primary and a secondary apply together; where both set the same value (step height, safe fall) the larger counts.
- `flyer` is primary only and already glides, so it never takes `glider` or `hover` as a secondary; `glider` and
  `hover` never pair.
- `snowstride` needs the frost element or the snow spirit archetype; `ember_stride` fire or the phoenix.

### 1.15 Left out

| Idea | Why it stays out |
|---|---|
| Teleport blinks, phasing through blocks | the player-like rule (decision 2); decoys and dashes cover the look |
| Ceiling cling | no vanilla movement model or pathfinding for upside-down travel; an ambush from a ceiling reads as appearing from nowhere |
| Walking on top of powder snow | vanilla decides it per entity type (`#powder_snow_walkable_mobs`, which would cover every hero) or by leather boots; `snowstride` stops the slowing instead |
| Frost-walker ice, placed platforms | abilities never place blocks (§0 decision 11) |
| Carrying players or NPCs through the air | lifting anyone out of control leaves no counter, and a flying grab could drop its victim from any height; grabs stay grounded (§6.5) |
| Mobility for workers, soldiers or player mounts | decision 1 |

## 2. Flight in detail

### 2.1 Numbers (PROPOSED)

Full values for ceilings I / II / III (level 20 and above). Below level 20 (1.1): stamina, climb rate and stamina
regained are x p, cruise speed is 4.3 + p x (cruise - 4.3) b/s, the take-off threshold is divided by p, and the glide
is the glider's of the same ceiling at the same power. Every flyer flies from level 1.

| Ceiling | I | II | III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stamina (seconds of powered flight) | 30 | 45 | 60 |
| Cruise speed | 6 b/s | 7 b/s | 8 b/s |
| Climb rate | 2 b/s | 3 b/s | 4 b/s |
| Glide (unpowered) | as glider I | as glider II | as glider III |
| Take-off needs | 30% stamina | 25% | 20% |
| Stamina regained on the ground (after 1 s) | 2.0 per s | 2.5 per s | 3.0 per s |

Drain per second: powered flight 1.0; cruising out of combat 0.5 (long glides between flaps); hovering in place 2.0;
gliding 0; carrying a backpack more than half full x1.5; while fighting x1.5; escaping (7.7) x1.0, with no air budget. A
ceiling-I flyer at full power on a long trip flies about 60 s (about 360 blocks), lands, rests 16 s and goes on: about
4.7 b/s on average in a straight line, against 4.3 for walking round the terrain. At level 1 it flies 36 s (about 190
blocks) and rests 16 s, about 3.7 b/s on average, so the mode choice (3.5) compares the two and a young flyer walks
long flat trips; Ambrose (ceiling II) averages 4.4 b/s at level 1 and 5.8 at level 20.

### 2.2 Take-off, cruise, landing, rest

- **Take-off** (0.6 s, the tell): needs the stamina threshold and 4 blocks of headroom; a crouch, the carrier opens
  (wings spread, the broom or cloud appears under the hero), one upward push of 1.5 blocks, then powered flight.
  Players hear and see it: the wing-beat or carrier sound plays at the hero.
- **Cruise**: out of combat the hero flies at a cruise layer 6-10 blocks above the highest terrain along the segment
  (2.3); in combat at most at the altitudes of 2.3.
- **Landing** (0.4 s): chosen at a standable cell with 2 blocks of headroom, never on farmland (a landing trampled it),
  lava, fire or powder snow; inside a hostile base only on purpose (a raid or a scouting round, 5.2), since its archers
  shoot what lands there; the fall distance is reset, so it takes no damage.
- **Low stamina**: at 25% the hero heads for the nearest landing cell; at 0 it can only glide (it descends at its glide
  tier) and is **exhausted** (`move.winded` on landing, no take-off until 50%). The `tired` bit (4.2) makes the wings
  droop and the face tired from 25%, a tell players can read.
- **Weather**: in a thunderstorm out of combat a flyer travels on foot (lightning rods are not flyers' friends).

### 2.3 Altitude and airspace

| Situation | Ceiling (PROPOSED) |
|---|---|
| Travel, scouting, escaping (7.7) | 24 blocks above the ground under it, cruise layer 6-10 |
| On a raid or a scouting round, outside a fight (the approach, the wall crossing, circling the target) | 10 blocks above the ground under it (a wall top counts as ground, so a flyer crosses any wall it meets); the first draft's "field mode" value, kept for raid travel only |
| While fighting (any enemy engaged, a player or an NPC: §6.1) | 6 blocks above the ground under it or above the feet of the highest engaged enemy standing on a block, whichever is higher (so an enemy on a pillar or a wall, a player or an NPC archer, is always in reach), plus the reach rule of 5.1.6 |
| Over a hostile base | the ceiling of the situation it is in (raid travel until it is engaged, then the fight's); there is no closed airspace (the first draft's rule is gone: 3.7) |
| Under a ceiling lower than 4 blocks, in caves, tunnels, forests too dense to take off | walks |

### 2.4 Shot down: air poise

An airborne hero (flying, gliding, or a leaper or acrobat in the air) has **air poise**, a bar of 100 that decays 10 per
second after 2 s without a hit. When it fills, or at once on a stun, a freeze, Slowness II or worse, or a lightning
strike, the hero is **downed**: the carrier folds, it falls (no control; its horizontal speed is cut to a fifth, so it
drops almost straight down where it was shot instead of sailing 4 blocks on its momentum), takes fall damage above
its safe fall distance (6 for flyers; the fall counts as one hit for the per-hit cap of §6.2, whoever downed it), then
lies 2 s on the ground (`move.downed`, +20% damage taken) and cannot take off for 12 s. A downing also adds 30 poise
(§6.9). Sources are in 5.3, and they count the same from a player and from an NPC: defenders' archers bring down a
raiding flyer (they shoot an airborne hero first: 5.2). Air poise applies while escaping as well: shooting it down is
how anyone stops a flyer that escapes (7.7).

### 2.5 Carriers

A flyer is always carried by something visible, so players can tell at a glance who flies. The roster's flight carriers
by archetype (`check_roster.py` `FLIGHT_CARRIER`): tengu crow wings, angels feathered wings, phoenixes ember wings,
western dragons dragon wings, demon lords demon wings, vampires bat wings, paladins and magical heroes light or star
wings (glow-painted feathered wings), witches a broom, eastern dragons a cloud, sword cultivators a flying sword,
onmyoji a great paper crane, androids jets. **Worn** wings (crow, feathered, bat, dragon, demon) are on the model all
the time, folded while the hero walks, so the roster look names them; **summoned** carriers (light, star and ember
wings, broom, cloud, sword, crane, jets) appear with the take-off, which is itself the 0.6 s tell. Gliders use wings,
capes and cloaks (bat cape, crow cloak), fans, a kite, a wind sack, or a canopy (dandelion-seed parasol, mushroom cap),
named in the roster look or prop and recorded in `roster.json` as `mobility.carrier`. Hover heroes need no carrier
(frost mist, qi, rune circles, cloak hems; a glow or particle under the feet). Art side: 4.3.

## 3. Navigation

### 3.1 Overview

`HeroEntity` keeps `NpcNavigator` (waypoints, repaths, the stall watchdog, stuck recovery) as its travel layer and
swaps what is underneath by mode. `Mob.getNavigation()` and `getMoveControl()` are public and overridable, so the hero
returns the navigation and move control of its current mode:

| Mode | Navigation | Move control | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground | `HeroGroundNavigation` (today's `NpcPathNavigation` with `HeroNodeEvaluator` links: 3.2) | vanilla `MoveControl` plus `HeroMovement` for links | everyone |
| climb | the ground navigation's climb links; `WallClimberNavigation` behaviour for direct pursuit | vanilla, with `onClimbable` | climbers |
| water | vanilla `AmphibiousPathNavigation` | vanilla | swimmers II-III (milestone 2) |
| air | `HeroFlightNavigation` (a `FlyingPathNavigation` near the ends, the cruise planner of 3.5 between) | `HeroFlightControl` (`FlyingMoveControl`-style steering, `setNoGravity` while powered) and an `Allay`-style `travel` | flyers |

`HeroMovement` (server, per hero, ticked from `HeroEntity.aiStep`) owns the physical actions: jump and leap impulses,
air jumps, wall kicks, mantles, climbing state, glide and hover controllers, take-off and landing, stamina, air poise
and downing. It sets `DATA_LOCO` (4.2).

### 3.2 Ground links (`HeroNodeEvaluator`)

The evaluator extends today's gate-aware walk evaluator. For each node it adds extra neighbours only where the vanilla
neighbour fails (a hole, a wall, a fluid), at most 8 per node:

| Link | From, to | Condition | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| jump-up | a node to a standable cell 1.5-3 blocks higher (fences, walls, ledges), 1 block across | the hero's current jump height (1.1) minus 0.3 clears it; headroom over both | 2 + rise |
| gap | across a gap 2 to the hero's current width, cardinal directions, landing at most 1 block higher | the leap solver (3.3) finds an impulse that lands within 0.15 blocks of the centre with 2 blocks of headroom along the arc | 1 + width / 2 |
| drop | down to the safe fall distance (vanilla's own drop search already uses `getMaxFallDistance`, which the hero overrides to its safe fall distance) | landing standable and not hazardous | 1 + drop / 4 |
| climb | from a wall's foot up the face to the standable cell on top, or to a cell mid-wall for work | climber, height within its current climb height; every face cell solid | 1.5 per block |
| chimney | up a 1-2 wide shaft by wall kicks | acrobat with ceiling II-III | 2 per block |
| glide | from an edge to a landing cell at most (drop x the hero's current glide ratio) away, 32 blocks at most | glider or flyer; a clear line | 1 per 2 blocks |

Every link is checked against the hazard rules before it is offered. The walls of any base, a hostile one's included,
are terrain like any other (3.7). Vanilla's `Path` holds plain nodes, so the navigation keeps a side table from node
index to link kind and impulse; the vanilla `PathType` enum is not extended.

### 3.3 Following links (`HeroMovement`, `LeapSolver`)

`HeroGroundNavigation.followThePath` hands a link to `HeroMovement` when the hero reaches its start node: the hero
faces the landing, plays the wind-up (`move.crouch_jump`, 0.3 s; a running acrobat skips it), applies the impulse,
waits for landing and hands back. `logic/hero/LeapSolver` (pure) picks the impulse: it simulates vanilla air physics
(vertical (v - g) x 0.98, horizontal x 0.91 per tick) over a grid of impulses within the hero's current limits
(ceiling and level, 1.1) and returns the lowest-speed one that lands on the target cell's centre within 0.15 blocks
(the hero is 0.6 wide, so it stands well on the cell) and whose bounding box clears every block along the arc; no
solution means no link. Failure modes and their rules (a missed leap is the main way a hero could die by its own
movement):

- **Take-off conditions**: the hero stands still (speed under 0.05) on the start cell before the impulse, except an
  acrobat's running take-off, which the solver re-solves from the actual position and velocity on that tick. No link
  starts from a block with a jump factor under 1 (honey) or a speed factor under 1 (soul sand), in water or powder
  snow, in a cobweb, or while the hero has Slowness, Levitation or Slow Falling (they change the arc); the hero walks
  instead.
- **Hazard floors**: a gap whose floor (anything the hero would fall into from the arc) is lava, fire, the void or a
  drop deeper than the safe fall distance is a **risky gap**. Out of combat a risky gap needs a wide landing (the target
  cell and the cell beyond it standable, or a wall beyond it); in combat (a combat goal running, a hostile within 16
  blocks, or damage taken in the last 5 s) risky gaps are refused, because a hit in the air would drop the hero into
  them; an escaping hero (7.7) treats a risky gap as out of combat (a wide landing is enough), because the pursuer is
  behind it. Fire-proof heroes (`ember_stride`) count lava and fire floors as plain drops.
- **In the air**: at the apex `HeroMovement` re-simulates the rest of the arc from the actual state; if it no longer
  lands (a hit, knockback, an entity in the way), an acrobat spends its air jump to correct it, any other hero lets the
  fall happen (safe fall applies) and the navigation rebuilds from where it lands.
- **Dead links**: a failed link (the hero did not land on the target cell) marks it dead for 10 minutes, the way
  `NpcNavigator` remembers dead ends today, and the path is rebuilt without it.

### 3.4 Climbing

A climb link is followed by walking into the face: `onClimbable` turns true and vanilla moves the hero up at 2.35 b/s;
at the top it mantles (III) or steps over the edge. For work cells mid-wall (a log, a wall block), the hero stops and
clings. A hit while climbing ends the climb (1.8); the navigation then rebuilds the path from where it landed.

### 3.5 Flight: the cruise planner and the ground fallback

- `logic/hero/FlightPlanner` (pure, unit-tested) plans flight from heightmap samples (`MOTION_BLOCKING`, which counts
  leaves, every 4 blocks along the line and 2 blocks to each side, the highest of the three): a polyline at the cruise
  layer. Outside a raid or a scouting round it gives hostile bases a berth (a cost, not a ban: 3.7). Between the ends
  the hero flies the polyline straight; `FlyingPathNavigation`
  (a 3D search) is used only within 16 blocks of take-off and landing, under trees and near buildings. This keeps
  flight cheaper than walking: no 3D search over long distances.
- **Only loaded terrain.** The planner samples only chunks that are already loaded (`getChunkNow`; it never loads or
  generates a chunk, which would stall the server), so it cannot plan a 360-block leg in one go: NPCs travel by 24-block
  `NpcNavigator` waypoints and carry a 3x3 chunk ticket (`ChunkLoading.updateTraveler`). A flying hero carries a 5x5
  ticket instead (the next waypoint is always loaded) and the planner extends the polyline one waypoint at a time. The
  stamina rule replaces "a landing at each leg's end": the hero keeps enough stamina to reach the best landing cell
  already seen in loaded terrain plus 25%, and lands when it would not. It never flies into a chunk that is not loaded
  (it waits at the edge, hovering at 2.0 drain, for at most 3 s, then lands).
- **Obstacles between samples** (a spruce top, a tower, a cliff edge): `HeroFlightControl` probes 4 blocks ahead along
  its heading every 4 ticks; a solid block in the way makes it climb at its climb rate until the probe is clear, or turn
  along the planner's next waypoint if the climb would pass the altitude ceiling.
- **Stuck in the air** (flyers wedged under leaves or a ceiling): a head bump while climbing (`verticalCollision` with
  upward speed) for 10 ticks, or less than 1 block of progress in 2 s, ends the flight: the hero descends at its glide
  rate (or falls with its safe fall distance) and the trip goes on with the ground navigation; that leg is a dead link
  for 10 minutes. If it cannot descend either, the ordinary stall watchdog and stuck recovery of `NpcNavigator` apply.
- **Take-off headroom** is checked over the full column the take-off rises through (4 blocks over the hero's 2x2
  footprint), not only above its head, so it never launches into an overhang.
- **Choosing a mode** (per `NpcNavigator` waypoint): fly when the ground route is at least 1.5 times the straight
  distance, or crosses water, a ravine, a cliff or a wall the hero cannot link over, or the trip is longer than 40
  blocks and the hero's average flight speed at its level (2.1) beats walking, and the stamina covers the next leg.
  Walk otherwise, and always when the planner finds no legal landing, when the headroom is under 4 blocks, in a
  thunderstorm out of combat (an escape flies in any weather), or while the hero is exhausted.
- **Ground fallback**: a flight that cannot continue (a blocked leg, stamina at 25%, a downing) lands at the best cell
  reached and the trip goes on with the ground navigation; the ground navigation that fails (no path, a stall) asks for
  a flight if allowed. `NpcNavigator`'s failure counting covers both modes, so a hero cannot alternate forever.

### 3.6 Water

Ceiling-I swimmers and every other hero swim with the ground navigation as workers do. Ceilings II-III switch to
`AmphibiousPathNavigation` for a waypoint whose path crosses or ends in water (milestone 2), and back on land.

### 3.7 Walls and reach (the owner's answer of 2026-11-12)

The owner accepts that heroes' abilities make things "unfair", so a hero's mobility has no wall rule:

- **No closed airspace.** The walls of any base, a hostile one's included, are terrain for a hero: it may climb them,
  leap or mantle onto them, glide or fly over them, in a raid, a siege, a scouting round or a fight, and it uses its
  links inside a hostile base as anywhere else. The first draft's `BaseZones.hostileAirspace`, its 2- and 4-block
  margins, the "pushed in" bookkeeping and the campaign check that voluntary crossings stay at 0 are gone. What a hero
  does once it is over the wall, and how defenders answer it, is in 5.2.
- **Ordinary NPCs are unchanged.** Soldiers and workers have no links: they still walk in through a gate or a breach
  and pillar or bridge at player speed (`workersMoveUnchanged`, 3.8).
- **A preference, not a rule.** Out of a raid or a scouting round, travel routes and flight plans give hostile bases a
  berth (path and plan cost x3 within their walls box plus 16 blocks, PROPOSED), because defenders shoot at what
  crosses; a travelling trader does not fly over an enemy's yard by chance.
- **Reaching a target at once.** A hero whose target cannot be reached on foot (a player on a pillar, in a pen, behind
  a wall or across a gap; an archer on a ledge or a wall walk) uses its mobility as the first step: climb, jump-up,
  gap leap, chimney, glide or flight, whichever reaches (`ReachPlanner`, 3.8). There is no far-band wait and no 10 s
  timer for it; §6.12's steps (far-band moves, then after 10 s `DigOut` and `BlockWork` at player speed) remain only
  for a hero with no link that reaches. Soldiers keep today's `Pursuit` exactly (a breach, a pillar of at most 3
  blocks or undermining planned at once by `ReachPlanner`, else waiting out of reach and calling the archers): the
  10 s step was never theirs, it is §6.12's hero rule. The fight's altitude ceiling counts from the highest engaged
  enemy standing on a block, a player or an NPC (2.3), so a flyer can rise to an enemy on a 20-block pillar (over more
  than one take-off at low levels: Ambrose climbs 1.8 b/s at level 1, about 16 blocks in his 8 s with the take-off
  push).
- **Striking what it reached.** Rules 5.1.5 (melee only on the ground) and 5.1.8 (nothing starts while clinging) keep
  their counterplay for moves, but alone they would make the reach hollow: an enemy on a 1x1 pillar (a player, or an
  NPC archer on its own pillar) leaves no standable cell beside it. So where the target stands with no standable cell
  for the hero within melee reach (a 1x1 pillar top, a 1-wide wall top, a ledge just big enough for one), the hero's
  **basic attack** (never a move)
  may land from where its mobility holds it: a climber clinging just below the target's feet, a leaper or acrobat at
  the apex of its jump, a flyer beside the target. The counterplay is unchanged: any hit or knockback of 0.5 knocks
  the climber off (5.1.8), blows and arrows add air poise to the flyer (2.4), and the air budget brings the flyer
  down. A climber II-III, which works while clinging, may instead break the block under the target's feet at player
  speed (`BlockWork`, under `Reach`'s may-break rule: what a soldier's undermining may break).
- **Reach flights.** Outside its aerial phases (7.4) a flyer takes off in a fight only to reach a target that no walk
  or other link reaches: no moves in the air, basic attacks only as above, the same per-take-off budget, reach clock
  and ground time (5.1.4, 5.1.6). So Ambrose, aerial only in phase 2, still rises to an enemy on a pillar in phases 1
  and 3.
- **Player builds outside faction bases** (a player's own fort, a pillar, a pit) are terrain, as before.
- **The counterplay stays** and is not a crossing rule: air poise and downing (2.4), archers that shoot an airborne
  hero first, interception (5.2), flyers landing to strike or diving with a tell (5.1.5), the air budget (5.1.4), all
  PROPOSED numbers to tune.

### 3.8 With today's navigation code

- `NpcNavigator`: unchanged API; a mode choice per waypoint (3.5); `canWalkToward` and the pit detour see links.
  Every new branch here and below sits behind `npc.mobility()`, which is empty for workers and soldiers, so they
  run exactly today's code (same evaluator, attributes, fall distance and chunk ticket; the static GameTest
  `workersMoveUnchanged`).
- `DigOut` (pillaring and bridging) and `Scaffold` (tall trees): a hero first tries its links (a climber climbs, a
  leaper leaps, a flyer flies); only when none applies does it pillar or bridge at player speed as today.
- `Reach` and `ReachPlanner` (unreachable targets): for a hero the plan gains mobility options before tools, tried at
  once without the 10 s wait (3.7): climb, jump-up, gap leap, glide, a flight within the air rules (5.1, a reach
  flight outside aerial phases), then breach and pillar; the plan's end may be a strike from a link (3.7). A soldier's
  plan and `Pursuit` are today's, with one targeting rule: an airborne enemy hero (its `DATA_LOCO` mode is airborne,
  glide or fly) is held for, not planned toward (no pillar toward something that moves in the air; the soldier waits
  under it out of reach and calls the archers, as `Pursuit` does for a target it cannot plan for) until it lands.
- Stuck recovery: unchanged and still the only teleport. A hero stuck mid-air (a flyer wedged under leaves) lands or
  glides first; the recovery then runs as for any NPC.

### 3.9 Performance budget (PROPOSED)

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Extra neighbours per node | at most 8, only where the vanilla neighbour fails |
| Leap solver | one precomputed table (gap, rise) -> lowest impulse at server start; the hero's current limits (ceiling and level) pick from it; a lookup at path time |
| Flight planning | heightmap samples every 4 blocks (3 across), loaded chunks only, one 24-block waypoint at a time (at most 48 samples per extension); `FlyingPathNavigation` only within 16 blocks of the ends, at most 1 search per hero per 20 ticks; the forward probe every 4 ticks |
| Chunk tickets | a flying hero's traveller ticket is 5x5 instead of 3x3 while it flies (`ChunkLoading`), released on landing |
| `HeroMovement` per tick | under 0.01 ms per hero (a few checks; stamina and air poise are counters) |

| `move.air_dive` | no | 0.8 s + | the generated default for a `dive` move without its own recipe |

Rules for `herogen check`: locomotion loops close; one-shots end on the pose of the loop that follows (`move.fall`,
`move.idle` or `move.fly`); no root motion (movement is physical, as for every animation); the riding poses keep the
feet on the mount locator. Style libraries may override any of them (a samurai's `move.leap` keeps the sword hand
still), and a hero may override for flavour (Natsuho's cat twist on `move.air_jump`).

### 4.2 Driving it: `DATA_LOCO`

The `base` controller (§4.4) picks its animation from speed, `onGround` and `isInWater` today; flight, gliding,
climbing and hovering cannot be read from those, so a synced field drives them:

| Bits | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | mode: 0 ground (today's rules), 1 airborne, 2 glide, 3 fly, 4 fly hover, 5 hover, 6 climb, 7 cling, 8 swim, 9 swim down, 10 charge run, 11 sprint, 12 downed, 13 winded, 14 ride, 15 reserved |
| 4-5 | one-shot: 0 none, 1 start (jump, take-off, glide start), 2 end (land, landing), 3 special (air jump, wall kick, mantle, gust; which one follows from the mode and bit 6) |
| 6 | sequence toggle: flips for each new one-shot, so two air jumps in a row both play |
| 7 | tired (stamina under 25%): wings droop, the face uses the tired frames |

One-shots play on the `base` controller over the loop, so an attack on the `action` controller is never interrupted by
a landing; an attack while airborne keys the upper body only and the legs keep the air pose.

### 4.3 Carriers on the model

- **Wings**: the rig's `wing_l`/`wing_r` bones and the `wings` part category (§13.3: crow, feathered white, bat,
  dragon, ember feather, mechanical, small paired, broken black). Mobility needs secondary loops `sec.wings_fold`
  (walking), `sec.wings_flap` (powered flight, rate from speed) and `sec.wings_glide` (spread), and light or star wings
  as a glow-painted feathered variant.
- **Mounts**: a new part category `mounts` drawn at a `mount` locator under the feet: broom, cloud, flying sword, paper
  crane, jet flames, kite. Shown only while the mode is fly, glide or ride (bone visibility, like phase parts). A hero
  whose weapon doubles as its mount (Ambrose's staff-broom) declares `carrier.part: "weapon"`: the weapon prop moves
  from the hand to the locator.
- **Canopies and capes** for gliders: existing capes and cloaks spread (a `glide` pose of the cape chain); fans held
  out; leaf, seed and mushroom-cap canopies held overhead (a small `mounts` variant at the hand).
- The culling box (§3.1, inflated by 1.5 blocks) already covers spread wings.

### 4.4 Reserved names

Move ids may not equal a locomotion name without the `move.` prefix (`jump`, `leap`, `land`, `glide`, `fly`, `hover`,
`climb`, `swim`, `takeoff`, `downed`, `ride`, `air_dive` and the rest of 4.1), because a hero's own `move.<id>`
would shadow the core animation (§4.5). The validator refuses such an id (NOT LOADED for that move, with the name).

## 5. Fairness

### 5.1 Rules (enforced by the mod)

1. **No teleporting**; every hero movement is velocity, stopped by blocks.
2. **Walls** (the owner's answer of 2026-11-12, replacing the first draft's "hostile ground" rule): a hero may climb,
   leap or mantle onto, glide or fly over the walls of any base, a hostile one's included, in raids, sieges, scouting
   rounds and fights, and reaches an enemy on a pillar, in a pen or behind a wall with its mobility at once, a player
   or an NPC alike (3.7). There are no margins and no crossing counter. Ordinary NPCs still need the gate or a breach.
3. **Altitude ceilings** of 2.3 (the fight's ceiling counts from the highest engaged enemy standing on a block too,
   so it never keeps a hero from its target, whoever that is).
4. **Air budget** (counterplay, PROPOSED): while fighting, against anyone, at most `bossAirSeconds` (8 s) airborne per
   take-off; at least 6 s on the ground before the next take-off (4 s in phase 3, never less); airborne at most 40% of
   any 30 s (so at most 12 s of every 30, whatever the take-offs). On a raid or a scouting round outside a fight (the
   approach and the wall crossing, before anyone has engaged it) at most 10 s per take-off, enough to cross any wall
   ring, and at least 6 s on the ground between: the first draft's "field mode" value, which now holds only until
   the first enemy engages it. Travel flight and an escape (7.7) have only the stamina limit.
5. **Strikes**: melee only on the ground or as a `dive` (7.3: heavy, a landing decal that grows over the wind-up, at
   least 1.0 s of recovery on the ground). In the air a hero may only cast light ranged moves (`shot`, `volley`,
   `bolt`, `barrage`; at most 3 per take-off) and wind up a telegraphed `dive` or `rain`. One exception, so that the
   reach of 3.7 is real: a basic attack (never a move) on a target that stands with no standable cell for the hero
   within melee reach (a 1x1 pillar top) may land from the air, the apex of a jump or a cling (3.7).
6. **Reach**: while fighting, at most 4 s per take-off where no engaged enemy can hit it in melee (more than 3 blocks
   above every engaged enemy's standing height; players and NPCs count the same), unless it is winding up a
   telegraphed move. The airborne case of rule 15; it does not apply while escaping.
7. **Shot down**: air poise and downing (2.4), escaping included.
8. **Climbing**: a hit or a knockback of 0.5 or more knocks a climber off; no move starts while clinging (the basic
   attack of 3.7's pillar case, and a climber II-III's work, may).
9. **Places its enemies cannot follow**: while fighting, deep water (swimmers) and lava (ember stride) count as
   unreachable after 4 s and 2 s; the hero comes back (rule 15). While escaping they are refuges (7.7).
10. **Travel bonuses off in fights**: runner speed and sprint, the charger's traversal charge and knockback bonus.
    Jumps, climbing, gliding, flight within these rules, swimming and footing stay. An escape is travel: the travel
    bonuses are on.
11. **Moves are not changed by mobility**: ranges, damage, frames and cooldowns keep the validator's caps (§14.1).
12. **No carrying** (1.15): grabs only on the ground; the validator refuses a grab in an aerial window.
13. **Hazards**: no leap, glide, landing or dive into lava, fire, powder snow over a drop, the void or next to a drop
    deeper than the safe fall distance, escaping included (a fire-proof hero excepted for lava and fire); the
    knockback it deals stays hazard-aware for every receiver (§6.12).
14. Creative and spectator players never count (as §6.1). Nothing else in this list reads whether an enemy is a
    player.
15. **Fighting versus escaping** (in every fight; replaces the first draft's "no escape links", rule 5.1.15). While
    **fighting**, a hero wants to win: it may use any link, climb, glide or flight to reach an engaged enemy or to
    take a position, including ground no engaged enemy can reach as an ordinary person moves (a player and an NPC
    soldier alike: walking, sprint-jumping gaps up to 3 blocks and rises of 1, or towering up 2 blocks), but it
    spends at most 4 s at a time there (PROPOSED) unless it is winding up a telegraphed move; then it comes back
    toward its enemies. Links toward an enemy, out of a pit, or back to ground an enemy stands on are always fine.
    This is what keeps a 3.5-block leaper or a 16-block climber from kiting a swordsman forever, player or soldier,
    without forbidding the leap. Against enemy heroes the clock runs on their own links: ground an engaged hero
    can reach is not out of reach. While **escaping** (7.7), none of this applies: the hero may use any mobility,
    even putting an obstacle between itself and every pursuer that no pursuer can cross.

### 5.2 Raids, sieges and scouting: what heroes do with walls

Heroes cross walls (3.7); the AI decides what for. Soldiers still go through the gate or a breach, so a hero inside is
worth most when it helps them in.

**Today's walls** (`BaseLayout`): a 49x49 ring, 5 blocks high and 1 thick, crenellated on top (a merlon on every
second block, so a figure can stand on the top between merlons), with no wall walk and no stairs, and two gates of
wooden double doors that players and every NPC open (vanilla `OpenDoorGoal`, which shuts them behind it); monsters
cannot. So any faction base's gate is always a way out from inside, and soldiers need no help with an unchanged gate.
Players may change all of this (iron doors, blocked gateways, their own forts), and base styles may add walks and
towers later; the rules below read the blocks, not the layout.

**A hero on a raid** (it joins one only as §3.2 allows) is in the party's `f.raiders`, so the muster, the party size and
the director's fall-back share count it as one raider, as a soldier; its losses count the same way. It musters with
the party at the rally point (`GuardJob.raidTravel`), and only after the muster runs its own raid layer
(`hero/HeroRaid`, whatever its duty: the way in, one goal inside, then the heart with the party, `RAID_LOOT`'s looting
after a victory, the way home). The raid ends as today (the heart destroyed, the party beaten back by the fall-back
share, the 72000-tick limit) or by its leader's death (§5.6); a hero's goal never decides when it ends. When the
raid ends without a victory it goes home (an escape when it is in or near the target: 7.7); after a victory it loots
and goes home as the soldiers do.

**Scouting ahead.** A flyer hero on a raid scouts first: it circles the target at 8-24 blocks, over the walls when it
chooses, and its sightings go into the faction's report of that base (`ScoutJob.observe`'s count of the base's NPCs it
sees, plus the heroes it saw by name, so a leader it saw is known to be home), because the faction only knows what
someone saw. A scouting hero on its duty round (a climber up a rival's wall, a flyer over the yard, a leaper onto a
tower) may look inside the same way; the base's alarm sees it as any intruder (below), and its step aside or retreat is
an escape's movement (7.7).

**Entry** (`logic/hero/WallEntry`, pure; chosen after the muster, when the party is 24 blocks from the walls, PROPOSED).
A way over the wall is a whole route (up, over, and down inside within its safe fall, by a climb down or a glide) and
is chosen only when the hero also has a **way out** (below):

| Family | Way in |
|---|---|
| flyer | over the wall ring at the raid ceiling (2.3), landing inside near its goal |
| glider | from a height outside (a ridge, a tree, a tower, its own jump apex for ceilings II-III) when the glide carries over the wall; otherwise the gate with the soldiers. It leaves by the gate (a glide only goes down) |
| climber | up the wall face nearest its goal and down the inside, when the wall is within its current climb height (a 5-high base wall: a ceiling-II climber from level 1, a ceiling-I climber from level 13) |
| leaper, acrobat | onto the wall by a jump-up link (at most 3 blocks, so not today's 5-high walls: a lower or broken wall, a player's fort, or a gap link onto the top from higher ground outside) and down inside by a drop link; otherwise the gate |
| runner, charger, hover, swimmer | the gate or the breach at the head of the soldiers (a charger leads the breach) |

**The way out** (a hero never ends up shut inside). `WallEntry` picks a way over the wall only when one of these is
open from inside: the gate (doors and fence gates any NPC opens, a button or lever beside an iron door, or a gateway
blocked only by blocks it may break under `Reach`'s rule), or its own mobility (a flyer whose stamina, after the air
budget of the way in, regains its take-off threshold within 20 s on the ground; a climber whose climb height covers the
inside face; a jump-up link from inside). If the way out closes while it is inside (a player bricks up the gate, a
downing grounds a flyer), it tries its links, the gate, then breaking at player speed what `Reach` allows, then
`NpcNavigator`'s stall watchdog and stuck recovery, as any NPC. The campaign check counts heroes still inside a hostile
base 60 s after their raid ended (17.3, must be 0).

**Goal inside** (one per raid, by role and personality, PROPOSED; shown on its status line and in `sim.py`). Every goal
ends, so a hero never holds up its raid: on its own end condition, when the raid ends, or after 60 s without progress
(PROPOSED). Then the hero joins the party at the heart (`RAID_ASSAULT`, which it may strike like any raider).

1. **Open the way** (the default while soldiers of its party are held outside: a door they cannot open, an iron door
   worked from inside, a player's blocked gateway, or defenders holding the doorway; with an unchanged gate of wooden
   doors the soldiers need no help and the hero takes another goal): it goes to the inside of the gate and opens it as
   a player would (the door itself, a button or a lever; a player's blocking blocks broken at player speed under
   `Reach`'s may-break rule) or attacks the defenders holding it from behind. Done when every living raider is inside
   the walls box.
2. **Hunt the hero** (`heroes_first` focus temperaments, or a defending hero seen inside): it goes for the home hero or
   the leader. Done when that hero is dead or has escaped, or is not seen inside within 60 s. (No hero yields; a
   raider with honour 60 or more does not chase a home hero that escapes, §5.4.)
3. **The heart or the storehouse**: the raid's goal, the heart, by default. A greedy hero (g at least 0.65) goes for
   the storehouse first and takes one backpack of loot as `GuardJob`'s `RAID_LOOT` does (the storehouse of a base still
   standing: today's raiders loot only after the heart falls); then it carries the loot home (`RAID_RETURN`, counted in
   the party until it unloads at home, as looting raiders are today, so its going does not beat the raid back).
4. **Clear the archers** (marksman and zoner heroes): it shoots the defending archers and crossbowmen for its party
   (today's archers stand at the gate posts, the station and the plaza: `GuardJob.choosePost`), from the wall top,
   from inside or from the air within the air budget (5.1.4). Done when none is in its range.

**Defenders' response** (the defending base's AI; ordinary NPCs get no new movement):

- **Archers first at an airborne hero.** A new target goal for ranged roles (`AirborneHeroTargetGoal`, above
  today's `NearestAttackableTargetGoal`s): an enemy hero in the air (`DATA_LOCO` airborne, glide or fly) within bow
  range and in sight is taken over any other target, then an intruding hero on the walls or inside, then the rest. It
  keeps the hero as its target for at least 3 s (PROPOSED), so archers do not switch as a flyer lands and takes off.
  Each arrow adds air poise (2.4), so a volley of five downs a flyer.
- **Interception.** A hostile hero inside the walls or on them is an intruder for today's `defenceAlarm` (every 20
  ticks, no sight needed, hostile `FactionNpc`s included, so heroes already are): the alarm sounds and the two nearest
  free guards go for it; new: the home hero (any hero of the base that is home) joins them against an enemy hero.
  Anything at the heart still draws every guard. Today's alarm box is the walls plus 4 blocks, 12 blocks up and down
  from the centre; it grows to 16 up (PROPOSED), so a flyer at the raid ceiling over the 5-high wall counts.
  Swordsmen sent after an airborne hero wait under it out of reach and call the archers (`Pursuit`, 3.8) and strike
  when it lands. New on the approach: a base NPC with a line of sight to an enemy hero within 24 blocks raises the
  alarm before the hero reaches the box (a check in the same sweep, a few line-of-sight tests per second per base;
  `lookout` widens it), so only an approach out of sight reaches the wall unannounced.
- **Defending heroes**: flyers patrol over their own walls and meet an airborne intruder with their light ranged moves
  and dives (5.1.5); climbers reach the wall top and acrobats and leapers roofs and ledges at once (a wall walk or a
  tower where a base style has one).
- **Gates**: a gate an intruder opened is shut again by the nearest guard once the intruder is dead or gone (a small
  new step beside `OpenGateGoal`, which today closes only the gates an NPC opened itself; wooden doors close behind the
  NPC that passes, as vanilla's `OpenDoorGoal` does).
- The director counts a hero as one soldier of its role by heads and at its war power by strength (§3.2, §8.1: with
  boss health against soldiers it is worth about three to seven of them, capped by the soldier backbone). What its
  wall crossing adds on top is not priced into the attack odds (PROPOSED: the campaign checks of 17.3 count the raids
  a hero's entry decided, and `war.heroPowerPercent` is tuned if heroes make raids too easy).
- A defending hero does not make the heart safe by itself: any number of raiding heroes and soldiers may press it at
  once (no attack tokens, §6.2). A home hero of the escaping kind that does not stand for its home (`stands_for`, §6.13)
  breaks off at its threshold like anywhere else; one that stands for its home fights to the death there while the heart
  stands, and runs to another base of its faction once the heart is destroyed (the lost-base trigger of 7.7); a
  last-stand hero fights on where it is until it dies or its enemies are gone.

### 5.3 How an airborne hero is countered

The same numbers whoever uses them: a player, an NPC archer or crossbowman, or another hero's projectile (equal
treatment, §6). The first draft called this "how players counter"; defenders' archers already counted (2.4).

| Counter | Effect on an airborne hero |
|---|---|
| Bow | damage as usual, +20 air poise per arrow (5 arrows down a flyer) |
| Crossbow | +30 per bolt, +15 per Multishot bolt; Piercing works as usual |
| Thrown trident | +40; with Channeling in a thunderstorm the vanilla lightning downs it at once |
| Wind charge (1.21) | the vanilla wind burst pushes it, +35 air poise, and ends a glide |
| Snowball, egg | +10 (and a fire hero's `snowball` weak point) |
| Fishing rod | the vanilla hook pull draws it toward the angler, +15, ends a hover in place |
| Splash potion of Slowness | Slowness II or worse downs it; Slowness I halves its climb rate |
| Melee on a low flyer (within 3 blocks up) | +25 per hit, +40 on a critical hit |
| Shield | blocks a `dive` like any heavy move (it is never unblockable) |
| Cover | flyers need 4 blocks of headroom: tunnels, roofs and dense trees force them to walk |

## 7. Mobility in combat

### 7.1 One mode (the first draft had field mode and boss mode)

A hero fights the same way against a player, NPC soldiers, another hero or monsters (§6.1), so there is one set of
mobility rules for a fight. In NPC wars mobility does what it does against a player: leapers and acrobats close over
broken ground, climbers reach archers on ledges and walls, flyers and gliders cross walls and harass within the air
budget (5.1.4, 5.2). Air poise counts NPC arrows as it counts a player's. What the first draft allowed only in "field
mode" is gone from fights (the charger's knockback bonus, the 10 s air budget and the 10-block ceiling once an enemy
has engaged it), and what it allowed only against players holds against everyone (the reach clock, the altitude
ceiling from the highest engaged enemy). No numbers of the kit change. How a losing fight ends is in 7.7: an
escaping-kind hero leaves at its threshold, a last-stand hero never does.

### 7.2 In a fight: what stays, what turns off

- Off: runner travel speed and sprint, the charger's traversal charge and knockback bonus (5.1.10).
- On: jumps, air jumps, wall kicks, climbing, gliding, flight with the air budget and the reach rule, hover, swimming,
  footing, step height, safe fall.
- Mobility is positioning between moves: the move selector (§6.3) runs as before; a hero never starts a move in the
  air except a `dive` or the light ranged moves of 5.1.5. It reaches an enemy on a pillar, in a pen or behind a wall
  at once (3.7) and comes back within 4 s from ground no engaged enemy can reach (5.1.15).

### 7.3 Gap closers and the `dive` form

`leap`, `dash` and `charge` (v1 and §6.5) are unchanged and remain the main gap closers. Mobility adds one form:

| Form | Affects | Parameters (range, default) | Implementation | Weight (cooldown floor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dive` | enemies within `radius` of the landing point | `range` 4-12 (8), `radius` 1-3 (2), `from` `air` (flyers, gliders) or `apex` (leapers, acrobats: from the top of their own jump) | class `heavy`; the landing decal grows over the wind-up; the hero is pushed along a straight line to the marker (velocity, stopped by blocks, never into hazards); ends on the ground with at least 1.0 s of recovery (a touch-and-go string of up to 3 dives in one aerial window plays a 0.5 s touch-down between dives and the full recovery after the last, as followup strings do: §6.3) | as `leap` + 1 |

The validator refuses `dive` for a hero without an airborne trait, `from: air` without flight or gliding, and an
`unblockable` dive. A hero with an airborne trait has its `dive` from level 1, whatever its ceiling (1.1).

### 7.4 Aerial windows

A flyer whose `mobility.aerial.phases` includes the current phase may take to the air in a fight, whoever the enemy:

```
NEUTRAL (ground) --take-off (0.6 s tell)--> AIR (at most 8 s, at most 6 blocks up)
   AIR: light ranged moves (at most 3), or wind up a dive or rain
   AIR --dive--> touch-and-go (0.5 s) --> AIR ...  (at most `dives` per window)
   AIR --last dive or landing--> GROUND recovery (at least 1.0 s: the punish window, +15% damage taken as in §6.9)
   GROUND for at least 6 s (4 s in phase 3) before the next take-off
```

- Take-off is chosen by the selector like a move (weight from the hero's file, default 4) when the phase allows it,
  the gap is over and the stamina threshold is met; the boss bar shows nothing new (the take-off animation and sound
  are the tell).
- Phase transitions happen on the ground: a hero that crosses a threshold in the air lands first (0.4 s), then plays
  the transition.
- Default phases: none for gliders; for flyers phase 2 onward (all phases for legendary flyers). An author may give a
  grounded flyer no aerial phases at all (it then flies in fights only for reach flights).
- Outside its aerial phases a flyer takes off only for a reach flight (3.7): to a target no walk or other link
  reaches, with no moves in the air and the same budget, reach clock and ground time.

### 7.5 Conditions, reactions, arena awareness

- New conditions: `self_airborne`, `self_flying`, `self_climbing`, `self_stamina_below` (percent), `target_flying`
  (anyone in the air on wings: a player gliding with an elytra or an airborne hero), `target_in_water`.
- Default reaction for flyers (overridable): `target_drawing_bow` while airborne -> land or dive (60%, cooldown 8 s),
  so a drawn bow is a real threat.
- Arena samples (§6.12) add: take-off headroom (under 4 blocks: no aerial window), ledges reachable by the hero's jumps
  or climbing (it may take any of them, and comes back within 4 s from one no engaged enemy can reach: 5.1.15), and
  climbable faces. The `low_ceiling` condition also forbids take-off.
- Elytra (only players wear them, so this is the one target state an NPC never has): `target_flying` lets anti-air
  moves answer a gliding player as they answer an airborne hero; heroes never chase a gliding target higher than
  their own ceilings.

### 7.6 Stagger, escape and death in the air

A stagger in the air is a downing (2.4). An escaping-kind hero that drops under its threshold in the air escapes from
where it is (7.7): it does not land first. A hero killed in the air dies there and falls; its revival is unchanged.
There is no yield to glide down for (`HEROES_V2.md` 6.13).

### 7.7 Escape (the owner's answers of 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13)

"If the hero tries to escape, its ok if they make it impossible for the catcher" (2026-11-12), and "a hero shouldnt
yield. they either try to escape or fight until die depending of what they want" (2026-11-13). The first draft made
escape the exception to a yield: a hero knelt unless it "refused". There is no yield now, so escape is simply what a
hero of the **escaping kind** does when a fight is lost, in any fight, and a hero of the **last-stand kind** never
does it. Which kind a hero is, where an escaping hero stands anyway (its home, beside its leader) and the authored
override are in §6.13. An escaping hero may use any mobility, even where no pursuer can follow; a fighting hero wants
to win and comes back (5.1.15). The rule that keeps a fight from turning into an endless chase
(`logic/hero/EscapeRules`, pure; numbers PROPOSED):

**Triggers** (for a hero of the escaping kind that no situation rule of §6.13 holds; who the enemy is never matters:
a player, NPC soldiers, another hero and monsters are the same)

| Trigger | When | Refuge |
|---|---|---|
| Low health | in any fight, its health drops under its escape threshold `flee_at` (8-15% of its max health: a third of its `retreat_below`, kept inside its last phase, §6.13) | its home base; when the fight is at its home base, the nearest other standing base of its faction, and with none the wild (below) |
| Lost raid | its raid falls back (beaten back by the fall-back share of §5.5, or its leader fell or escaped: §5.6) while the hero is in or near the target (its walls box plus 16 blocks) or fighting; elsewhere it walks home with the party | its home base |
| Lost base | the heart of the base it is defending is destroyed (a `home` stand ends with the heart) | the nearest standing base of its faction; with none the wild, and then the elimination rule of §5.6 |

A **last-stand hero** has no trigger. Under the same health it has its last-stand beat (§6.13) and fights on. When
its raid falls back or its base's heart falls, it finishes the fight it is in (until its engaged enemies are dead or
gone, or it is) and only then walks home or to the nearest standing base. Two triggers of the first draft are gone:
the "refused yield" (honour 30 or less, or a cautious leader: there is nothing to refuse) and "a leader saving itself"
(a leader is an escaping or a last-stand hero like any other, and one whose loyalty holds it at its capital dies at
its heart).

**Refuge.** The refuge is never the base it flees. A hero whose home base has fallen takes the nearest standing base
of its faction as its home (`HeroState.baseId` moves there). **The wild**: with no base to go to, it runs until no
pursuer has been within 48 blocks for 10 s, keeps at least 64 blocks from the fight it left for its stay-away time
(PROPOSED), then walks home if its home still stands. This also answers the hero that runs from a fight at its own
home base: another base of its faction if there is one, else the wild.

**Not escapes.** A scout's step aside or retreat (`ScoutJob`: enemy soldiers seen, badly hurt under 40% among
monsters, a monster in the way; once an enemy has engaged it, it is in a fight like any other) uses an escape's
movement (below) but none of the rest: no boss bar, no stay-away, no window spent, no morale, no line; it goes out
of sight and on with its round, or home when retreating. A raid that ends in victory or by the time limit sends the
hero home as the soldiers go. And no hero "withdraws" as soldiers do (`WithdrawGoal`: step back, eat, return at
60%): that goal is not a hero's (§3.5).

**While escaping**

- Any mobility: links, climbs, chimneys, glides, flight with the stamina limit only (no air budget, no altitude ceiling
  but the travel one, 2.3), deep water for swimmers, lava for fire-proof heroes, risky gaps with a wide landing (3.3).
  Rules 5.1.4, 5.1.6, 5.1.9 and 5.1.15 do not apply; no teleporting (5.1.1: stuck recovery is never used to get away),
  air poise and downing (5.1.7), no carrying (5.1.12) and the hazard rules (5.1.13) still do.
- It starts no moves and no basic attacks; it runs. **Cornered** (no path or link away from its pursuers for 3 s,
  PROPOSED), it fights back where it stands and runs again as soon as a way opens.
- **Pursuers** are the enemies engaged in the fight it fled, of every kind, and anything whose target it is; a raid it
  fled is left, and it is out of `f.raiders`. A player follows as far as they can; soldiers as far as their leash and
  their legs go (`Pursuit` is unchanged: no links, no flight); enemy heroes with their own mobility within their
  aggression leash, except that a hero with honour 60 or more lets it go (§5.4).
- **The fight ends for it** when the escape starts: the bar shows `★ Name · fled` for 3 s and goes, its health keeps
  its share, and no intro or new fight starts while it escapes. It stays a boss (one mode, §6.1): the per-hit cap,
  poise and weak points do not switch off. So an escape is stopped by a stagger (2.5 s where it is), by shooting a
  flyer or a glider down (2.4), by cornering it, or by landing the two to four blows it has left.
- The escape ends when it is inside its refuge's walls, or when no pursuer has been within 48 blocks for 10 s; it then
  travels to its refuge as usual.

**After an escape**

- **Stays away** for `heroes.escapeAwaySeconds` (300 s) and until it is above 50% health: it rests at its refuge (the
  out-of-combat regeneration of §6.10), does not re-engage those it fled and does not rejoin the raid or fight it
  left. Attacked there or on the way, it defends itself (and its heart); that is a new fight.
- **Once in 10 minutes** (`heroes.escapeCooldownSeconds`, 600 s, counted from the escape's start): within that window
  no trigger starts an escape. Under its threshold again it makes a last stand (§6.13), so whoever follows it home
  or catches it on the way gets a fight to the death. A lost raid inside the window sends it home with the party on
  foot, a lost base to the nearest standing base the same way.
- Then it goes back to its duty (a leader to `court`).
- **Loot and rewards**: an escape is neither a death nor a defeat. Nothing drops, nobody gets XP orbs, a crest or a
  token fragment (§6.13), and it keeps its gear and whatever it carries (raid loot included). `HeroState` counts
  `escapes`. Killed during the escape, it has died: the usual rewards and drops, to whoever killed it.
- **War council**: while it is away its war power is in no raid party and in no base's defence but its refuge's, at
  half until it is above half health (§8.1).
- **Morale** (milestone 2, `research-systems.md` 2.8): its home base -4, a leader's escape -6 at every base of its
  faction (against -8 for a hero killed and -20 for a leader killed); nothing for the side it fled.
- **Lines**: its `escape` line as it goes (temperament fallback banks cover it), and a chat line to the players
  within 32 blocks: "Name fled".
- Its home base is gone: the nearest standing base of its faction; no base left: the elimination rule of §5.6 (an
  exiled free hero).

**Why it cannot loop.** An escape is followed by at least 300 s away and above 50% health, during which it starts
nothing against those it fled, and by a 600 s window in which nothing escapes: a hero caught again inside it fights
to the death. After the window a new escape needs a new lost fight. The refuge is never the base it flees, so
escape, return, escape cannot cycle within a fight, and an escape always ends: at the refuge, 10 s clear of
pursuers, or by its death. `EscapeRulesTest` walks these sequences.

# Part 2: Work mastery

## 9. Principles

- **The action stays player-like**: a block still takes ticks to break (`BlockWork` with the hero's tool, Efficiency,
  Haste and the off-ground rule), furnaces and smokers are real block entities with real fuel, the enchanting table and
  the anvil work as for players, fish and crops come from vanilla loot tables, trades are the villagers' own offers.
- **The hero is better at it**: faster, carries more, reaches further, wears its tools less (proficiency, 10), and its
  primary talent adds a much stronger personal effect when the hero does that work itself (specialty, 11).
- **Bonuses are hero bonuses**: some create a little more than vanilla would (an extra loot roll, a second dish, a
  second recruit). They are capped (13), shown on the hero sheet, and logged by `sim.py`.
- **Talents stay base-wide** (§7.2: auras, ranges, odds for the whole base, strongest copy counts); specialties are
  personal and never stack across heroes.
- **The duty decides**: proficiency applies to the job the hero works as its duty (and a leader's `court` visits to a
  workplace); the specialty applies when that job is its talent's job (`TALENT_DUTY`; in the roster every hero's duty
  matches its primary talent by construction).

## 12. How it plugs into the jobs

### 12.1 The work API on `FactionNpc`

Jobs stop reading affixes and constants directly for these values and call small methods instead; `FactionNpc`
returns exactly today's numbers (a unit test and the static GameTest `workersUnchangedByMastery` hold that), and
`HeroEntity` overrides them from `logic/hero/WorkMastery` (pure):

| Method | Worker returns | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `workSpeed()` | 1 + Diligence | `BlockWork` (as a `block_break_speed` modifier for heroes), `Crafting`, `BuilderJob` ticks per block, `HaulerJob`, `CookingJob` loading |
| `backpackSlots()` | 18 | `Inv` helpers, every "backpack full" check; the hero's container is created with 36 slots and only the usable ones are offered |
| `workReach()` | the job's constant | jobs that reach for blocks (mining faces, logs, crops, building) |
| `wearMultiplier()`, `hungerMultiplier()` | Thrifty, Hardy | `FactionNpc` wear and `NpcHunger` |
| `extraRollChance(kind)` | the affix's chance | `MinerJob.prospect`, `FarmerJob`, `FishermanJob`, `WoodcutterJob` regrowth |
| `virtualLevel(enchantment, kind)` | 0 | 12.2 |

### 12.2 Virtual enchantment levels

NeoForge fires `GetEnchantmentLevelEvent` whenever gameplay asks an item's enchantment level (`getEnchantmentLevel`,
`getAllEnchantments`), never for tooltips; in 21.1.209 `EnchantmentHelper`'s level lookups and its iteration over an
item's enchantments (loot functions, fishing luck and lure, equipment levels) go through those two methods (checked in
the sources). `HeroWork.lootScope(hero, kind)` opens a scope (a server-thread
thread-local, closed in `finally`) around the job's loot call; inside it, the handler adds the specialty's levels to the
hero's main-hand item, recognised by identity (`event.getStack() == hero.getMainHandItem()`), so no other stack read
in the same call (a worker's tool, a dropped item, the victim's gear) is touched:

- block drops (`Block.getDrops` in `BlockWork.breakNow`): Fortune for surveyors, foresters and harvest heroes. Not in
  `dropExtra`: an extra loot roll (affix, aura) rolls the table with the tool's real enchantments only, so virtual
  levels and extra rolls never multiply each other (13);
- fishing: `FishermanJob` already asks `EnchantmentHelper.getFishingLuckBonus` and `getFishingTimeReduction` for the
  rod, so the scope sits around those two calls (Luck of the Sea and Lure);
- kills (`renown`): the scope opens in a `LivingDeathEvent` handler at the lowest priority that skips cancelled events
  (so it opens only for a death that goes ahead) when the killer is the hero, and closes in `LivingDropsEvent` (fired
  at the end of `dropAllDeathLoot`), so the vanilla Looting functions of the victim's loot table see it. Because those
  are two events and not one call, the scope is also cleared at the end of every server tick: a death another mod
  cancels later cannot leave it open.

The item itself never changes: no enchantment shows or persists. Levels never exceed the vanilla maximum together with
the tool's own (Fortune III, Looting III, Luck of the Sea III, Lure III), and Fortune is never added to a Silk Touch
tool. If the event ever proves unsuitable, the fallback for blocks and fishing passes a copy of the tool carrying the
levels to `Block.getDrops` and the loot parameters.

### 12.3 Furnaces, timers, prices, recruits

- **Furnace progress**: `AbstractFurnaceBlockEntity.cookingProgress` is package-private, so an access transformer
  exposes it (the decal setters of §6.4 already need one). While the hero tends a furnace (within 4 blocks, its job at
  that furnace) each tick adds the bonus as a fraction to an accumulator and moves `cookingProgress` on, never past
  `cookingTotalTime - 1`; the vanilla tick finishes the item and pays the XP. `litTime` (fuel) is untouched, so each
  item burns less fuel.
- **Timers**: crafting, building, sorting and loading timers divide by `workSpeed()`.
- **Prices**: `MerchantOffer.addToSpecialPriceDiff(-k)` with k = floor(cut x the offer's base cost), as `TraderJob`
  does for Haggler today, with the combined cap of 11.1.
- **Second dish, second recruit**: a roll when the output is collected or the payment made; the extra is one more of
  the same item or recruit.
- **Enchanting offers**: `EnchanterJob` already uses the vanilla table logic; the hero evaluates the three offers it
  would see (scored by enchantment and level) and rerolls by enchanting a book with the cheapest offer, which changes
  the seed as it does for a player.

## 13. Caps and balance checks (PROPOSED)

| Cap | Value |
|---|---|
| Work speed from Diligence, proficiency, specialty and talents together | at most 2.0x a worker with the same gear (Efficiency, Haste and beacons are outside the cap, as for players) |
| Extra loot roll per action (affix + aura + specialty) | at most 75%, never more than one extra roll |
| Virtual enchantment levels | the vanilla maximum with the tool's own; none with Silk Touch |
| Virtual levels and extra rolls on the same drop | never both: a drop takes whichever has the larger expected gain (Fortune I/II/III on ores 1.33x / 1.75x / 2.2x, an extra roll 1 + its chance), so one ore block yields at most 2.2x the plain table; an extra roll uses the tool's real enchantments only (12.2) |
| Wear, hunger | at least 40% and 50% of a worker's |
| Backpack | at most 36 slots |
| Price cut | at most 40% |
| Furnace speed | at most 2x vanilla |
| Output | `sim.py` prints per hero and day the blocks broken, items produced, catches, trades and recruits beside the same-job worker average; a level-20 hero above `work.outputCap` (3x a level-1 worker of the same job and the same tool, counting the job's own products: ores for a miner, logs and saplings for a woodcutter, crops, catches, smelted items, dishes, trades, recruits) is flagged in the campaign check |

**Why there is no runaway.** Every multiplier is per action and capped above, and none compounds over time: talents
count base-wide only once (the strongest copy), specialties are personal and never stack across heroes, extra recruits
and bred animals stay within the population and pen caps, prices within the villagers' own restock, furnace speed
within 2x. The worst case the caps allow, worked: a level-20 surveyor with a plain iron pickaxe breaks blocks at most
2x as fast, but a miner's day is mostly walking, hauling, eating and sleeping (about 40% breaking, PROPOSED, measured
by `sim.py`), so it breaks about 1 / (0.6 + 0.4 / 2) = 1.25x the blocks; at most 2.2x per ore block gives about 2.75x
the ores of a level-1 miner with the same pickaxe, under the 3x flag. (Allowing virtual Fortune and an extra roll on
the same drop, as the first draft did, gave 2.2 x 1.75 = 3.85x per block and about 4.8x per day: the flag would have
fired on the design's own numbers.) A flag therefore means a stacking bug, not a strong hero.
