Faction Clash - artifacts
Items only heroes can wear | 61 artifacts in five tiers | crafted, upgraded, grown, looted, stolen
Status: designed, not in the game yet (kit version 4, 2026-11-18: reviewed again, see CHANGED
AFTER THE SECOND REVIEW at the end; version 3, the same day, rewritten to your answers;
docs/heroes-v2/ARTIFACTS.md). All numbers are first values, tuned after play.
====================================================================================================

WHAT CHANGED IN VERSION 3 (your answers of 2026-11-18)
- Slots: a hero has one artifact slot at level 1 and gets one more every five levels, five from
  level 20.
- Strength: an artifact gives the same numbers on every hero, at every level. It does not grow
  with its hero.
- Choice: each hero decides for itself to wear an artifact, swap it in, or leave it for someone
  else (give it away, ask for one, refuse one). The leader only asks; nobody is ordered.
- Tinkering: a new worker, the artificer, grows out of the blacksmith. With heroes who have the
  new tinkerer talent, it upgrades artifacts at a new block, the Artificer's Bench.
- Growth: eight new artifacts grow stronger from what their hero does (blood, ore, nights, ...).
- Research: a few upgrades and three new artifacts need the faction's research. Research comes
  with the faction labs later, so these stay locked until then. Nothing vanilla is ever locked.
- You: you can offer an artifact to a hero or ask for one. The hero decides. Pins are gone.
- The two signature artifacts (with the boss heroes' revision, the same day): the Wither's Hollow
  Star grows, and the Ender Dragon's Spirecore can be upgraded once the faction has the research.

WHAT ARTIFACTS ARE
Your idea, in our own words: artifacts are named items that make a hero stronger at something,
built the way a MOBA builds items (cheap pieces first, then finished items with a special power),
but every name, look and effect is our own and made from Minecraft things: flint, lapis, copper,
blaze rods, echo shards, shulker shells, the archetypes and the eight elements.
- Each artifact is a normal item with its own name and a 16x16 icon in the vanilla style. Its name
  is coloured by tier: yellow for components, aqua for advanced, purple for greater and rare finds,
  gold for the two signature artifacts. Rare finds and signatures shimmer like enchanted items.
- An upgraded artifact shows its level after its name, like an enchantment: Kiln Heart II.
- Hovering over one shows what it gives, its special power, what it is made from and a line of
  lore, for example:

      Kiln Heart II
      Advanced artifact · fire
       +15% fire damage
       +8% ability power
       +13% fire resistance
      Banked Embers (primed): an enemy its moves hit smoulders for 6 s;
      its next basic hit on it deals 2 fire and sets it alight for 3 s.
      Works only for a hero wearing it in an artifact slot.
      Next: upgrade III at an Artificer's Bench (needs Artifice 1).
      Built from: Lapis Lens + Block of Coal
      "Banked coals that never quite go out."

WHO GETS THEIR POWER
- Only a hero, and only from its artifact slots.
- Anyone else can carry them with no effect at all: you, a soldier, a hauler, a trader, a chest, a
  hopper, even a hero's own backpack. You cannot wear one, and neither can an ordinary NPC: players
  and NPCs are treated exactly the same, as everywhere in the hero design.
- What an artifact's power does to others is the same for everyone: a slow, a heal or a push works
  on a player exactly as on an NPC soldier (and an ally heal reaches you too if you are a member of
  the hero's faction).

SLOTS: ONE MORE EVERY FIVE LEVELS
    Hero level    1-4   5-9   10-14   15-19   20-30
    Slots          1     2      3       4       5
- Any artifact fits any slot. A hero cannot wear two copies of the same artifact.
- Not more for rarer heroes, leaders or talents: rarity and leading already give more health.
- Five, not six: five strong artifacts already reach most of the ceilings below.
- A new slot opens at the level-up; the hero fills it the next time it chooses.
- The Wither and the Ender Dragon join at level 1, so their own artifact fills their one slot.

FIXED STRENGTH
- An artifact's numbers are its own. Kiln Heart II gives +15% fire damage on a level-1 hero and on
  a level-30 hero alike. (The first version gave 60% at level 1 and full power at level 20; that
  is gone.)
- A "+10% attack" line is a tenth of that hero's own attack, so a stronger hero gets more out of
  it, as a Speed potion speeds a fast runner and a slow one by the same share. The artifact itself
  never changes.
- Only two things make an artifact stronger, and both stay on the item wherever it goes: its
  upgrade level (TINKERING below) and, for the eight growing artifacts, its growth.

CEILINGS
Each kind of bonus has a ceiling for the whole build, so artifacts can never pile one bonus
sky-high [health +20%, attack +15%, ability power +20%, armour +4, shorter cooldowns 20%, attack
speed +15%, speed +8%, energy gain +25%, poise +25%, element damage +20%, one element's
resistance +25%, all resistances +10%, drain 6%, heal power +20%, resolve 25%, knockback
resistance +20, stamina +25%, work speed +15%, critical damage +25%]. The hero sheet marks a
bonus that has reached its ceiling. With five slots the ceilings matter more: a fifth artifact
mostly adds its power and the bonuses the others lack.
In a fight, everything artifacts heal their own hero is held to 10% of its health in any 30 s, so
a hero cut down by a crowd of soldiers cannot out-heal them.

WHAT AN ARTIFACT CAN GIVE
  Health                   more maximum health (the boss health too)
  Attack                   harder basic hits and weapon-based moves
  Ability power            stronger moves, heals and shields
  Armour                   plain armour points; best against many small hits
  Recharge                 shorter cooldowns on the hero's moves (never shorter wind-ups)
  Attack speed             faster basic swings and shots (never faster moves)
  Speed                    faster on foot, in fights too
  Energy gain              the ultimate fills sooner
  Poise                    harder to stagger
  Element damage           more damage of the hero's own element
  Resistance               less damage of one element, or of all eight
  Drain                    heals the hero by a share of the damage it deals
  Heal power               stronger heals and shields it gives
  Resolve                  shorter stuns, freezes, slows, tethers and silences on the hero
  Knockback resistance     harder to push
  Stamina                  longer flights and glides (not more air time in a fight)
  Work speed               faster at its job at home
  Critical damage          harder jump-critical hits

WHAT ARTIFACTS NEVER CHANGE
The boss rules that keep a hero fight fair, whatever the upgrades or growth. No hit on a hero takes
more than 12% of its health; no wind-up, ground mark or warning gets shorter; the waits between
moves stay; a stun is never longer and never chains; no hit from a hero goes over the 40-point
ceiling; nothing saves a hero from a killing blow; no artifact lets a hero fly, teleport or break
blocks, and none is an explosive. An artifact's attack speed speeds basic swings and shots only.
An artifact that pushes (Updraft, Ram Strike) follows the same rules as any hero's push, so, as
you allowed, it may send someone off a cliff or into lava. No artifact pushes farther than the
Ender Dragon's Wing Gust (knockback 3, about 6 blocks on flat ground).

HOW TO GET THEM
Crafting, the player-like way. The faction's blacksmith does it in its workshop, from real
materials, until the faction has an artificer, who then takes it over. You can do every step
yourself with the vanilla screens:
  1. Components at a crafting table (flint, gold, lapis, copper, iron, tuff, deepslate, amethyst,
     feathers, a spider eye, a glistering melon slice...).
  2. An Artificer's Sigil at a crafting table: a gold ingot, 4 lapis lazuli and 4 redstone make 2.
  3. Finished artifacts at a smithing table: the Sigil as the template, a component or an artifact
     as the base, and a second component or a material as the addition.
  4. Waking at an anvil: what comes off the smithing table is dormant and does nothing until it is
     woken with 2 lapis lazuli and experience levels (5 for advanced, 10 for greater). The faction
     pays with the experience its people really gathered, and the anvil wears out as vanilla's does.
The blacksmith only works on artifacts when its own orders are done (tools, weapons, armour and
repairs come first), keeps some gold, lapis and diamonds back, and makes at most one finished
artifact or waking a day at each base. The forgemaster talent makes waking cheaper.

Materials follow the world. Components, advanced and most growing artifacts come from today's
economy. Greater artifacts each need something from farther away: a breeze rod or a wind charge
(trial chambers), netherite scrap, a blaze rod or a ghast tear (the Nether), an echo shard (an
ancient city), a totem of undying (a woodland mansion), a shulker shell (the End), a slime block,
a nautilus shell (fishermen catch them). So builds grow as a faction explores.

Rare finds cannot be made. They lie in vanilla chests and bosses: an ancient city (Hushstone), a
bastion (Bastion Hoard-Ring), an end city (Chorus-Bloom Pendant), a woodland mansion or an evoker
(Fangspeaker Charm), an elder guardian (Elderscale Lens), a pillager outpost (Steward's Seal), and
any of them from a destroyed Hollow lair's heart. You and the NPCs find them the same way.

Signature artifacts: the Wither hero and the Ender Dragon hero each come with one of their own
(designed with those two heroes, docs/heroes-v2/BOSS_HEROES.md). Any hero can wear one for its
basic power; only its own hero gets its full bond (extra bonuses, a stronger power and changes to
two of her own moves). Only one of each exists in a world, and if one is destroyed (thrown into the
void) it comes back to its hero when she next returns, as new. Each signature gets stronger in one
way: the Wither's Hollow Star grows as its wearer withers enemies, and is never upgraded; the Ender
Dragon's Spirecore can be upgraded at the bench (with research, like a rare find), and never grows.
Its own hero never gives it up: she keeps it until she dies or leaves. If a hero of her faction
picks it up after her death, it counts as lent by her, and she asks for it back.

Villagers never buy or sell artifacts. Later, when factions make treaties, an artifact can be part
of a tribute or a peace offer.

TINKERING: UPGRADES
"Some npcs or hero with can tinker with artefacts may upgrade the artifact."
- Who: the artificer, a new worker. It grows out of the blacksmith: once the base has its bench
  and its blacksmith reaches level 5, that blacksmith becomes the artificer and a new blacksmith
  is hired. A hero whose job is artificer (heroes with the new tinkerer talent) does the same work,
  better. You can use any bench yourself, for the same price.
- Where: the Artificer's Bench, a new block for the workshop (iron, a lapis block, amethyst, a
  smithing table and polished deepslate). It works like a brewing stand: put the artifact and the
  materials in, press Begin, it takes the materials and the experience levels, then works by
  itself for a minute or three. A tinkerer standing at it makes it faster.
- What: upgrade level II makes every number on the artifact a quarter larger, level III half
  larger (still under the ceilings), and at III its special power is tuned: a fifth shorter
  cooldown, or a fifth stronger where it has none. A growing artifact is not made stronger by an
  upgrade; instead it can grow one step further.
- Components are never upgraded (they are built into bigger artifacts). Of the two signatures,
  only Spirecore is (as a rare find, with research); Hollow Star grows instead.
- An upgrade never fails and never breaks the item. Its price is the cost:
    advanced or growing to II    2 lapis + its own material                   5 levels    60 s
    advanced or growing to III   4 lapis, a diamond + more of its material    8 levels    90 s
    greater to II                4 lapis, a diamond + its own material       10 levels   120 s
    greater to III               4 lapis, a netherite scrap + more material  15 levels   180 s
    rare find or Spirecore       as greater, Spirecore with end crystals (needs research)
  "Its own material" is in the catalogue below (Kiln Heart: blocks of coal, 2 for II, 4 for III).
- Every level III, and every upgrade of a rare find, needs research (RESEARCH below).
- Smithing an upgraded artifact into its bigger form starts the new one again at level I; the
  tooltip warns you, and the factions never do it.
- The tinkerer talent (I / II / III): upgrades at its base cost 1 / 2 / 3 fewer levels and less
  time; at the bench itself it works 25 / 40 / 55% faster and sometimes saves a material.

GROWING ARTIFACTS
"An artifact may become stronger of its in the artifact design, like an artifact that needs blood
to get stronger."
- Eight artifacts grow from what their hero does while wearing them. Carried in a backpack or a
  chest they count nothing.
- Each grows in steps. The first step comes quickly; the fifth takes fifteen times as long.
- A new one starts as weak as a component (with a small special power). Each step adds a bonus.
  It can grow three steps at upgrade I, four at II and five at III. Full, it is as strong as a
  greater artifact of the same upgrade level.
- Its growth is kept for good: when its hero dies, when it is stolen, traded or given away, the
  next hero starts from where it was. It never shrinks. Only destroying the item loses it.
- No hero goes looking for fights or mines just to feed one: it grows from what the hero does
  anyway. Blood from you is the same as blood from a soldier.
    BLOODGLASS AMPOULE   grows with the blood its hero draws from living foes (not undead)
    VEINSEEKER GEODE     grows with every ore block its hero mines
    VIGIL LANTERN        grows with every night its hero spends awake on watch
    SKYROAD PENNANT      grows with every block its hero flies or glides
    GRAVEWARD BELL       grows with every undead its hero lays to rest
    RIVAL'S CORD         grows with every enemy hero or boss its hero outlasts
    SCARBARK AMULET      grows with every fight its hero lives through at the edge of death
    HEARTHROOT SEED      grows with every crop, sapling and animal pair its hero tends
  (Their numbers are in the catalogue below.)

RESEARCH (TECH LEVELS)
"Some artifacts may nees tech level to be built and/or upgraded."
- When the faction labs come (the roadmap's technology phase), factions research a line called
  Artifice. Artifice 1 opens upgrade level III. Artifice 2 opens the three engineered artifacts
  and the upgrades of rare finds.
- Everything else here needs no research, ever: components, the Sigil, advanced, growing and
  greater artifacts, waking, upgrade level II, the bench, the artificer and the talent. Nothing
  vanilla is ever locked.
- Research is checked only at the bench, against the faction of whoever uses it: you use your
  faction's research.
- Until the labs exist, the researched steps stay locked (the bench shows them greyed). A server
  setting can open them for anyone who wants them sooner.

HOW HEROES CHOOSE
"Heroes decide if they want to use, replace, or leave for someone else the artifact."
- Each hero chooses for itself, at a base and out of a fight (never in the middle of one):
    use       it takes the artifact it likes best for an empty slot, from its backpack or the
              storehouse;
    swap      it replaces one it wears when another is clearly better (fickle heroes swap more,
              careful ones less; at most once a day unless something new reaches it, so no
              artifact goes back and forth);
    give      it gives an artifact to another hero who needs it much more, if it is generous
              enough (sociable heroes give, greedy ones keep);
    ask       it asks another hero for an artifact it needs much more (bold, greedy or sociable
              heroes ask; a lone wolf never does);
    refuse    it hands back an offer it does not want.
- What a hero likes comes from its kit, role and element (a healer wants heal power, a flyer
  stamina, a fire hero the fire focus), what its scouts saw of the enemy (the right resistance),
  and its personality: a cautious hero likes health and armour, an aggressive one damage, a
  sociable one powers that help allies, a greedy one gold and work artifacts, an ambitious one
  the rarest and the upgraded, and a patient one is happy to grow an artifact.
- Temperaments add their touch: a tsundere refuses your first gift and takes the second ("I-it's
  not like I wanted it!"), a proud noble turns up her nose at components, a perfectionist upgrades
  what she wears before trying anything new, a glutton loves the torcs and the Bloodglass Ampoule,
  a melancholic never gives away what a fallen friend wore, an honourable hero always gives back
  what it was lent.
- Friends and rivals count: heroes give more readily to friends and never to a sworn enemy.
- When two heroes want the same artifact from the store, each decides whether to insist or give
  way; if both insist, the one who asked first gets it, and the other asks the workshop for one.
- The leader only wishes. A greedy leader (or a tyrant) wishes "the best to me", an ambitious one
  "the best to our strongest", a fair-minded one "to whoever has least". Each hero weighs the
  wish by its loyalty and honour against its own greed and ambition: a loyal, honest hero mostly
  follows it, a greedy schemer mostly does not. Nobody is forced.
- The workshop makes what the heroes wish for (each keeps up to two wishes: an artifact or an
  upgrade). There the leader does decide the order, because the workers are ordinary NPCs.
- Haulers carry artifacts between bases; the hero who gets one still decides.

YOU AND YOUR FACTION'S HEROES
- Offer: sneak-use an artifact on one of your faction's heroes (or use the Offer slot on its
  sheet), out of a fight and close by. The hero decides at once: it wears it (and hands you what
  it took off), keeps it, or gives it back with a line.
- Ask: the Ask button beside an artifact the hero holds, saying whom it is for. For another hero,
  the hero weighs it exactly as if that hero had asked (who needs it more, how generous it is),
  and your good standing helps a little. For yourself, it gives you only what it does not need.
  What you lent it, an honourable hero always gives back. So you cannot strip a hero of what it
  wants: the heroes decide, as you asked.
- The storehouse: you can put artifacts in it for the heroes, and take from it like any chest.
- No more pins: nothing you put on a hero is locked there. To move an artifact from one hero to
  another, ask the first for the second and offer it to the second.
- These are the same rules a hero uses with another hero; only the screen is yours.

WHEN A HERO DIES
- Everything it wears drops where it died, like a player's inventory, with its backpack. Its kit
  weapon still vanishes and comes back with it, as before. A hero that escapes keeps everything.
- The drops keep their upgrade level and growth.
- The drops never despawn. Fire, lava (they float out of it), explosions and cactus do not destroy
  them; only the void and commands do. A small coloured sparkle shows where they lie.
- Whoever gets there first takes them: the side that holds the ground picks them up before any
  other loot, a passing hero, you, or even a zombie (which drops it again when killed). Soldiers
  carry them home to their storehouse; a hero wears what it wants and stores the rest.
- So a faction can recover its fallen hero's artifacts, and an enemy can steal them and wear them.
  When a faction knows where its hero fell, it may send the revived hero, or two soldiers, to
  fetch them, unless the place is next to an enemy base.
- The hero comes back 10 minutes later as always, with empty slots, and claims from its store.
- Raiders who loot a storehouse take artifacts first (and break a bench with one in it), and a
  greedy hero on a raid goes for the storehouse while the base still stands.

WHAT YOU SEE
- Boss bar: after the phase pips, one small square per artifact in its tier's colour, so you can
  tell at a glance how well equipped a hero is.
    ★ Benika Akagane · Warlord of the Crimson Anvil  ◆◆◇ ⚡ ■■■■■
- Hero sheet: each artifact with its level and numbers, its power and cooldown, a growth bar for
  a growing one, who lent it, the locked slots and the level that opens each, the build's
  totals, what is in its backpack, and what it wishes for.
- Reporting station: each hero's artifacts, the faction's stock, who asked whom for what, and what
  the blacksmith or the artificer is making ("upgrading Blackforge Girdle to II for Benika").
- Chat (your faction): who now wears what, who gave or refused what, what was woken or upgraded,
  when an artifact grows a step, and what lies where a hero fell. Heroes say a line when they put
  on something rare, accept a gift, refuse one or ask for one.
- Sounds: a chime by tier when a hero puts one on, the anvil and enchanting table when one wakes,
  the smithing table and a soft amethyst hum at the bench, a sound of its own when an artifact
  grows, and a vanilla sound for each power (a fire charge, frost, a thorns prick, a totem, a
  shulker closing, evoker fangs, a piston...).
- Each hero's kit sheet will get an ARTIFACTS section: what it would choose for itself at levels
  1, 10 and 20 (one, three and five slots), and why.
- Artifacts are not drawn on the heroes' bodies (the two signature artifacts may be).

IN A WAR
A hero's war power (what the war councils count) grows by its artifact rating: about 3% for each
component it wears, 7% for each advanced, 9% for each greater, 10% for each rare find and 12% for a
signature (16.5% for a grown Hollow Star, 16% for Spirecore III), 2% more per upgrade level, and 4%
to 11.5% for a growing one by its steps; at most +40% in all (it was +30% with three slots). Rival
factions know a hero's artifacts only from what their scouts saw (the tiers, never the upgrades or
growth). Everything else stays: heroes still count for at most 1.5 times their side's soldiers, half
while badly hurt, nothing while dead or away.

HOW STRONG, WORKED THROUGH (on paper, to be measured in campaigns)
- A level-1 common marksman holds off about 3.25 iron soldiers bare, about 3.55 with its one slot
  filled. A small, honest boost: artifacts matter more as slots open.
- Benika at level 20 as a leader, five slots built to last: 236 health instead of 197, soldiers'
  blows about a quarter weaker. Six iron soldiers need about 15 s of landed blows instead of 9:
  she beats six, about nine break even, a dozen or another hero beat her. Against you in iron,
  about 73 s of landed hits instead of 45: the endgame boss, but every warning and weakness stays.
- The strongest burst: a level-20 striker in five damage artifacts reaches the 40-point ceiling
  more often, never above it; diamond with Protection IV still takes about 9 from its best hit.
- A level-1 hero with one artifact against a level-20 hero with five loses about 19 times in 20:
  levels, and the slots they open, are the main way heroes grow.
- Two heroes of level 10: the built one wins about 70% of duels; both built, it is even again.
- A growing artifact: a raiding hero fills the Bloodglass Ampoule's first three steps in about
  three raids; a guard fills the Vigil Lantern's three steps in six nights.
