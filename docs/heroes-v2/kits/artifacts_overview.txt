Faction Clash - artifacts
Items only heroes can wake | 50 artifacts in five tiers | crafted, looted, stolen
Status: designed, not in the game yet (design of 2026-11-18, reviewed the same day,
docs/heroes-v2/ARTIFACTS.md). All numbers are first values, tuned after play.
====================================================================================================

WHAT ARTIFACTS ARE
Your idea, in our own words: artifacts are named items that make a hero stronger at something,
built the way a MOBA builds items (cheap pieces first, then finished items with a special power),
but every name, look and effect is our own and made from Minecraft things: flint, lapis, copper,
blaze rods, echo shards, shulker shells, the archetypes and the eight elements.
- Each artifact is a normal item with its own name and a 16x16 icon in the vanilla style. Its name
  is coloured by tier: yellow for components, aqua for advanced, purple for greater and rare finds,
  gold for the two signature artifacts. Rare finds and signatures shimmer like enchanted items.
- Hovering over one shows what it gives, its special power, what it is made from and a line of
  lore, for example:

      Kiln Heart
      Advanced artifact · fire
       +12% fire damage
       +6% ability power
       +10% fire resistance
      Banked Embers (primed): an enemy its moves hit smoulders for 6 s;
      its next basic hit on it deals 2 fire and sets it alight for 3 s.
      Wakes only for a hero in an artifact slot: 60% of its power at
      hero level 1, full at level 20.
      Built from: Lapis Lens + Block of Coal
      "Banked coals that never quite go out."

WHO GETS THEIR POWER
- Only a hero, and only from its three artifact slots.
- Anyone else can carry them with no effect at all: you, a soldier, a hauler, a trader, a chest, a
  hopper, even a hero's own backpack. You cannot wear one, and neither can an ordinary NPC: players
  and NPCs are treated exactly the same, as everywhere in the hero design.
- What an artifact's power does to others is the same for everyone: a slow, an armour cut or a heal
  works on a player exactly as on an NPC soldier (and an ally heal reaches you too if you are a
  member of the hero's faction).

SLOTS AND HOW STRONG THEY GET
- Every hero has three slots, from level 1, whatever its rarity or whether it leads. Any artifact
  fits any slot. A hero cannot wear two copies of the same artifact.
- Like the heroes' own abilities, an artifact is complete from level 1 but grows in power: its
  numbers are at 60% on a level-1 hero, 79% at level 10 and full from level 20 [the same curve as
  hero mobility]. Its special power is there in full from level 1 and grows with the hero's ability
  power, like the hero's own passives.
- Each kind of bonus has a ceiling for the whole build, so three artifacts can never pile one
  bonus sky-high [health +20%, attack +15%, ability power +20%, armour +4, shorter cooldowns 20%,
  attack speed +15%, speed +8%, energy gain +25%, poise +25%, element damage +20%, one element's
  resistance +25%, all resistances +10%, drain 6%, heal power +20%, resolve 25%, knockback
  resistance +20, stamina +25%, work speed +15%, critical damage +25%]. The hero sheet marks a
  bonus that has reached its ceiling.
- What a full build is worth: three components make a hero about a tenth stronger, three advanced
  artifacts about a fifth, three greater ones about a quarter (at level 20; about 60% of that at
  level 1).
- What artifacts never change: the boss rules that keep a hero fight fair. No hit on a hero takes
  more than 12% of its health; no wind-up, ground mark or warning gets shorter; the waits between
  moves stay; a stun is never longer and never chains; no hit from a hero goes over the 40-point
  ceiling; nothing saves a hero from a killing blow; no artifact lets a hero fly, teleport or break
  blocks. An artifact's attack speed speeds a hero's basic swings and shots only, never its moves.

WHAT AN ARTIFACT CAN GIVE
  Health                   more maximum health (the boss health too)
  Attack                   harder basic hits and weapon-based moves
  Ability power            stronger moves, heals and shields
  Armour                   plain armour points; best against many small hits
  Recharge                 shorter cooldowns on the hero's moves (never shorter wind-ups)
  Attack speed             faster basic swings and shots (never faster moves)
  Speed                    faster on foot, in fights too
  Energy gain              the ultimate fills sooner
  Poise                    harder to stagger
  Element damage           more damage of the hero's own element
  Resistance               less damage of one element, or of all eight
  Drain                    heals the hero by a share of the damage it deals
  Heal power               stronger heals and shields it gives
  Resolve                  shorter stuns, freezes, slows, tethers and silences on the hero
  Knockback resistance     harder to push
  Stamina                  longer flights and glides (not more air time in a fight)
  Work speed               faster at its job at home
  Critical damage          harder jump-critical hits
In a fight, everything artifacts heal their own hero is held to 10% of its health in any 30 s, so
a hero cut down by a crowd of soldiers cannot out-heal them.

HOW TO GET THEM
Crafting, the player-like way. The faction's blacksmith does all of it in its workshop, from real
materials, and you can do every step yourself with the vanilla screens:
  1. Components at a crafting table (flint, gold, lapis, copper, iron, tuff, deepslate, amethyst,
     feathers, a spider eye, a glistering melon slice...).
  2. An Artificer's Sigil at a crafting table: a gold ingot, 4 lapis lazuli and 4 redstone make 2.
  3. Finished artifacts at a smithing table: the Sigil as the template, a component or an artifact
     as the base, and a second component or a material as the addition. The workshop gets a
     smithing table for this (the same one netherite will need later).
  4. Waking at an anvil: what comes off the smithing table is dormant and does nothing until it is
     woken with 2 lapis lazuli and experience levels (5 for advanced, 10 for greater). The faction
     pays with the experience its people really gathered, as it already does for enchanting and
     repairs, and the anvil wears out as vanilla's does.
The blacksmith only works on artifacts when its own orders are done (tools, weapons, armour and
repairs come first), keeps some gold and lapis back, and makes at most one finished artifact and
one waking a day at each base. The forgemaster talent makes waking cheaper.

Materials follow the world. Components and advanced artifacts come from today's economy (mines,
farms, animals, night kills, traders who buy ender pearls from clerics and packed ice from
wandering traders). Greater artifacts each need something from farther away: a breeze rod or a wind
charge (trial chambers), netherite scrap, a blaze rod or a ghast tear (the Nether), an echo shard
(an ancient city), a totem of undying (a woodland mansion), a shulker shell (the End), a slime
block, a nautilus shell (fishermen catch them). So builds grow as a faction explores.

Rare finds cannot be made. They lie in vanilla chests and bosses: an ancient city (Hushstone), a
bastion (Bastion Hoard-Ring), an end city (Chorus-Bloom Pendant), a woodland mansion or an evoker
(Fangspeaker Charm), an elder guardian (Elderscale Lens), a pillager outpost (Steward's Seal), and
any of them from a destroyed Hollow lair's heart. You and the NPCs find them the same way.

Signature artifacts: the Wither hero and the Ender Dragon hero each come with one of their own
(designed with those two heroes, docs/heroes-v2/BOSS_HEROES.md). Any hero can wear one for its
basic power; only its own hero gets its full bond (extra bonuses, a stronger power and changes to
two of her own moves). Only one of each exists in a world, and if one is destroyed (thrown into the
void) it comes back to its hero when she next returns. While she is in the faction, her own
signature stays on her: the faction never gives it to another hero.

Villagers never buy or sell artifacts. Later, when factions make treaties, an artifact can be part
of a tribute or a peace offer.

WHEN A HERO DIES
- Everything it wears drops where it died, like a player's inventory, with its backpack. Its kit
  weapon still vanishes and comes back with it, as before. A hero that escapes keeps everything.
- The drops never despawn. Fire, lava (they float out of it), explosions and cactus do not destroy
  them; only the void and commands do. A small coloured sparkle shows where they lie.
- Whoever gets there first takes them: the side that holds the ground picks them up before any
  other loot, a passing hero, you, or even a zombie (which drops it again when killed). Soldiers
  carry them home to their storehouse.
- So a faction can recover its fallen hero's artifacts, and an enemy can steal them and put them on
  its own heroes. When a faction knows where its hero fell, it may send the revived hero, or two
  soldiers, to fetch them, unless the place is next to an enemy base.
- The hero comes back 10 minutes later as always, with empty slots, and puts on whatever its
  faction has in store.
- Raiders who loot a storehouse take artifacts first, and a greedy hero on a raid goes for the
  storehouse while the base still stands.

HOW FACTIONS USE THEM
- Each faction plans its heroes' builds from what it knows: each hero's moves, role and element,
  the elements of enemy heroes its scouts have seen (it then values that resistance), and
  personality. A cautious hero likes health, armour and last-ditch powers; an aggressive one likes
  damage; a sociable one likes powers that help allies; a greedy one likes work artifacts and gold;
  an ambitious one likes the rarest things. A glutton likes the torcs that heal on a kill, a
  merchant the Hoard-Ring, a perfectionist finishes one build before starting another.
- The leader decides who gets the best: a greedy leader (or a tyrant) keeps them for itself, an
  ambitious one gives them to its strongest hero, and a fair-minded one shares them out to whoever
  has least.
- Heroes put artifacts on and swap them only at a base and never in a fight. Putting one on takes
  2 s (the hero holds it to its chest; a hit interrupts it). In the field a hero may only fill an
  empty slot with something it picked up. Haulers carry artifacts between bases.
- You and your faction's heroes: as a member you can open a hero's slots from its sheet (out of a
  fight, standing next to it), put artifacts in and take them out. What you put in stays: the
  faction AI never swaps it, until you take it out or the hero dies.

WHAT YOU SEE
- Boss bar: after the phase pips, one small square per artifact in its tier's colour, so you can
  tell at a glance how well equipped a hero is.
    ★ Benika Akagane · Warlord of the Crimson Anvil  ◆◆◇ ⚡ ■■■
- Hero sheet: each artifact with its numbers at that hero's level, its power and cooldown, who
  pinned it, the build's totals, and what is in its backpack.
- Reporting station: each hero's artifacts, the faction's stock, and what the blacksmith is making.
- Chat (your faction): who now wears what, what was woken, and what lies where a hero fell. Heroes
  may say a line when they put on something rare.
- Sounds: a chime by tier when a hero puts one on, the anvil and enchanting table when one wakes,
  and a vanilla sound for each power (a fire charge, frost, a thorns prick, a totem, a shulker
  closing, evoker fangs...).
- Each hero's kit sheet will get an ARTIFACTS section: what its faction would build for it at
  levels 1, 10 and 20, and why.
- Artifacts are not drawn on the heroes' bodies (the two signature artifacts may be).

IN A WAR
A hero's war power (what the war councils count) grows by its artifact rating: about 3% for each
component it wears, 7% for each advanced, 9% for each greater, 10% for each rare find and 12% for a
signature, times its level's attunement, at most +30% in all. Rival factions know a hero's
artifacts only from what their scouts saw. Everything else stays: heroes still count for at most 1.5
times their side's soldiers, half while badly hurt, nothing while dead or away.

HOW STRONG, WORKED THROUGH (on paper, to be measured in campaigns)
- A level-1 common marksman holds off about three iron soldiers bare, about four in three greater
  artifacts. Artifacts make a weak hero stand; they do not win a war.
- Benika at level 20 as a leader, built to last (Blackforge Girdle, Shulker-Shell Ward, Ghast-Tear
  Torc): 236 health instead of 197, soldiers' blows about a sixth weaker. Six iron soldiers need
  about 13 s of landed blows instead of 9: still beaten by six. Against you in iron, about 65 s of
  landed hits instead of 45: the endgame boss, but every warning and weakness is unchanged.
- A level-20 striker built for damage hits about 30% harder with its swings and about a sixth
  harder with its moves; its ultimate stays under the 40-point ceiling, so diamond with Protection
  IV still takes about 8 from it.
- A level-1 hero in three greater artifacts still loses to a bare level-20 hero about three times
  in four: levels stay the main way heroes grow.
- Two heroes of the same level: the built one wins about 60-65% of duels; both built, it is even
  again.

QUESTIONS FOR YOU (with what we recommend)
1. Three slots for every hero, with power growing by level instead of more slots: good, or would
   you rather have more slots (four to six) with smaller artifacts? We recommend three: a fourth
   slot would mostly run into the ceilings above, and the boss bar stays easy to read.
2. As a member you can put artifacts on your faction's heroes and take them off again, and the AI
   leaves what you put there. Is that the control you want? We recommend keeping it: it is your
   only direct say over your faction's heroes, and it changes nothing for the NPC factions.
Decided unless you say otherwise: waking at the anvil stays (say no and artifacts come off the
smithing table ready), and artifacts are not drawn on the heroes' bodies, except the two signature
artifacts on their own heroes.

CHANGED AFTER REVIEW (2026-11-18)
So that no name or power copies another game: Ravenous Torc is now Marrow Torc, Mender's Censer is
Mender's Brazier, Farsight Pearl is Starless Pearl; the powers Quickened Mind, Rising Tempo and
Tailwind are Racing Thoughts, Gathering Pace and Fair Wind; Banked Embers and Rune-Struck now mark
the enemy a move hits; Stoked Heat replaces "every third hit"; and Snag tethers instead of cutting
armour. Artifact attack speed now speeds basic attacks only, so no move ever gets a shorter
warning.
